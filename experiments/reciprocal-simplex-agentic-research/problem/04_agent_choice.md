# Approach 4 — Choose a suitable construction

Choose the route you judge most promising for the **exact**, prescribed-intensity
problem. Explain your choice and its difference from the standard first three
approaches. You may use a Cox, Gibbs, random-geometric, dependent-cluster or
other rigorously defined construction. Class labels may overlap; the actual
law and calculations matter.

Inspect the two shared manuscripts before proposing a new model. The spherical
result is approximate, and the finite-cluster result chooses intensity together
with its other constants. Focus on the precise gap between a known construction
and the current target. Determine whether a valid transformation can close it
without changing the diameter/altitude domains.

Do not prescribe three nonnegative functions and assume joint realizability.
Construct a probability law and derive its full density hierarchy. If using
latent carriers, account for coincident/singular configurations and prove the
required unconditional absolute continuity. If using an interaction energy,
prove existence and the resulting correlations; the interaction weight is not
automatically the correlation function.

An exact limit needs a valid limiting process with controlled moments and
densities. An arbitrarily accurate approximation is not that limit by itself.
If several routes fail, document the precise reason and develop the strongest
correct restricted result. Use your own context and do not consult current
submissions from the other approaches.
