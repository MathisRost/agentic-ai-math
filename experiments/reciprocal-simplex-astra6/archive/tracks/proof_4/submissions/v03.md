## Exact claim and scope

S1 asks for a process for every independently prescribed pair \(\lambda_0>0,M>0\), with all three exact full factorial density identities on the entire sets \(E_n(M)\). Throughout this submission, \(D,A,V,E_n(M)\) retain their exact S1 definitions. Write \(\lambda=\lambda_0\). No positive lower distance, area, volume or altitude cutoff is introduced.

The preceding submission constructs such a process when
\[
 \lambda M^3\leq\tau,\qquad
 \tau=\min\left\{\frac1{36e\pi},
       \frac{J^2}{8\pi^2Z_0},
       \sqrt{\frac{J^3}{32\pi^4Z_0^2}}\right\},
 \quad J=\frac{\pi^2}{4},\quad Z_0=\frac{\pi^2}{4}+\frac{2\pi}{3}.
 \tag{1}
\]
That remains a partial result. The new work addresses possible extensions beyond this range:

1. Every S1 process automatically has finite third count moments on every bounded set. More precisely, the lower two identities imply the necessary coefficient inequality
   \[
    \lambda\frac{\pi M^3}{6}
    \leq 2+\frac{a_3}{a_2}\frac{\pi^2M^2}{2}.
    \tag{2}
   \]
   This places no universal intensity ceiling, because S1 does not bound \(a_3/a_2\).
2. An exact reciprocal-fourth process with finite third local moments cannot retain an exact reciprocal-fourth law after independent superposition with a positive-intensity process having finite fourth local moments. Nor can two positive-intensity processes, both with exact reciprocal-fourth laws and finite third local moments, be independently superposed while retaining that law.
3. The slab law from v02 cannot be mixed with **any** finite-fourth-local-moment correction to obtain S1 at an intensity satisfying \(\lambda M^3>18/\pi\). No cluster partition, separation, bound on cluster sizes or independence assumption on the correction is needed. This strictly enlarges the class of corrections excluded by v02's Theorem 2, while retaining its stated intensity ceiling.
4. A different route, extending an unknown process by an independent Poisson process, has an explicit residual hierarchy through order four. That hierarchy can be made pointwise nonnegative for every \(\lambda,M>0\). Its realisation by a stationary, simple, locally finite whole-space process is **not proved**.

Statements 2 and 3 are restrictions on representations, not general non-existence for S1. Statement 4 is not a point-process construction. There is no assertion that S1's missing parameter range is impossible. Historical S0 remains false; none of the results is relabelled as an S0 success.

## Argument

### 1. A geometric bound and automatic local third moments

We first give a bound slightly sharper than the one used for count moments in v02. If \(B=B(c,r)\subset\mathbb R^3\) and \(\ell\) is any affine line, then
\[
 \int_B\frac{dz}{\operatorname{dist}(z,\ell)}\leq\pi^2r^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
To prove this, slice the ball in planes perpendicular to \(\ell\). A slice is a planar disk of radius \(R=\sqrt{r^2-t^2}\); the line meets the slice at an arbitrary point with planar displacement \(a\) from its centre. For every \(R>0\), layer-cake integration gives
\[
 \int_{\{|u|<R\}}\frac{du}{|u-a|}
 =\int_0^\infty
       |\{|u|<R\}\cap\{|u-a|<s^{-1}\}|\,ds
 \leq\int_0^\infty\min\{\pi R^2,\pi s^{-2}\}\,ds
 =2\pi R.
 \tag{4}
\]
All integrands are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies; the value at \(s=0\) is irrelevant. Integrating (4) over \(-r<t<r\) gives (3), since
\(\int_{-r}^r2\pi\sqrt{r^2-t^2}\,dt=\pi^2r^2\).
The upper bound is attained when the line passes through the ball's centre.

For distinct \(x,y\), area satisfies
\[
 A(x,y,z)=\frac{|x-y|}{2}\operatorname{dist}(z,\ell_{xy}).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \int_B A(x,y,z)^{-1}\,dz
 \leq\frac{2\pi^2r^2}{|x-y|}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Collinear \(z\) form a null set and do not affect the integral.

**Theorem 1.** Let \(X\) be a simple, almost surely locally finite point process with homogeneous finite positive intensity \(\lambda\). Suppose its full second and third factorial measures are globally absolutely continuous and equal \(a_2/D\) and \(a_3/A\) almost everywhere on \(E_2(M)\) and \(E_3(M)\), where \(a_2,a_3>0\). Then every bounded-set third count moment is finite, and (2) holds.

**Proof.** Fix \(0<r<M/2\), let \(B=B(c,r)\), and write
\[
 N=X(B),\qquad \mu=\mathbb EN=\lambda\frac{4\pi r^3}{3},
 \qquad f_j=\mathbb E(N)_j.
\]
The full absolute-continuity hypotheses exclude mass on coincident or collinear tuples. Every nondegenerate tuple of the relevant orders in \(B\) has diameter below \(M\). Hence
\[
 f_2=a_2\int_{B^2}D^{-1},\qquad
 f_3=a_3\int_{B^3}A^{-1}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The pair integral is positive and finite: for fixed \(x\in B\), enlarge the other vertex domain to \(B(x,2r)\) and integrate \(1/|x-y|\) radially. Equation (5) now gives
\[
 0<f_2<\infty,\qquad
 0\leq f_3\leq q_r f_2<\infty,
 \qquad q_r=\frac{a_3}{a_2}\,2\pi^2r^2.
 \tag{7}
\]
The identity \(N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N\) proves \(\mathbb EN^3<\infty\).

Every bounded Borel set is covered by finitely many balls of radius below \(M/2\). If their counts are \(N_1,\ldots,N_k\), its count is at most their sum, and
\[
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^kN_i\right)^3\leq k^2\sum_{i=1}^kN_i^3.
\]
This proves the asserted third moment on every bounded set, including sets whose diameter exceeds \(M\). No density formula outside the local domains was used.

Cauchy–Schwarz, with \(\sqrt N\) and \(\sqrt N(N-1)\), gives
\[
 f_2^2\leq\mu\,\mathbb E[N(N-1)^2]
 =\mu(f_3+f_2)\leq\mu(1+q_r)f_2.
\]
All terms are finite by the preceding argument, and \(f_2>0\). Nonnegative variance then yields
\[
 \mu^2\leq f_2+\mu\leq\mu(2+q_r),
 \qquad
 \lambda\frac{4\pi r^3}{3}\leq2+\frac{a_3}{a_2}\,2\pi^2r^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
Letting \(r\uparrow M/2\) proves (2). This is a limit of deterministic inequalities, not an assumption about densities at diameter exactly \(M\). \(\square\)

### 2. The local reciprocal-volume singularity

We record explicitly the divergence used in the new obstructions. For every nonempty open ball \(B\) whose diameter is below \(M\),
\[
 \int_{B^4\cap E_4(M)}V^{-1}\,d\mathbf x=\infty.
 \tag{9}
\]
Choose three small, disjoint vertex neighbourhoods inside \(B\), around a noncollinear reference triangle. They can be chosen so that all their triangles have areas between two positive finite constants. For each such triangle, a small planar neighbourhood of its barycentre and a uniformly small normal interval lie inside \(B\); shrink the neighbourhoods if needed. Parametrise the fourth vertex as \(z+h n\), where \(z\) lies in that planar neighbourhood and \(n\) is a unit normal to the triangle. This orthonormal coordinate change has Jacobian one, and
\[
 V(x_1,x_2,x_3,z+hn)=\frac{A(x_1,x_2,x_3)}{3}|h|.
\]
Integrating over \(0<|h|<\varepsilon\) gives an infinite integral for every such base triangle. Tonelli and the positive measure of the three vertex neighbourhoods prove (9). Null sets of coincidences or coplanarity do not change this calculation. Any nonempty open ball contains a smaller ball to which this argument applies.

Thus a full density \(b/V\), \(b>0\), on \(E_4(M)\) forces infinite fourth factorial, and hence raw fourth, count moment in every nonempty open ball. It is compatible with almost-sure local finiteness. A finite-fourth-moment correction cannot carry a nonzero multiple of this density on the local domain.

### 3. Independent superposition does not increase intensity while preserving the fourth law

**Theorem 2.** Fix \(M>0\). Let \(Y\) be stationary, simple and almost surely locally finite, with positive finite intensity, full factorial measures of orders two through four globally absolutely continuous, and finite third count moments on every bounded set. Suppose
\[
 \rho_Y^{(4)}=b/V\quad\text{on }E_4(M),\qquad b>0.
 \tag{10}
\]
Neither of the following independent-superposition procedures can produce a process whose full factorial measures of orders two through four are globally absolutely continuous and whose fourth density is exactly \(a/V\) on \(E_4(M)\), for some \(a>0\):

(a) form \(X=Y\cup C\), where \(C\) is an independent stationary, simple, locally finite process with positive finite intensity and finite fourth moments on all bounded sets;

(b) form \(X=Y\cup Z\), where \(Z\) is an independent process with the same stated regularity and moment properties as \(Y\), and \(\rho_Z^{(4)}=c/V\) on \(E_4(M)\), with \(c>0\).

Therefore the obstruction applies, in particular, whenever the proposed result would satisfy S1.

**Proof.** Independent processes with diffuse intensity measures have no common points almost surely. Indeed their cross product measure is the product of their first measures, which assigns zero mass to the spatial diagonal in each bounded product window; then use countable exhaustion. Their union is consequently simple and locally finite.

First consider (a), and suppose for contradiction that \(X\) has the global full measure regularity of S1 and
\(\rho_X^{(4)}=a/V\) on \(E_4(M)\). Domination
\(\alpha_C^{(j)}\leq\alpha_X^{(j)}\) gives full absolute continuity for \(C\) at the needed orders if it was not separately assumed. Label each point of an ordered fourth tuple by its component. Independence and Tonelli give the full measure identity
\[
 \alpha_X^{(4)}=\alpha_Y^{(4)}+Q,
 \tag{11}
\]
where \(Q\) contains \(\alpha_C^{(4)}\) and all allocations using both components. In density form, the mixed part is exactly
\[
 \sum_{\substack{I\subset\{1,2,3,4\}\\1\leq |I|\leq3}}
 \rho_Y^{(|I|)}(x_I)\,
 \rho_C^{(4-|I|)}(x_{I^c}).
 \tag{12}
\]
Here first-order densities are their intensities. All 14 mixed allocations are present: four of type \(3+1\), six of type \(2+2\), and four of type \(1+3\).

For every bounded \(B\), \(Q(B^4)<\infty\). For a mixed allocation of size \(j\), its mass is
\(\mathbb E(Y(B))_j\,\mathbb E(C(B))_{4-j}\), which is finite for \(1\leq j\leq3\). The pure \(C\) term is finite by hypothesis. The resulting density \(q\) of \(Q\) is nonnegative and, almost everywhere on \(E_4(M)\), (11) gives
\[
 q=(a-b)/V.
 \tag{13}
\]
This is subtraction of finite pointwise density values on nondegenerate configurations, not subtraction of infinite measure masses.

Nonnegativity implies \(a\geq b\). If \(a>b\), (9) makes \(Q(B^4)\) infinite in a sufficiently small ball, a contradiction. Hence \(a=b\), so \(Q\) has zero mass on that ball's fourth product: all its tuples are locally admissible except a Lebesgue-null set, and \(Q\) is absolutely continuous.

On the other hand, the four allocations selecting three \(Y\) points and one \(C\) point contribute
\[
 4\,\mathbb E(Y(B))_3\,\mathbb EC(B)>0.
 \tag{14}
\]
To justify strict positivity, (9) and (10) imply
\(\mathbb E(Y(B))_4=\infty\), hence \(\mathbb P(Y(B)\geq4)>0\) and \(\mathbb E(Y(B))_3>0\). The latter expectation is finite by hypothesis, and \(\mathbb EC(B)>0\) by the positive homogeneous intensity. Equation (14) contradicts \(Q(B^4)=0\).

For (b), retain both pure fourth measures separately:
\[
 \alpha_X^{(4)}=\alpha_Y^{(4)}+\alpha_Z^{(4)}+Q_{\rm mixed}.
 \tag{15}
\]
All mixed terms have finite mass on \(B^4\), because both processes have finite third local moments. If an exact coefficient \(a\) existed, their nonnegative density would be
\((a-b-c)/V\) on \(E_4(M)\). The argument from (9) forces \(a=b+c\) and zero mixed mass there, contradicted by the positive \(3+1\) allocations. No subtraction of the two infinite pure masses is made. \(\square\)

By Theorem 1, any S1 process has the finite third local moments required in part (b). Thus independent superposition of two S1 solutions on the same diameter domain cannot itself be an S1 solution. Part (a) also excludes adding an independent Poisson background to the slab law or to any such exact-fourth base. These conclusions do not concern dependent additions, a redesign of the base's fourth density, or arbitrary point processes outside the stated representation.

### 4. Exact slab dependencies and coalescence limits

The next theorem uses the actual slab law, rather than a formal fourth density. The dependencies are v01, Argument Sections 2–7, and the scaled statement and endpoint correction in v02, Argument Section 2, equations (5)–(10). These sections supply its whole-space construction, every slab allocation, stationarity, simplicity, local finiteness, global density regularity and local moment properties. Their mathematical statements, not their review status, are the dependencies.

For clarity the parameters and local formulas needed below are repeated. Put
\[
 H_M(v)=1+M^2|v|^2,\quad T_M(v)=2+2M|v|,\quad
 Z_M=M^{-3}Z_0,\quad I=[-1/2,1/2).
\]
Sample \(W\) with density \(T_M/(Z_MH_M^4)\), a conditional uniform phase \(B\) modulo \(T_M(W)\), and then a Poisson process with intensity
\[
 \Lambda_Y(x)=sH_M(W)\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
        \mathbf1_I(W\cdot x-B-kT_M(W)),\qquad 0<s<\infty.
 \tag{16}
\]
Its intensity is \(sJ/Z_0\). Its full fourth density on \(E_4(M)\) is
\[
 r_4=b/V,\qquad b=\frac{s^4}{6Z_M}>0.
 \tag{17}
\]
The canonical expected-product lower densities on their local domains are
\[
 r_2(d)=\frac{c_2}{d}\Phi(d/M),\quad c_2=\frac{\pi s^2M}{Z_0},
 \quad
 \Phi(t)=\int_{I^2}\frac{dq_1dq_2}{1+(q_2-q_1)^2/t^2},
 \tag{18}
\]
\[
 r_3(0,u,w)=\frac{c_3}{A(0,u,w)}\Psi_M(u,w),\quad
 c_3=\frac{\pi s^3M^2}{2Z_0},
 \tag{19}
\]
\[
 \Psi_M(u,w)=\int_{I^3}\frac{d\mathbf q}
                   {\sqrt{1+M^2|v_0(\mathbf q;u,w)|^2}},
 \quad
 v_0\cdot u=q_2-q_1,\quad v_0\cdot w=q_3-q_1,\quad
 v_0\in\operatorname{span}\{u,w\}.
 \tag{20}
\]
In (20) the vectors \(u,w\) are linearly independent, so \(v_0\) is unique. The zero-index allocation is the only local slab allocation: a nonzero index would require an indicator-argument difference at least
\(T_M(v)-|v||u|=2+(2M-|u|)|v|\geq2\), instead of less than one. This includes \(v=0\). The global allocation and resonance arguments remain those in the cited sections; none is replaced by a same-slab assertion outside the local domain.

We need, for any fixed unit vector \(e\) and \(0<R<M/2\),
\[
 d r_2(d)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 d\int_{B(0,R)}r_3(0,de,z)\,dz\longrightarrow0
 \quad(d\downarrow0).
 \tag{21}
\]
Take \(d<M-R\), so almost every \(z\in B(0,R)\) forms an eligible triple. In (18) the integrand is bounded by one and tends to zero for almost every phase pair, proving the first limit by dominated convergence. For the second, \(v_0\cdot de=q_2-q_1\) implies \(|v_0|\geq|q_2-q_1|/d\). Thus \(\Psi_M(de,z)\to0\) for every \(z\) off the line through \(e\), by dominated convergence in the phase variables. Moreover
\[
 0\leq d\,r_3(0,de,z)
 \leq\frac{2c_3}{|z_\perp|}.
 \tag{22}
\]
The right side is integrable on \(B(0,R)\), with integral \(2c_3\pi^2R^2\). A second dominated-convergence application proves (21). These are limits of the explicit canonical versions, not assigned values of arbitrary densities on a null ray.

### 5. Arbitrary finite-fourth-moment corrections still have a finite ceiling

**Theorem 3.** Let \(Y\) be the slab process (16), for arbitrary \(s>0\) and \(M>0\). Let \(C\) be any simple, almost surely locally finite point process with finite fourth count moments on every bounded set. Suppose a whole-law mixture
\[
 \mathcal L(X)=\theta\mathcal L(Y)+(1-\theta)\mathcal L(C),
 \qquad0\leq\theta\leq1,
 \tag{23}
\]
satisfies all S1 measure and density requirements at intensity \(\lambda>0\). Then
\[
 \frac{a_3}{a_2}\frac{\pi^2M^2}{2}\leq1,
 \qquad
 \lambda M^3\leq\frac{18}{\pi}.
 \tag{24}
\]
There is no assumption that \(C\) has a cluster representation. It may have arbitrary dependencies and arbitrary geometry outside the restriction forced in the proof.

**Proof.** The endpoint \(\theta=0\) is impossible by (9), and \(\theta=1\) is impossible by (18), (21) and the required positive reciprocal pair coefficient. Hence \(0<\theta<1\).

Full absolute continuity for \(C\) at orders two through four follows from
\((1-\theta)\alpha_C^{(n)}\leq\alpha_X^{(n)}\). The exact full mixture identity and (17) give
\[
 (1-\theta)\rho_C^{(4)}
 =\frac{a_4-\theta b}{V}\quad\text{a.e. on }E_4(M).
 \tag{25}
\]
Nonnegativity and finite fourth local moments, together with (9), force
\[
 a_4=\theta b,\qquad \rho_C^{(4)}=0\quad\text{on }E_4(M).
 \tag{26}
\]
No fourth-moment assumption on \(X\) or \(Y\) is made.

It follows that almost surely \(C\) has **no four distinct points whose diameter is below \(M\)**. To verify the assertion for actual configurations, in each bounded window count ordered distinct quadruples of diameter below \(M\). The expectation of the nondegenerate ones is zero by (26). The expectation of the degenerate ones is zero by global absolute continuity: coplanar quadruples form a Lebesgue-null set. Thus the count is zero almost surely. A countable exhaustion of space makes the conclusion simultaneous for all quadruples. The argument uses the full measure hypothesis precisely where a possible coplanar singular component would otherwise invalidate it.

Fix \(0<R<M/2\). For any ordered pair \(x,y\) of distinct \(C\) points with \(|x-y|<M-R\), there is at most one other \(C\) point in \(B(x,R)\). If two such points \(z,w\) existed, all six pair distances among \(x,y,z,w\) would be below \(M\):
\[
 |z-w|<2R<M,\quad |y-z|,|y-w|<|y-x|+R<M,
\]
and the other distances satisfy the same strict bound directly. This contradicts the preceding no-quadruple property.

Integrate this deterministic inequality against a nonnegative pair test supported on \(0<|x-y|<M-R\) and take expectations. Tonelli and the full densities give
\[
 \int_{B(x,R)}\rho_C^{(3)}(x,y,z)\,dz
 \leq\rho_C^{(2)}(x,y)
 \quad\text{for a.e. such }(x,y).
 \tag{27}
\]
Every noncollinear triple appearing here lies in \(E_3(M)\), and the pair lies in \(E_2(M)\). Substituting the two exact mixture densities eliminates \(C\):
\[
 \frac{2\pi^2R^2a_3}{d}
   -\theta\int_{B(0,R)}r_3(0,de,z)\,dz
 \leq \frac{a_2}{d}-\theta r_2(d),
 \qquad 0<d<M-R,
 \tag{28}
\]
for almost every distance \(d\). The geometric integral on the left is exact, since the line through \(0,de\) passes through the centre of \(B(0,R)\), giving
\(\int_{B(0,R)} A(0,de,z)^{-1}dz=2\pi^2R^2/d\).

For completeness, (28) is first obtained for almost every translated pair. The substituted expressions are translation invariant and radial in the pair separation, by the target formulas and the isotropy of (16). Fubini in the translation variable and polar coordinates in the separation imply its almost-everywhere distance formulation. Evaluation of arbitrary Radon–Nikodym versions on a specified ray is not being used.

Multiply (28) by \(d\), take a sequence of valid distances tending to zero, and apply (21). This gives \(2\pi^2R^2a_3\leq a_2\). The argument holds for every \(R<M/2\), so \(R\uparrow M/2\) yields the first inequality in (24). Theorem 1 now gives
\[
 \lambda\frac{\pi M^3}{6}
 \leq2+\frac{a_3}{a_2}\frac{\pi^2M^2}{2}\leq3,
\]
which proves the intensity ceiling. \(\square\)

This theorem excludes every finite-fourth-moment correction to this slab law, not merely changes of its finite-cluster marks or parent thinning. It does **not** exclude a correction with infinite fourth moments and a nonzero local reciprocal-fourth contribution, a base with different lower coalescence behaviour, or a different whole-space representation. The finite fourth moment is an explicit restriction on the auxiliary law \(C\), not a newly imposed condition on S1.

### 6. A residual hierarchy for a different extension route

Theorem 2 excludes adding a Poisson process to a base whose fourth density is already exactly \(b/V\). It does not exclude a base with carefully chosen lower-order finite corrections in its fourth density. We now determine those corrections exactly.

Fix arbitrary \(\lambda,M>0\) and \(0<\nu<\lambda\), and put \(\mu=\lambda-\nu\). Seek a stationary simple process \(Y_*\) of intensity \(\mu\) such that, with an independent homogeneous Poisson process \(\Pi_\nu\),
\[
 X=Y_*\cup\Pi_\nu
 \tag{29}
\]
satisfies S1 at intensity \(\lambda\).

For any actual independent superposition, allocation of the ordered tuple labels gives
\[
 \rho_X^{(n)}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 =\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}
       \nu^{\,n-|I|}\rho_{Y_*}^{(|I|)}(x_I),
 \qquad \rho^{(0)}=1.
 \tag{30}
\]
This is the full density identity, including every Poisson and non-Poisson allocation. Its subset inverse is obtained by replacing \(\nu\) with \(-\nu\): for each fixed \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), the coefficient of \(\rho_{Y_*}^{(|J|)}(x_J)\) in the composition is
\[
 \sum_{I:\,J\subseteq I\subseteq[n]}
       (-\nu)^{n-|I|}\nu^{|I|-|J|}
 =(\nu-\nu)^{n-|J|},
\]
equal to one if \(J=[n]\) and zero otherwise. This is a finite pointwise algebraic identity; no signed infinite measure manipulation is required.

A nondegenerate simplex has all its lower-dimensional faces nondegenerate. Its subsets also have diameter below \(M\). Therefore the local target identities force the following candidate densities on their corresponding \(E_n(M)\):
\[
 r_1=\mu,\qquad
 r_2=\frac{a_2}{D}-2\nu\lambda+\nu^2,
 \tag{31}
\]
\[
 r_3=\frac{a_3}{A}
       -\nu a_2\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset[3]}D_{ij}^{-1}
       +3\nu^2\lambda-\nu^3,
 \tag{32}
\]
\[
 r_4=\frac{a_4}{V}
       -\nu a_3\sum_{\substack{I\subset[4]\\|I|=3}}A_I^{-1}
       +\nu^2a_2\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset[4]}D_{ij}^{-1}
       -4\nu^3\lambda+\nu^4.
 \tag{33}
\]
The four face terms, six pair terms and four intensity terms at order four are all retained.

**Proposition 4.** For every prescribed \(\lambda,M>0\) and \(0<\nu<\lambda\), finite positive constants \(a_2,a_3,a_4\) can be chosen such that (31)–(33) are pointwise nonnegative on all their local nondegenerate domains. For example, it suffices to choose successively
\[
 a_2\geq(2\nu\lambda-\nu^2)M,
 \tag{34}
\]
\[
 a_3\geq\frac32\nu a_2M,
 \tag{35}
\]
\[
 a_4\geq\frac{4M}{3}\nu a_3
              +\frac{M^3}{6}(4\nu^3\lambda-\nu^4).
 \tag{36}
\]
These candidate functions are finite on every nondegenerate configuration, their pair and triple functions are integrable on bounded product sets contained in the local domain, and their fourth function differs from \(a_4/V\) by a function integrable on every sufficiently small bounded product ball.

**Proof.** Because \(0<D<M\), (34) proves \(r_2\geq0\). For each triangle edge, its corresponding altitude is below \(M\), so
\[
 \frac{A}{D_{ij}}\leq\frac M2,\qquad
 A\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset[3]}D_{ij}^{-1}\leq\frac{3M}{2}.
 \tag{37}
\]
Equation (35) therefore makes the first two terms of (32), taken together, nonnegative. Its remaining constant is \(\nu^2(3\lambda-\nu)>0\).

For a tetrahedron, \(V/A_I\) is one third of the altitude above face \(I\). Every such altitude is below \(M\), and Hadamard's elementary determinant bound on three edges of length below \(M\) gives \(V\leq M^3/6\). Hence
\[
 V\sum_{|I|=3}A_I^{-1}\leq\frac{4M}{3},
 \qquad V(4\nu^3\lambda-\nu^4)
       \leq\frac{M^3}{6}(4\nu^3\lambda-\nu^4).
 \tag{38}
\]
The constant in the last expression is positive. After multiplication of (33) by \(V\), (36) dominates both potentially negative contributions. The pair contribution is nonnegative and can be discarded in that lower bound. Thus \(r_4\geq0\).

Finiteness on nondegenerate tuples is explicit. Pair integrability follows from radial integration, and triple integrability from (5). The pair corrections in \(r_3\) are also integrable by integrating the unused third vertex. On a ball of diameter below \(M\), each face correction in (33) is integrable by (5) followed by integration of the fourth vertex; each pair correction is treated similarly. Constants are integrable on bounded products. This proves the stated integrability assertions.
