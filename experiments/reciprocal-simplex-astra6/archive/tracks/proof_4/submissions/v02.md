## Exact claim and scope

Put
\[
 J=\frac{\pi^2}{4},\qquad Z_0=\frac{\pi^2}{4}+\frac{2\pi}{3},
\]
and define the universal positive number
\[
 \tau=\min\left\{
 \frac1{36e\pi},\quad
 \frac{J^2}{8\pi^2Z_0},\quad
 \sqrt{\frac{J^3}{32\pi^4Z_0^2}}
 \right\}>0.
 \tag{1}
\]

**Theorem 1.** For every prescribed $M>0$ and every prescribed $\lambda>0$ with
\[
 \lambda M^3\leq\tau,
 \tag{2}
\]
there is one stationary, isotropic, simple, almost surely locally finite point process $X$ on $\mathbb R^3$ with intensity exactly $\lambda$. Its full factorial expectation measures of orders two, three and four have densities finite Lebesgue-almost everywhere globally, with no singular component. For positive constants $a_2,a_3,a_4$ depending on $(\lambda,M)$,
\[
 \rho_X^{(2)}=a_2/D\text{ on }E_2(M),\qquad
 \rho_X^{(3)}=a_3/A\text{ on }E_3(M),\qquad
 \rho_X^{(4)}=a_4/V\text{ on }E_4(M),
 \tag{3}
\]
as almost-everywhere identities of the full measures. There are no lower geometric or shape cutoffs. All bounded-set third count moments are finite and every nonempty open ball has infinite fourth count moment.

Here $D,A,V$ and $E_n(M)$ have exactly their S1 meanings. Unlike v01, this theorem obtains all three reciprocal identities simultaneously. Condition (2) nevertheless excludes part of S1's independently prescribed parameter range. It is a sufficient condition for this construction, not a necessary condition for S1, and the theorem is **not an S1 pass**.

Theorem 2 below proves that the particular method used here cannot cover all intensities: a convex mixture of the specified slab component with a process made of separated clusters of size at most three can satisfy the two lower reciprocal laws only if
\[
 \lambda M^3\leq18/\pi.
 \tag{4}
\]
This is a restriction of that representation, not general non-existence. No target change is proposed.

## Argument

### 1. Why a mixture supplies a different correction mechanism

The accepted v01 slab law has the required full fourth density but lacks both lower identities. Independently superposing another process would add mixed tuples at order four. We instead choose one of two whole-space laws by a single random coin. Factorial expectation measures then combine linearly, without tuples crossing between the alternative laws.

The second law is formed from diffuse clusters with at most three points. Their centres are separated sufficiently far that different clusters have mutual point distance greater than $M$. This second law therefore has zero fourth factorial measure on $E_4(M)$, while its second and third densities can be chosen through explicit nonnegative residual kernels. Its law is not in the single-slab class excluded in v01.

### 2. The retained slab component and its precise properties

We use the partial construction proved in v01, Sections 2–7, with the two precision corrections recorded below. The returned review independently verified the law, the full allocation formula, global density regularity and the lower-order calculations. We state the needed scaled version explicitly so that its parameter use can be checked.

For fixed $M>0$, set
\[
 H_M(v)=1+M^2|v|^2,\qquad T_M(v)=2+2M|v|,
 \qquad Z_M=M^{-3}Z_0,
 \qquad s=\frac{\lambda Z_0}{J}.
\]
Sample $W$ with density $T_M(v)/(Z_MH_M(v)^4)$, and conditional on $W=v$ sample a phase $B$ uniformly in $[0,T_M(v))$. Given these marks, let $Y$ be Poisson with intensity
\[
 \Lambda_Y(x)=sH_M(W)
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathbf1_{[-1/2,1/2)}
       (W\cdot x-B-kT_M(W)).
 \tag{5}
\]
Changing variables $w=Mv$ in the normalising integrals gives
$\int T_M/H_M^4=M^{-3}Z_0$ and $\int H_M^{-3}=M^{-3}J$. Thus (5) has intensity $\lambda$, with no restriction on $\lambda$ for this component.

This is the spatial dilation by $M$ of the v01 law with diameter parameter one and intensity $\lambda M^3$. Its existence, stationarity, simplicity, local finiteness and global full density requirements therefore follow from the accepted v01 theorem. This use of dilation is valid because that partial theorem already covered every intensity and diameter; it does not remove condition (2) for the new combined construction.

For $d=|x_2-x_1|<M$ and noncollinear triples of diameter below $M$, the canonical expected-product densities are
\[
 r_2(d)=\frac{c_2}{d}\Phi(d/M),\qquad
 c_2=\frac{\pi s^2M}{Z_0},
 \tag{6}
\]
\[
 r_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)=\frac{c_3}{A(x_1,x_2,x_3)}\Psi_M(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1),
 \qquad c_3=\frac{\pi s^3M^2}{2Z_0},
 \tag{7}
\]
where
\[
 \Phi(t)=\int_{[-1/2,1/2)^2}
       \frac{dq_1dq_2}{1+(q_2-q_1)^2/t^2},
 \quad 0<\Phi(t)\leq1,
 \tag{8}
\]
and
\[
 \Psi_M(u,w)=\int_{[-1/2,1/2)^3}
       \frac{d\mathbf q}{\sqrt{1+M^2|v_0(\mathbf q;u,w)|^2}},
 \quad 0<\Psi_M(u,w)\leq1.
 \tag{9}
\]
Here $v_0$ is the unique vector in $\operatorname{span}\{u,w\}$ solving
$v_0\cdot u=q_2-q_1$ and $v_0\cdot w=q_3-q_1$.
The powers of $M$ in (6)–(7) can also be checked directly: integration over the two free normal coordinates at order two contributes
$\pi/[M^2(1+M^2\beta^2)]$, and integration over the single free normal coordinate at order three contributes
$\pi/[M\sqrt{1+M^2|v_0|^2}]$.

At order four, cancellation of $H_M^4$ against the mark density and the affine determinant change give
\[
 r_4(\mathbf x)=\frac{s^4}{6Z_MV(\mathbf x)},
 \qquad\mathbf x\in E_4(M).
 \tag{10}
\]
All slab allocations are included before restricting the domain. If a nonzero allocation uses a different slab, the difference of its indicator arguments has absolute value at least
\[
 T_M(v)-|v|\,|u_i|=2+(2M-|u_i|)|v|\geq2,
\]
which contradicts their required difference being less than one. This corrected non-strict bound also covers $v=0$.

The accepted proof additionally gives finite third local count moments, infinite fourth moments on balls and finite-almost-everywhere full densities globally, including outside $E_n(M)$. We use those whole-space conclusions, not merely (6)–(10).

### 3. Nonnegative residual kernels for the two missing identities

Define translation- and rotation-invariant symmetric kernels, assigned zero on degenerate configurations,
\[
 K_2(x_1,x_2)=\frac{\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}}{D(x_1,x_2)},\qquad
 K_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)=\frac{\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}}{A(x_1,x_2,x_3)}.
\]
Let $\mathcal M$ denote integration over one last coordinate and put
\[
 I_2=\int_{\mathbb R^3}K_2(0,u)\,du=2\pi M^2,
 \qquad B_3=2\pi^2M^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
We shall use
\[
 \mathcal M K_3\leq B_3K_2.
 \tag{12}
\]
To prove it, fix a pair of length $d\in(0,M)$. With $\ell$ its affine line, area is $d\,\operatorname{dist}(z,\ell)/2$. Enlarging the allowed third vertices to $B(x_1,M)$ gives
\[
 \int K_3(x_1,x_2,z)\,dz
 \leq\frac2d\int_{B(x_1,M)}
       \frac{dz}{\operatorname{dist}(z,\ell)}
 =\frac{2\pi^2M^2}{d}.
\]
The cylindrical integral equals
$4\pi\int_0^M\sqrt{M^2-r^2}\,dr=\pi^2M^2$. If the pair is outside $E_2(M)$, the triangle kernel is zero for every third vertex except irrelevant degenerate configurations. Tonelli justifies these nonnegative integrals and also proves anchored integrability of $K_3$.

Choose the final target coefficients
\[
 a_2=\frac{\lambda}{4I_2}=\frac{\lambda}{8\pi M^2},
 \qquad
 a_3=\frac{a_2}{4B_3}=\frac{\lambda}{64\pi^3M^4}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Define the second component's desired local densities by
\[
 q_2=2a_2K_2-\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}r_2,
 \qquad
 q_3=2a_3K_3-\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}r_3.
 \tag{14}
\]
The second and third bounds in (1) are exactly sufficient for
\[
 c_2\leq a_2,\qquad c_3\leq a_3.
 \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, substituting $s=\lambda Z_0/J$ into (6)–(7) reduces the first inequality to
$\lambda M^3\leq J^2/(8\pi^2Z_0)$, and the second to
$(\lambda M^3)^2\leq J^3/(32\pi^4Z_0^2)$.
Equations (6)–(9) and (15) imply
\[
 a_2K_2\leq q_2\leq2a_2K_2,
 \qquad a_3K_3\leq q_3\leq2a_3K_3.
 \tag{16}
\]
Set
\[
 F_3=q_3,\qquad F_2=q_2-\mathcal Mq_3.
 \tag{17}
\]
By (12), (13) and (16),
\[
 F_2\geq(a_2-2a_3B_3)K_2=\frac{a_2}{2}K_2>0
 \quad\text{on }E_2(M),
 \tag{18}
\]
and $F_3\geq a_3K_3>0$ on $E_3(M)$. Both are integrable in anchored relative coordinates. They are symmetric, measurable and translation invariant. Their supports have diameter below $M$.

Write
\[
 Q_j=\int q_j(0,x_2,\ldots,x_j)\,dx_2\cdots dx_j,
 \quad
 Z_j=\int F_j(0,x_2,\ldots,x_j)\,dx_2\cdots dx_j.
\]
Then $Z_3=Q_3>0$, $Z_2=Q_2-Q_3>0$ and
\[
 Q_2\leq2a_2I_2=\lambda/2,
 \qquad Q_3\leq2a_3B_3I_2=\lambda/8.
 \tag{19}
\]
All these are finite positive numbers; no subtraction of infinite measures occurs.

### 4. A stationary process of separated clusters with the residual densities

Let
\[
 h=3M,\quad v_h=|B(0,h)|=36\pi M^3,\quad
 \eta=1/v_h,
 \quad \kappa=\eta e^{-\eta v_h}=\frac1{36e\pi M^3}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Start with a homogeneous Poisson process $\Pi$ of intensity $\eta$. Retain a centre $z\in\Pi$ if it has no other point of $\Pi$ within distance $h$. Call the retained process $P$. This is a measurable, translation- and rotation-invariant thinning. Distinct retained centres have separation greater than $3M$ almost surely; equality of an original pair distance to $3M$ has zero expectation and thus probability zero in any bounded window.

The intensity of $P$ is $\kappa$. Here is the Poisson insertion identity needed for that calculation:
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{z\in\Pi} f(z,\Pi\setminus\{z\})
 =\eta\int_{\mathbb R^3}\mathbb E f(z,\Pi)\,dz
 \tag{21}
\]
for nonnegative $f$. For $z$ in a bounded set and $f$ depending on a bounded region, condition on the Poisson number of points in a region containing both. In the resulting nonnegative series, selecting one point replaces $n/n!$ by $1/(n-1)!$, giving (21). A monotone-class argument and nonnegative approximation extend the identity; the bounded-region case already covers the application here. Apply it to the indicator that $z$ is in the observation set and that the ball of volume $v_h$ around $z$ is empty. Its void probability is $e^{-\eta v_h}$, proving (20).

We now give each retained centre an independent cluster mark, using the same mark law at every centre. Define
\[
 \beta=\lambda-Q_2+Q_3/2=\lambda-Z_2-Z_3/2>0,
\]
\[
 p_1=\beta/\kappa,
 \qquad p_2=Z_2/(2\kappa),
 \qquad p_3=Z_3/(6\kappa),
 \qquad p_0=1-p_1-p_2-p_3.
 \tag{22}
\]
These are probabilities. In fact $\beta\geq\lambda/2$ by (19), and
\[
 \beta+Z_2/2+Z_3/6
 =\lambda-Q_2/2+Q_3/6\leq\lambda\leq\kappa.
 \tag{23}
\]
The first inequality uses $Q_3\leq Q_2$, a consequence of $F_2\geq0$; the last follows from the first bound in (1). Thus $p_0\geq0$, and all the other probabilities are nonnegative.

A mark zero emits nothing. A mark one emits the centre itself. A mark $j\in\{2,3\}$ emits the centre $z$ and $j-1$ offsets with joint probability density
\[
 (y_2,\ldots,y_j)\longmapsto
       F_j(0,y_2,\ldots,y_j)/Z_j.
 \tag{24}
\]
Call the union of the emitted points $C$. Every cluster has at most three points and diameter below $M$. In particular, every emitted point lies within distance $M$ of its centre. Points from different clusters have distance greater than $3M-2M=M$.

The probability law of $C$ is now specified throughout space. Its stationarity and isotropy follow from the thinning and the corresponding invariance of (24). To check existence, simplicity and global measure regularity together, attach the independent marks in (22)–(24) to every point of $\Pi$ before retaining centres. Let $U$ be the union of all clusters, including those at discarded centres. Then $C\subseteq U$ under this coupling.

Only the finitely many Poisson centres in the $M$ enlargement of a bounded observation set can influence it, and each emits at most three points. Thus $U$, and hence $C$, is almost surely locally finite; indeed $U$ has every local count moment finite. Diffuse relative-coordinate densities in (24), and diffuse Poisson centre locations, give simplicity within and between clusters by a countable union over pairs.

For explicit full-measure regularity of $U$, set $G_k=0$ for $k>3$, and for $1\leq k\leq3$ use the same-cluster kernels
\[
 G_k(x_1,\ldots,x_k)
 =\eta\sum_{j=k}^3 p_j\frac{j!}{(j-k)!Z_j}
       \mathcal M^{j-k}F_j(x_1,\ldots,x_k).
 \tag{25}
\]
For the $j=1$ term, the convention is $Z_1=1$, $F_1=1$, so it contributes $\eta p_1$ only at $k=1$.
Symmetry and translation invariance of $F_j$ show that each ordered selection of $k$ among the $j$ emitted points has the same integrated relative-coordinate density. Re-rooting at a selected point and permuting the remaining points has Jacobian one, which gives the factor $j!/(j-k)!$ in (25). Assigning the labels of an observed ordered tuple to their Poisson parents now gives the full density
\[
 \rho_U^{(n)}(\mathbf x)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_n}\prod_{I\in\pi}G_{|I|}(x_I).
 \tag{26}
\]
The Poisson factorial measure for the distinct parents follows from the same insertion calculation as (21), or from independent Poisson counts and finite partitions. All terms are nonnegative, so the construction and the full partition identity are justified by Tonelli. This proves full absolute continuity for $U$ at every order. Its finite local moments imply that these densities are finite almost everywhere on every bounded product cube, hence globally.

For every nonnegative tuple test, the sum for $C$ is at most the sum for $U$. Thus $\alpha_C^{(n)}\leq\alpha_U^{(n)}$, proving full absolute continuity and finite-almost-everywhere densities for $C$, with no need to assume independent retained centres. This also supplies the global properties outside the local formula domains.

Only one cluster can contribute to a tuple in $E_n(M)$. A first-moment sum over retained centres, whose intensity is $\kappa$, therefore suffices for the local densities. The selection calculation in (25), now with $\kappa$ replacing $\eta$, gives
\[
 \rho_C^{(2)}=F_2+\mathcal MF_3=q_2\quad\text{on }E_2(M),
\]
\[
 \rho_C^{(3)}=F_3=q_3\quad\text{on }E_3(M),
 \qquad
 \rho_C^{(4)}=0\quad\text{on }E_4(M).
 \tag{27}
\]
These include every actual tuple: distinct clusters cannot contribute in those domains, and each cluster contains at most three points. The intensity is
\[
 \kappa(p_1+2p_2+3p_3)=\beta+Z_2+Z_3/2=\lambda.
 \tag{28}
\]

### 5. Combining the two whole-space laws

Choose $X$ to have law
\[
 \mathcal L(X)=\tfrac12\mathcal L(Y)+\tfrac12\mathcal L(C).
 \tag{29}
\]
This means a single random choice of law for the entire process. It is not a union or independent superposition. Its stationarity, isotropy, simplicity and almost-sure local finiteness follow from the same properties of both alternatives, and its intensity is $\lambda$ by (28) and the corresponding identity for $Y$.

For every nonnegative tuple test, linearity of expectation in the probability law gives the full measure identity
\[
 \alpha_X^{(n)}=\tfrac12\alpha_Y^{(n)}+\tfrac12\alpha_C^{(n)}.
 \tag{30}
\]
Both measures are globally absolutely continuous with finite-almost-everywhere densities at the required orders, so their sum has these properties. In particular, no singular component is added on coplanar configurations or elsewhere.

Substituting (14), (27) and (10) into (30) proves (3), with $a_2,a_3$ as in (13) and
\[
 a_4=\frac{s^4}{12Z_M}=\frac{s^4M^3}{12Z_0}>0.
 \tag{31}
\]
Every lower-order correction is exact. No background or cross-component partition term is discarded: alternative component laws are selected by (29), and their full measures combine by (30). The construction of each alternative, including all parent or slab allocations, was handled before this mixing step.

Both components have finite third local moments. The slab component has infinite fourth moment in every nonempty open ball and has positive mixture probability, so the same is true of $X$. This completes the proof of Theorem 1.

### 6. Why this new method still cannot establish all of S1

The parameter restriction in Theorem 1 is not merely left unexamined. The next result identifies a genuine limitation of the correction method, even if its sufficient constants and mark probabilities are improved.

**Theorem 2.** Fix $M>0$. Let $Y$ be any slab law of the form (5) with any finite positive amplitude $s$. Let $C$ be a simple locally finite point process whose points are almost surely partitioned into clusters of size at most three, each of diameter below $M$, such that points belonging to different clusters have distance at least $M$. Consider a convex mixture
\[
 \mathcal L(X)=\theta\mathcal L(Y)+(1-\theta)\mathcal L(C),
 \qquad0\leq\theta\leq1.
\]
Assume $X$ has homogeneous finite positive intensity $\lambda$, that its full second and third factorial measures are globally Lebesgue absolutely continuous, and that their densities equal $a_2/D$ and $a_3/A$ on $E_2(M)$ and $E_3(M)$, with $a_2,a_3>0$. Then
\[
 a_2\geq B_3a_3,
 \qquad \lambda M^3\leq18/\pi.
 \tag{32}
\]
The full global density hypotheses of S1 suffice for the density manipulations in this statement. No fourth identity or finite fourth moment is used.

**Proof.** If $\theta=1$, the pair law already fails by v01, so suppose $\theta<1$. For an ordered pair of points of $C$ at distance less than $M$, there is at most one third point forming a triple of diameter less than $M$. Indeed all such points must lie in the same cluster, and that cluster has size at most three. Integrating this deterministic count inequality against a nonnegative pair test and taking expectations gives
\[
 \int \mathbf1_{E_3(M)}(x_1,x_2,z)
           \rho_C^{(3)}(x_1,x_2,z)\,dz
 \leq \rho_C^{(2)}(x_1,x_2)
 \tag{33}
\]
for almost every pair in $E_2(M)$. Full absolute continuity of $C$ follows from that of $X$ and $Y$ when $\theta<1$, since $(1-\theta)\alpha_C^{(n)}\leq\alpha_X^{(n)}$.

Use the two reciprocal identities for $X$ and the exact mixture identity to eliminate $\rho_C^{(2)}$ and $\rho_C^{(3)}$ from (33). For a pair with separation $d\in(0,M)$ this yields, almost everywhere,
\[
 \frac{a_2}{d}-\theta r_2(d)
 \geq a_3 L_M(d)-\theta R_M(d),
 \tag{34}
\]
where, with $e$ any fixed unit vector,
\[
 L_M(d)=\int \mathbf1_{E_3(M)}(0,de,z)\,
           A(0,de,z)^{-1}\,dz,
\]
\[
 R_M(d)=\int \mathbf1_{E_3(M)}(0,de,z)\,
           r_3(0,de,z)\,dz.
\]
The right expressions depend only on $d$, by isotropy of $Y$ and the kernels. The change to relative coordinates and polar integration consequently makes (34) valid for almost every $d$; no density version on a single prescribed ray is being inferred from a general null-set assertion.

Let $z_\perp$ be the component of $z$ perpendicular to $e$. Since $A(0,de,z)=d|z_\perp|/2$, dominated convergence on the ball $B(0,M)$ gives
\[
 dL_M(d)=2\int_{B(0,M)\cap B(de,M)}\frac{dz}{|z_\perp|}
 \longrightarrow 2\int_{B(0,M)}\frac{dz}{|z_\perp|}
 =B_3.
 \tag{35}
\]
The dominating function is integrable by the cylindrical calculation in (12).

Equation (6), or its explicit v01 expression, gives $d r_2(d)\to0$. We also have
\[
 dR_M(d)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{36}
\]
To verify this last limit, fix $z_\perp\ne0$. In (9), with $u=de,w=z$, the equation $v_0\cdot de=q_2-q_1$ implies
$|v_0|\geq|q_2-q_1|/d$. For almost every $\mathbf q$ the right side diverges as $d\downarrow0$, so bounded convergence gives $\Psi_M(de,z)\to0$. By (7),
\[
 0\leq d\,\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}r_3(0,de,z)
 \leq\frac{2c_3\mathbf1_{B(0,M)}(z)}{|z_\perp|}.
\]
This is an integrable dominating function, proving (36). Taking (34) along a sequence from its full-measure set with $d\downarrow0$, and using (35)–(36), proves $a_2\geq B_3a_3$.

Now let $N=X(B(0,r))$ for any $0<r<M/2$, and put
\[
 \mu=\mathbb EN=\lambda|B(0,r)|,
 \quad F_2=\mathbb E(N)_2,
 \quad F_3=\mathbb E(N)_3.
\]
The reciprocal kernels are integrable on this bounded product domain at orders two and three; (11)–(12) prove the needed bounds. Thus these moments are finite, and the raw third moment is finite as well. The full density identities give
\[
 F_2=a_2\int_{B(0,r)^2}D^{-1},
 \qquad F_3=a_3\int_{B(0,r)^3}A^{-1}.
\]
For each pair in the ball, the integral over a third point in the ball is at most $B_3/D$ by (12). Together with $a_3\leq a_2/B_3$, this proves
\[
 F_3\leq F_2.
 \tag{37}
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz applied to $\sqrt N$ and $\sqrt N(N-1)$ gives
\[
 F_2^2\leq\mu\,\mathbb E[N(N-1)^2]
 =\mu(F_3+F_2)\leq2\mu F_2.
\]
Here $N(N-1)^2=(N)_3+(N)_2$, and all the required moments are finite. Since $F_2>0$, we have $F_2\leq2\mu$. Nonnegativity of variance then gives
\[
 \mu^2\leq\mathbb EN^2=F_2+\mu\leq3\mu,
\]
so $\mu\leq3$. Let $r\uparrow M/2$ in the deterministic inequality
$\lambda(4\pi/3)r^3\leq3$. The result is
$\lambda M^3\leq18/\pi$, proving (32). $\square$

Theorem 2 concerns the specified mixture and correction structure. The inequality $F_3\leq F_2$ was derived from that structure and is not asserted for an arbitrary S1 process. The result therefore supplies no general upper-intensity obstruction for S1. In particular, a different law with larger clusters and an exact treatment of their fourth-order contribution is outside its scope.

### 7. Remaining obligation

Theorem 1 obtains every geometric and measure requirement of S1 and all three identities, but only for the parameter range (2). Theorem 2 proves that this particular correction scheme cannot be extended to all intensities merely by optimising the sufficient constants, changing the mixture probability, or changing the separated cluster law while retaining clusters of size at most three and the stated slab component.

For the unchanged S1, a construction covering the remaining values of $\lambda_0M^3$ is still required. It must handle the fourth-order contribution of any additional source of high local counts while preserving both lower identities. No such construction or general non-existence theorem is supplied here. Spatial dilation preserves $\lambda_0M^3$ and cannot fill this gap. Independent superposition produces new full-density allocations and has not been proved to preserve (3).
