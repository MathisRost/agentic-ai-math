## Exact claim and scope

**Theorem.** For every independently prescribed \(\lambda_0>0\) and \(M>0\), there is one stationary, simple, almost surely locally finite point process \(X\) on all of \(\mathbb R^3\), with
\[
 \mathbb EX(B)=\lambda_0|B|
\]
for every bounded Borel set \(B\), whose full ordered factorial expectation measures of orders \(n=2,3,4\) have nonnegative measurable densities finite Lebesgue-almost everywhere globally, with no singular component. For finite positive constants \(a_2,a_3,a_4\) depending on \((\lambda_0,M)\),
\[
 \rho_X^{(2)}=\frac{a_2}{D}\ \text{on }E_2(M),\qquad
 \rho_X^{(3)}=\frac{a_3}{A}\ \text{on }E_3(M),\qquad
 \rho_X^{(4)}=\frac{a_4}{V}\ \text{on }E_4(M),
 \tag{1}
\]
as almost-everywhere identities of full measures. Here \(D,A,V,E_n(M)\) are exactly those of S1. All nondegenerate shapes of diameter below \(M\) are included, with no positive lower geometric threshold.

The process has finite third count moments on every bounded set and infinite fourth count moments in every nonempty open ball. Neither a fourth-moment assumption nor a representation assumption is added to S1. No restriction on \(\lambda_0M^3\) is imposed.

The new ingredient is a Cox process supported, conditionally, on a stationary family of separated spherical surfaces. Its full local fourth density is exactly reciprocal volume. Its lower densities approach reciprocal distance and area in an integrable manner as a freely chosen sphere scale increases. A small separated-cluster component corrects the remaining two deficits. The components may have different intensities; their whole-law mixture has the prescribed intensity.

## Argument

Write \(\lambda=\lambda_0\), and put
\[
 \kappa=\frac1{36e\pi M^3},\qquad
 \lambda_C=\min\{\lambda,\kappa/2\},\qquad
 \lambda_Y=2\lambda-\lambda_C>0.
 \tag{2}
\]
The sphere component will have intensity \(\lambda_Y\), the cluster component intensity \(\lambda_C\), and the final law will choose between them with equal probabilities.

### 1. Sphere parameters and a finite probability budget

Set
\[
 C_0=\frac{\pi^{3/2}}{3\sqrt3}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Choose a finite \(K>0\) large enough that, on defining
\[
 c=\sqrt{\frac{C_0K^3}{\lambda_Y}},\qquad
 F=\frac{K^4}{c^3}
   =\frac{\lambda_Y^{3/2}}{C_0^{3/2}\sqrt K},
\]
we have
\[
 F\leq\frac1{10000M}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For example \(K=10^8M^2\lambda_Y^3/C_0^3\) gives equality. The scale \(L\geq M\) will be chosen later; (4) is independent of \(L\). For \(q\geq0\) and \(R>0\), put
\[
 f(q)=\frac K L e^{-q/L^2},\qquad
 a(R)=\frac{c}{f(R^2)},\qquad
 \gamma(R)=\frac{2R f(R^2)^4}{c^3}
           =\frac{2FR}{L^4}e^{-4R^2/L^2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\gamma(R)\,dz\,dR\) will be the first intensity measure of the random sphere centres and radii. The function \(a(R)/(2R)\) will multiply surface area on a retained sphere.

We now realise that sphere intensity with surfaces separated by more than \(M\). Let
\[
 H=2M,\qquad b_k=2^kM,\qquad P_k=4b_k+2M,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,
\]
and partition the positive radii into
\[
 I_0=(0,M],\qquad I_k=(b_k/2,b_k]\quad(k\geq1).
 \tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
 p_k=H P_k^3\,\sup_{R\in I_k}\gamma(R).
 \tag{7}
\]
Each \(p_k\) is finite and positive. We claim that \(\sum_kp_k<1\), uniformly over \(L\geq M\).

Indeed \(P_k\leq6b_k\). For \(k=0\),
\[
 p_0\leq864FM(M/L)^4\leq864FM.
 \tag{8}
\]
For \(k\geq1\), with \(t_k=b_k/L\), the lower endpoint of the band gives
\[
 p_k\leq864FM\,t_k^4e^{-t_k^2}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Among the dyadic \(t_k\leq1\), the fourth powers sum to at most \(16/15\). For \(t>1\), the elementary series bound \(e^{t^2}\geq t^6/6\) gives \(t^4e^{-t^2}\leq6/t^2\); the remaining dyadic sum is at most \(8\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{k\geq0}p_k
 \leq864FM\left(1+\frac{16}{15}+8\right)
 =\frac{130464}{15}FM<9000FM\leq0.9.
 \tag{10}
\]
No limiting probability law is required: these are explicit probabilities for each finite choice of the parameters.

### 2. An actual stationary family of separated spheres

With probability \(p_k\), choose band \(k\). With the remaining probability, choose the empty sphere family. Conditional on band \(k\):

1. Choose \(Z\) uniformly in \([0,P_k)^3\), and use the lattice of centres \(Z+P_km\), \(m\in\mathbb Z^3\).
2. Independently choose \(U\) uniformly in \([0,H)\). At every centre the candidate radii are \(R=U+jH\) that lie in \(I_k\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\).
3. Independently for every centre and candidate radial index, retain the sphere with probability
   \[
    t_k(R)=\frac{\gamma(R)HP_k^3}{p_k}\in[0,1].
    \tag{11}
   \]

All marks can be specified on the countable set of centre and radial indices. The random number of radial indices in a realised band is finite. A zero radius, which in any case has phase probability zero, is omitted.

Uniform lattice translation gives centre intensity \(P_k^{-3}\,dz\). Uniform radial phase gives
\[
 \mathbb E_U\sum_{j:\,U+jH\in I_k}g(U+jH)
 =\frac1H\int_{I_k}g(R)\,dR
 \tag{12}
\]
for every nonnegative \(g\). Multiplication by \(p_k\) and (11), and summation over the bands, proves that the retained sphere parameter measure has first intensity exactly
\[
 \gamma(R)\,dz\,dR.
 \tag{13}
\]
This is a first-moment calculation; independence of different sphere parameters is neither asserted nor used.

The family is stationary. A spatial translation changes \(Z\) modulo its lattice period and relabels the centre indices; conditional on \(U,k\), the retention marks have a law invariant under that relabelling. The uniform phase is invariant, and the empty alternative is stationary.

At the same centre, distinct sphere surfaces have distance at least \(H=2M\). At different centres in band \(k\), their distance is at least
\[
 P_k-2b_k=2b_k+2M>M.
 \tag{14}
\]
Consequently any tuple of diameter below \(M\) lies on a single sphere.

Let \(S(z,R)\) denote the sphere and \(\sigma_{z,R}\) its two-dimensional surface area measure. Given the sphere family, place independent Poisson processes on its spheres with respective intensity measures
\[
 \frac{a(R)}{2R}\,\sigma_{z,R}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Explicitly, for each retained sphere sample a Poisson number of points with mean \(2\pi a(R)R\), and give them independent directions uniform on \(S^2\) about its centre. Independent count and direction sequences can be attached to the countable set of sphere indices. This specifies a measurable probability law directly.
Their union is \(Y\), with directing measure
\[
 \Lambda=\sum_{\text{retained spheres }(z,R)}
                   \frac{a(R)}{2R}\,\sigma_{z,R}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Every sphere has finite total directing mass \(2\pi a(R)R\), and its measure has no atoms. In a realised band only finitely many spheres can meet a bounded set: only centres within distance \(b_k\) of it can matter, and only finitely many radial indices are available. Thus \(Y\) is simple and almost surely locally finite. The Poisson draws preserve stationarity.

For any bounded Borel set \(B\), translation of the sphere centre gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}\sigma_{z,R}(B)\,dz=4\pi R^2|B|.
 \tag{17}
\]
Using (13), Tonelli, and \(q=R^2\), the intensity is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\Lambda(B)/|B|
 &=2\pi\int_0^\infty\gamma(R)a(R)R\,dR\\
 &=\frac{2\pi}{c^2}\int_0^\infty \sqrt q\,f(q)^3\,dq
 =\frac{\pi^{3/2}K^3}{3\sqrt3\,c^2}
 =\lambda_Y.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
The Gaussian integral here is
\(\int_0^\infty q^{1/2}e^{-3q/L^2}dq=L^3\sqrt\pi/(6\sqrt3)\).
In particular the mean intensity is independent of \(L\).

### 3. Full absolute continuity everywhere

Conditional Poisson factorial identities give
\[
 \alpha_Y^{(n)}=\mathbb E\Lambda^{\otimes n}
 \tag{19}
\]
as extended nonnegative measures. This identity follows first for products of disjoint sets from Poisson factorial count identities and then for general nonnegative tests by the usual simple-function/monotone-class extension. The directing measures are nonatomic, so the product diagonals have zero measure. Equation (19) includes every allocation among the spheres.

We establish full absolute continuity before computing local densities. Fix a band \(k\), an order \(1\leq n\leq4\), and an allocation of the tuple labels to specified lattice-centre and radial indices \((m_i,j_i)\), allowing repeated sphere indices. Write a point in that allocation as
\[
 x_i=Z+P_km_i+(U+j_iH)\omega_i,\qquad \omega_i\in S^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
The parameter weight is absolutely continuous in \(Z,U\) and product surface measure in the \(\omega_i\). Factors from (15), band membership and the retention probabilities only multiply it by a nonnegative measurable function. A retained sphere repeated in an allocation contributes its retention probability once, as appropriate; this distinction does not affect absolute continuity.

For positive radii, the angular derivatives in (20) span the tangent plane \(\omega_i^\perp\) of the \(i\)-th position coordinate. Modulo these tangent directions, the derivative with respect to \((Z,U)\) has rows
\[
 (\omega_i^{\mathsf T},1),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
 \tag{21}
\]
These rows have rank \(n\) for almost every product collection of directions when \(n\leq4\). For \(n=4\), three distinct unit vectors are almost surely affinely independent, and their affine plane meets \(S^2\) in a surface-null circle or a smaller set, which excludes a fourth vector almost surely. For \(n\leq3\), a line meets the sphere in at most two points, giving the corresponding simpler argument.

It follows that (20) has differential of rank \(3n\) almost everywhere. In local angular charts, a nonzero maximal minor and the inverse-function theorem give coordinates in which this map is a coordinate projection. Fubini then shows that the pushforward assigns zero mass to every Lebesgue-null position set. Cover the regular parameter set by countably many such charts; the rank-failure set already has parameter measure zero. This proves absolute continuity for this allocation. Countable summation over allocations, followed by the mixture over \(k\), proves full absolute continuity of \(\alpha_Y^{(n)}\), \(n\leq4\), on the entire position space. No coplanar or other singular component is left over.

For each fixed band the local moments of all orders are finite. In fact the number of spheres meeting a fixed bounded set is bounded for that band, \(a(R)\) is bounded for \(0<R\leq b_k\), and the total mass of each sphere is at most \(2\pi b_k\sup_{0<R\leq b_k}a(R)<\infty\). Thus the directing mass, and hence all conditional-Poisson count moments after averaging within this band, have deterministic finite bounds. The fixed-band full densities are therefore finite almost everywhere.

### 4. Third local moments and global fourth-density finiteness

The mixture over bands needs additional checks: a countable sum of finite densities need not be finite.

First, a bounded ball \(B=B(x,r)\) can meet at most \(J_B<\infty\) candidate spheres, with \(J_B\) independent of the band. Any relevant centre lies in \(B(x,b_k+r)\). The number of such lattice centres is uniformly bounded because their spacing is \(P_k\geq4b_k\) and \(b_k\geq M\). At each centre, the radii of spheres meeting \(B\) lie in an interval of length at most \(2r\), so the grid of step \(H\) contributes at most \(\lfloor2r/H\rfloor+2\) of them. Retention can only decrease the number.

Also
\[
 \sigma_{z,R}(B)\leq C_B:=4\pi r^2
 \tag{22}
\]
uniformly in \(z,R\). For \(R\leq r\), use the entire sphere area. For \(R>r\), a nonempty intersection is a cap. If \(d=|z-x|>0\), its boundary cosine is
\[
 \frac{R^2+d^2-r^2}{2Rd}
 \geq\frac{\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}R.
 \]
The cap area is at most \(2\pi R^2(1-\sqrt{1-r^2/R^2})\leq2\pi r^2\), proving (22).

Put \(m_{z,R}(B)=a(R)\sigma_{z,R}(B)/(2R)\). The deterministic surface-count bound, (22), (17) and (13) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\Lambda(B)^3
 &\leq J_B^2\,\mathbb E\sum_{(z,R)}m_{z,R}(B)^3\\
 &\leq\frac{\pi J_B^2 C_B^2|B|}{2}
           \int_0^\infty\frac{\gamma(R)a(R)^3}{R}\,dR\\
 &=\frac{\pi J_B^2 C_B^2|B|}{2}
           \,2\int_0^\infty f(R^2)\,dR<\infty,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{23}
\]
since the last integral, including its factor two, equals \(K\sqrt\pi\).
Therefore all bounded third count moments of \(Y\) are finite. In view of full absolute continuity, its full second and third densities are finite almost everywhere globally.

For order four, a geometric radius bound excludes all sufficiently large bands in a neighbourhood of almost every quadruple. Fix a noncoplanar quadruple, translate its first point to zero, let \(u_i=x_i-x_1\), \(i=2,3,4\), and let \(W\) be the invertible matrix with these rows. Let \(D_*\) be its diameter. Once \(2b_k+2M>D_*\), (14) prevents an allocation using two different centres. Thus all four points must be on concentric spheres.

Their radii then have the form \(R+Hm_i\), with \(m=(m_2,m_3,m_4)\in\mathbb Z^3\), and the reverse triangle inequality implies \(|Hm_i|\leq D_*\). Only finitely many \(m\)'s are possible for a fixed diameter bound. If the common centre relative to \(x_1\) is \(z\), then \(|z|=R\) and
\[
 |u_i-z|^2=(R+Hm_i)^2.
 \]
Consequently, with \(n=z/R\) and
\(b_i=|u_i|^2-H^2m_i^2\),
\[
 Wn=-Hm+\frac{b}{2R},\qquad |n|=1,
 \]
so
\[
 R\,\bigl|1-H|W^{-1}m|\bigr|
       \leq\frac12|W^{-1}b|.
 \tag{24}
\]
This bounds \(R\) unless
\[
 H|W^{-1}m|=1.
 \tag{25}
\]
For \(m=0\), the exceptional condition is impossible. For every fixed nonzero integer \(m\), (25) is contained in the zero set of the nonzero polynomial
\[
 H^2|\operatorname{adj}(W)m|^2-(\det W)^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
Nontriviality follows by substituting \(W=tI\), which gives
\(t^4(H^2|m|^2-t^2)\). Thus the union of these exceptional sets over all integer \(m\), together with the coplanarity set, is Lebesgue-null.

Near a quadruple outside that union, bound the diameter and keep \(W^{-1}\) bounded. Only finitely many \(m\)'s can occur under that diameter bound, and their gaps in (24) stay uniformly away from zero in a sufficiently small neighbourhood. Equation (24) gives a uniform finite radius bound there. All sufficiently large bands have lower radius endpoint above this bound and cannot contribute; different-centre allocations in those bands were already excluded by (14).

On this bounded configuration neighbourhood, the full fourth measure is therefore a finite sum of fixed-band measures. Each is locally finite by Section 3, so the density is finite almost everywhere on the neighbourhood. A countable neighbourhood cover proves finite-almost-everywhere fourth density globally outside the null exceptional union. Section 3 already proved absence of mass on that union. This establishes the full global regularity required by S1, including outside the local formula domains.

### 5. Exact local full densities of the sphere component

By (14) and the radial separation \(H>M\), every tuple in \(E_n(M)\) lies on one sphere. Thus the local part of (19) is exactly the same-sphere contribution, which by (13) is
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}\!\int_0^\infty
   \gamma(R)\left(\frac{a(R)}{2R}\right)^n
   \sigma_{z,R}^{\otimes n}(d\mathbf x)\,dR\,dz.
 \tag{27}
\]
This conclusion is drawn only after the full all-allocation identity and global regularity were established.

Here are the elementary coarea calculations for (27). The notation
\(\delta(|x-z|^2-q)\,dx\), for \(q>0\), means the measure
\(\sigma_{z,\sqrt q}(dx)/(2\sqrt q)\), by the one-dimensional coarea formula. Products use different position coordinates. With \(q=R^2\), set
\[
 w_n(q)=\frac{\gamma(\sqrt q)a(\sqrt q)^n}{2\sqrt q}.
 \]
Then
\[
 w_4=c,\qquad w_3=f,\qquad w_2=f^2/c,\qquad w_1=f^3/c^2.
 \tag{28}
\]
The local density is obtained by integrating
\[
 w_n(q)\prod_{i=1}^n\delta(|x_i-z|^2-q)
 \tag{29}
\]
over \(z\in\mathbb R^3,q>0\). Formula (29) is coarea notation, not multiplication of unspecified distributional densities. One precise justification is to work first with continuous compactly supported tests in a regular incidence chart, with \(q\) bounded away from zero, and replace each delta by a smooth one-dimensional approximate identity. Integrating in the radial position coordinates gives (27) in the limit, since the radial derivative of each constraint is \(2\sqrt q\). Alternatively, keep the position coordinates and change \(q\) and \(n-1\) centre coordinates to the \(n\) constraints, leaving \(4-n\) centre coordinates free. This is an ordinary change of variables with the nonzero determinants computed below; the same approximate-identity limit gives (29). Subtracting the first constraint from the others has determinant one. Smooth orthonormal coordinates on the relevant nondegenerate configuration charts have the stated plane and normal Jacobians. A countable chart decomposition and nonnegative exhaustion extend the identity to arbitrary nonnegative tests and unbounded \(z,q\). Thus no interchange of unspecified distributions or finite fourth-moment assumption is involved.

For a pair, take \(x_1=0,x_2=de\). Subtracting the first sphere equation from the second fixes \(z\cdot e=d/2\), with Jacobian \(2d\). The first equation then fixes \(q=d^2/4+|z_\perp|^2\), with Jacobian one in \(q\). Polar integration of the two free centre coordinates gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_2(d)
 &=\frac{\pi}{2d}\int_{d^2/4}^\infty w_2(q)\,dq\\
 &=\frac{A_2}{d}e^{-d^2/(2L^2)},
 \qquad A_2=\frac{\pi K^2}{4c}>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{30}
\]

For a noncollinear triangle \(0,u,v\), the two subtracted equations fix the projection of \(z\) onto the triangle plane at its circumcentre. Their Jacobian in the two plane coordinates is
\(4|u\times v|=8A\). Let \(R_\triangle\) denote the triangle's circumradius and \(h\) the normal coordinate of \(z\). The first equation fixes \(q=R_\triangle^2+h^2\). Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)
 &=\frac1{8A}\int_{\mathbb R}f(R_\triangle^2+h^2)\,dh\\
 &=\frac{A_3}{A}e^{-R_\triangle^2/L^2},
 \qquad A_3=\frac{K\sqrt\pi}{8}>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{31}
\]

For four affinely independent points, the equations
\(|x_i-z|^2-q=0\), \(i=1,\ldots,4\), have the unique circumsphere solution, with \(q>0\). Subtracting the first row from the other three in their derivative with respect to \((z,q)\) gives absolute determinant
\[
 8\left|\det[x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1]\right|=48V.
 \]
Using \(w_4=c\) proves
\[
 r_4(\mathbf x)=\frac{c}{48V(\mathbf x)}
 \quad\text{on }E_4(M).
 \tag{32}
\]
There is no missing permutation factor: (19) and (27) concern ordered tuples, and the conditional Poisson factorial measure is the product intensity measure.

The exact fourth identity holds for every sphere scale \(L\geq M\). Meanwhile the two lower errors can be made small in anchored integral by increasing \(L\), without changing \(\lambda_Y,A_2,A_3,c\).

### 6. Small nonnegative kernels that correct both lower identities

Write
\[
 K_2=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}/D,\qquad
 K_3=\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}/A,
 \]
assigned zero on degenerate configurations, and let \(\mathcal M\) integrate over one final vertex. Put
\[
 B_L=\frac{8\pi A_3M^3}{L},\qquad \delta=2B_L.
 \tag{33}
\]
Define
\[
 q_3=\frac{A_3\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}}{A}
          \left(1-e^{-R_\triangle^2/L^2}\right),
 \tag{34}
\]
\[
 q_2=\frac{\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}}D
          \left[\delta+A_2\left(1-e^{-D^2/(2L^2)}\right)\right].
 \tag{35}
\]
These are symmetric, translation invariant, nonnegative kernels, with the indicated diameter supports.

The crucial uniform bound is
\[
 \mathcal Mq_3\leq B_LK_2.
 \tag{36}
\]
To prove it, fix a pair \(0,de\) with \(0<d<M\), and write \(r=|z_\perp|\) for the third vertex's distance to its line. On the allowed triangle domain,
\[
 R_\triangle=\frac{|z|\,|z-de|}{2r}\leq\frac{M^2}{2r}.
 \tag{37}
\]
Since \(1-e^{-t}\leq\min\{1,t\}\), with \(\varepsilon=M^2/(2L)\leq M/2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d\int q_3(0,de,z)\,dz
 &\leq2A_3\int_{B(0,M)}
       \frac1r\min\{1,\varepsilon^2/r^2\}\,dz\\
 &\leq8\pi A_3M\int_0^M\min\{1,\varepsilon^2/r^2\}\,dr\\
 &\leq16\pi A_3M\varepsilon
 =B_L.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{38}
\]
The second line uses cylindrical coordinates and bounds the axial length by \(2M\). The integral in the last bound is at most \(2\varepsilon\). Collinear third vertices form a null set. For pairs outside \(E_2(M)\), both sides of (36) are zero.

Set
\[
 F_3=q_3,\qquad F_2=q_2-\mathcal Mq_3.
 \tag{39}
\]
Equations (33), (35) and (36) give
\[
 F_2\geq B_LK_2>0\quad\text{on }E_2(M).
 \tag{40}
\]
Also \(F_3>0\) on \(E_3(M)\), since a nondegenerate triangle has positive circumradius.

Let \(Q_j\) and \(Z_j\) be the anchored integrals of \(q_j\) and \(F_j\), respectively. Radial integration gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}K_2(0,u)\,du=2\pi M^2
 \]
and, using \(1-e^{-d^2/(2L^2)}\leq d^2/(2L^2)\),
\[
 Q_2\leq
 \frac{32\pi^2A_3M^5}{L}
 +\frac{\pi A_2M^4}{2L^2}.
 \tag{41}
\]
Equation (36) proves anchored integrability of \(q_3\). Tonelli and (39) give
\[
 Z_3=Q_3>0,\qquad Z_2=Q_2-Q_3>0.
 \tag{42}
\]
In particular \(Q_3\leq Q_2\); all these quantities are finite.

We now fix the previously free scale, taking
\[
 L=\max\left\{
 M,\ \frac{128\pi^2A_3M^5}{\lambda_C},\
 M^2\sqrt{\frac{2\pi A_2}{\lambda_C}}
 \right\}.
 \tag{43}
\]
All parameters in this expression were fixed independently of \(L\). Equation (41) now implies \(Q_2\leq\lambda_C/2\). The sphere probability budget (10) still holds, because it was uniform over \(L\geq M\).

### 7. An actual correction process with these kernels

This section reproduces the finite-cluster construction used in v02, with the new kernels and the smaller prescribed intensity \(\lambda_C\).

Start with a homogeneous Poisson process of centres of intensity
\[
 \eta=\frac1{36\pi M^3}.
 \]
Retain a centre if there is no other centre within distance \(3M\). The retained centres have mutual separation greater than \(3M\) almost surely and intensity
\[
 \eta\exp\{-\eta|B(0,3M)|\}
 =\eta e^{-1}=\kappa.
 \tag{44}
\]
For completeness, the first-intensity calculation follows from Poisson insertion:
\(\mathbb E\sum_{z\in\Pi}g(z,\Pi\setminus\{z\})
=\eta\int\mathbb Eg(z,\Pi)\,dz\).
In a bounded window this identity is obtained by conditioning on the Poisson count, selecting one point, and replacing \(n/n!\) by \(1/(n-1)!\) in the nonnegative series. Exhaustion gives the stated identity. The probability of the required empty ball is \(e^{-\eta36\pi M^3}\). Original centre pairs at exactly distance \(3M\) have zero expected count in bounded windows, so the strict separation holds.

Let
\[
 \beta=\lambda_C-Q_2+Q_3/2
       =\lambda_C-Z_2-Z_3/2\geq\lambda_C/2>0.
 \]
Give every retained centre an independent size mark with probabilities
\[
 \pi_1=\beta/\kappa,\qquad
 \pi_2=Z_2/(2\kappa),\qquad
 \pi_3=Z_3/(6\kappa),\qquad
 \pi_0=1-\pi_1-\pi_2-\pi_3.
 \tag{45}
\]
They are nonnegative and sum to one, because
\[
 \beta+Z_2/2+Z_3/6
 =\lambda_C-Q_2/2+Q_3/6
 \leq\lambda_C\leq\kappa.
 \tag{46}
\]
A mark zero emits no points. A mark one emits the centre. A mark \(j=2,3\) emits the centre and \(j-1\) offsets with joint density
\[
 \frac{F_j(0,y_2,\ldots,y_j)}{Z_j}.
 \tag{47}
\]
These are genuine probability densities by (40)–(42). The emitted process is \(C\).

Every emitted cluster has diameter below \(M\), contains its centre and has size at most three. Points from different clusters are at distance greater than \(3M-2M=M\). Thus every eligible local tuple lies in one cluster.

Symmetry and translation invariance of \(F_j\) imply that every ordered selection of \(n\) of its \(j\) points has the same integrated relative-coordinate density. Re-root at any selected point and permute coordinates; the change has absolute Jacobian one. There are \(j!/(j-n)!\) such selections. The first intensity \(\kappa\) of retained centres therefore suffices for the local calculation, giving
\[
 \rho_C^{(2)}=F_2+\mathcal MF_3=q_2,\qquad
 \rho_C^{(3)}=F_3=q_3,\qquad
 \rho_C^{(4)}=0
 \tag{48}

on the corresponding \(E_n(M)\). Its intensity is

 \kappa(\pi_1+2\pi_2+3\pi_3)
 =\beta+Z_2+Z_3/2=\lambda_C.
 \tag{49}
\]
No independence of the retained centres is claimed.

\[
 C^\sharp(B)\leq3\Pi(B^{+M}),
 \tag{50}
\]
which has moments of all orders. Diffuse offsets and centre positions give simplicity. Define the same-parent kernels, for \(1\leq n\leq3\), by
\[
 G_n=\eta\sum_{j=n}^3
       \frac{\pi_j j!}{(j-n)!Z_j}\,\mathcal M^{j-n}F_j,
 \qquad G_n=0\quad(n>3),
 \tag{51}
\]
where the singleton convention is \(Z_1=F_1=1\). Labelling each tuple by its distinct Poisson parents gives the full density
\[
 \rho_{C^\sharp}^{(n)}(\mathbf x)
   =\sum_{\mathcal P}\prod_{I\in\mathcal P}G_{|I|}(x_I),
 \tag{52}
\]
summed over every partition of the tuple labels. The Poisson distinct-parent factorial identity follows from the same insertion calculation; Tonelli applies to all terms. Thus \(C^\sharp\) has full absolute continuity. Its finite local moments imply finite-almost-everywhere densities globally. For nonnegative tuple tests,
\(\alpha_C^{(n)}\leq\alpha_{C^\sharp}^{(n)}\), so \(C\) inherits these full global properties. Stationarity follows from the translation-equivariant thinning and invariant offset kernels.

### 8. The single law satisfying S1

Choose one whole-space law by one fair coin:
\[
 \mathcal L(X)=\tfrac12\mathcal L(Y)+\tfrac12\mathcal L(C).
 \tag{53}
\]
Both alternatives are stationary, simple and almost surely locally finite. Their mean intensities give
\[
 \mathbb EX(B)=\frac{\lambda_Y+\lambda_C}{2}|B|
              =\lambda|B|.
 \tag{54}
\]
For every nonnegative ordered-tuple test, conditioning on the coin gives the full extended measure identity
\[
 \alpha_X^{(n)}
 =\tfrac12\alpha_Y^{(n)}+\tfrac12\alpha_C^{(n)}.
 \tag{55}
\]
There are no tuples crossing between the alternative laws. All allocations within each actual alternative have already been included.
