## Exact claim and scope

S1 asks for a process at every independently prescribed pair
$(\lambda_0,M)\in(0,\infty)^2$, with all three exact full reciprocal-simplex
densities on nondegenerate configurations of diameter less than $M$. It
requires global absolute continuity of the full factorial measures, and
permits infinite fourth local moments.

We prove that **no Cox process can satisfy S1 for any such pair**. The
obstruction applies to arbitrary locally finite diffuse random directing
measures, including singular ones. It does not assume a log-Gaussian,
shot-noise, Poisson-carrier or other representation of the directing measure.
It uses neither stationarity nor the pair formula nor any fourth-moment
bound.

Here is the more precise result. All measures in the statement are full
measures, not cumulants or selected carrier contributions. Let
\[
 A(x,y,z)=\tfrac12|(y-x)\times(z-x)|,\qquad
 V(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
 =\tfrac16|\det(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1)|.
\]
Write $\Gamma_n=\mathbb E\Lambda^{\otimes n}$ for the extended moment measure
of a random locally finite nonnegative Borel measure $\Lambda$.

**Theorem.** Suppose that, for some $M>0$ and $a_3\in(0,\infty)$, the
restriction of $\Gamma_3$ to triples of diameter less than $M$ is exactly
\[
 \Gamma_3(dx\,dy\,dz)=\frac{a_3}{A(x,y,z)}\,dx\,dy\,dz,                 \tag{1}
\]
where the kernel is assigned zero on the Lebesgue-null collinear set and
there is no additional singular mass in this domain. Then, for every open
ball $U$ of radius less than $M/8$,
\[
 \Gamma_4\bigl(U^4\cap\{V>0\}\bigr)=0.                              \tag{2}
\]
More specifically, almost surely the restriction $\Lambda|_U$ is either
zero or carried by an affine plane, possibly by a smaller affine subspace.

In particular, (1) is incompatible with a full fourth density $a_4/V$ on all
noncoplanar quadruples of diameter less than $M$, for every $a_4>0$.
It is also incompatible with global absolute continuity of $\Gamma_4$:
that additional condition would force $\Lambda=0$ almost surely.

For a simple Cox process, its directing measure is diffuse almost surely
and $\alpha_X^{(n)}=\Gamma_n$. Thus the theorem excludes the entire assigned
Cox approach under S1. This is a representation-specific obstruction;
S1 itself allows processes that are not Cox. It supplies no authority to
narrow S1. S0 remains a separate historically false target.

## Argument

The decisive feature is the radial factorisation of the triangle kernel.
With one vertex fixed and the other two written in polar coordinates,
$A^{-1}$ separates as $2/(rs|u\times v|)$. After the volume Jacobians are
included, both radial variables have weight $r\,dr$. A derivative whose
integral against this weight vanishes therefore has zero squared integral
under the directing measure, when averaged with a third directing point.
The resulting local identities cannot hold about four affinely independent
centres carrying directing mass.

### 1. The Cox identity concerns the full moment measure

Let $\mu$ be a deterministic diffuse locally finite Borel measure and let
$X$ be Poisson with intensity measure $\mu$. On a bounded set $K$ with
$t=\mu(K)>0$, the count is Poisson with mean $t$, and conditional on the
count its locations are independent with law $\mu|_K/t$. Diffuseness makes
these locations distinct almost surely. For nonnegative measurable $f$
supported in $K^n$, conditioning on the count gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\mu\sum_{x_1,\ldots,x_n\in X}^{\ne}f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 =\mathbb E_\mu(N)_n\int f\,d(\mu|_K/t)^{\otimes n}
 =\int f\,d\mu^{\otimes n},
\]
because a Poisson count has $\mathbb E(N)_n=t^n$. For $t=0$ both sides
vanish. Exhausting space by bounded sets and using monotone convergence
extends the identity to every nonnegative $f$ on the whole product space.
Conditioning on a random locally finite diffuse $\Lambda$, and then using
Tonelli, proves
\[
                  \alpha_X^{(n)}=\mathbb E\Lambda^{\otimes n}.       \tag{3}
\]
No finite expected $n$th local mass is needed.

A simple Cox process necessarily has diffuse directing measure almost
surely. Indeed, conditional on a directing measure with any atom of mass
$t>0$, the Poisson count at that atom has probability
$1-e^{-t}(1+t)>0$ of being at least two. Such a conditional law is not
almost surely simple. Conditional almost-sure simplicity therefore
excludes atomic directing measures.

Formula (3) includes every observed tuple. If $\Lambda$ has a decomposition
into carriers, expanding its tensor power includes every assignment to
those carriers. No assumption about such a decomposition and no omission
of cross-carrier terms occurs below.

### 2. A zero-square consequence of the third moment

Fix
\[
                              R=M/4.
\]
For a real test function $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(B(0,R))$, define
\[
 L\varphi(w)=w\cdot\nabla\varphi(w)+2\varphi(w),\qquad
 F_\varphi(x)=\int L\varphi(y-x)\,\Lambda(dy).                       \tag{4}
\]
The latter integral is finite for every $x$ on the event of local
finiteness: its integrand is bounded and has compact support. It is
measurable as the integral of a Borel kernel against a random measure.

Let $W$ be any bounded Borel set. Whenever $y-x$ and $z-x$ lie in
$B(0,R)$, the triple has diameter less than $2R<M$. Consequently (1)
determines every part of its third moment measure, including the absence
of singular mass on collinear triples.

We first justify signed integration. In polar coordinates $w=ru$ and
$w'=sv$, with $u,v\in S^2$ and $r,s>0$,
\[
 \frac{dw\,dw'}{A(0,w,w')}
 =\frac{2rs}{|u\times v|}\,dr\,ds\,d\sigma(u)\,d\sigma(v).            \tag{5}
\]
Here $\sigma$ is ordinary surface area measure. For any fixed $u$,
\[
 \int_{S^2}\frac{d\sigma(v)}{|u\times v|}
 =2\pi\int_0^\pi d\theta=2\pi^2,
\]
so
\[
 \int_{B(0,R)^2}\frac{dw\,dw'}{A(0,w,w')}
 =2(8\pi^3)(R^2/2)^2=4\pi^3R^4<\infty.                            \tag{6}
\]
Null collinear directions do not affect this integral. In particular,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E\int_W
       \left(\int |L\varphi(y-x)|\,\Lambda(dy)\right)^2\Lambda(dx)\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\leq
   a_3|W|\,\|L\varphi\|_\infty^2\,4\pi^3R^4<\infty.                \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The equality of nonnegative integrals underlying (7) uses (1) and Tonelli.
It establishes absolute integrability before any signed rearrangement.

We may therefore expand the square in (4), apply (1), and use signed
Fubini. Translation of the relative coordinates yields
\[
 \mathbb E\int_W F_\varphi(x)^2\,\Lambda(dx)
 =a_3|W|\int_{B(0,R)^2}
      \frac{L\varphi(w)L\varphi(w')}{A(0,w,w')}\,dw\,dw'.             \tag{8}
\]
For each $u\in S^2$, the radial integral is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_\varphi(u)
 &=\int_0^R r L\varphi(ru)\,dr\\
 &=\int_0^R\{r^2\partial_r\varphi(ru)+2r\varphi(ru)\}\,dr\\
 &=[r^2\varphi(ru)]_{r=0}^{r=R}=0.                                \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]
At the outer endpoint the function vanishes near $R$; at zero it is
bounded and the factor $r^2$ vanishes. Applying (5) to (8), with the
absolute integrability from (7), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\int_W F_\varphi(x)^2\,\Lambda(dx)
 =2a_3|W|\int_{S^2\times S^2}
       \frac{H_\varphi(u)H_\varphi(v)}{|u\times v|}
          \,d\sigma(u)\,d\sigma(v)
 =0.                                                             \tag{10}
\]
This calculation is valid at arbitrarily small radii and uses no lower
geometric cutoff.

We need the resulting identity simultaneously for all test functions.
Choose a countable family dense in $C_c^\infty(B(0,R))$ in the uniform
$C^1$ norm of their zero extensions to $\overline{B(0,R)}$. Such a family
exists: this test-function space is a subspace of the separable metric
space $C^1(\overline{B(0,R)})$ with the uniform function-and-gradient norm.
Alternatively, one may take countable dense families on a sequence of
compact inner balls and take their union.

Apply (10) to that family and the countable exhaustion
$W_k=[-k,k]^3$. Nonnegativity and the countable intersection of the
resulting probability-one events give, almost surely, a Borel set $G$
of full $\Lambda$ measure on which $F_\varphi(x)=0$ for every member of
the family. For a fixed such $x$, the linear functional in (4) is
continuous in the uniform $C^1$ norm, since
\[
 |F_{\varphi_1}(x)-F_{\varphi_2}(x)|
 \leq (R+2)\|\varphi_1-\varphi_2\|_{C^1}
                    \Lambda(\overline{B(x,R)}).
\]
Thus, on the same event, the identity holds for every
$\varphi\in C_c^\infty(B(0,R))$ and every $x\in G$.

For an arbitrary $\psi\in C_c^\infty(B(x,R))$, take
$\varphi(w)=\psi(x+w)$. We have proved the simultaneous local identity
\[
 \int\bigl((y-x)\cdot\nabla\psi(y)+2\psi(y)\bigr)\,\Lambda(dy)=0
 \quad
 \text{for every }x\in G,\quad
 \psi\in C_c^\infty(B(x,R)).                                     \tag{11}
\]
The extension to all test functions was made before this $x$-dependent
choice. There is no uncountable intersection of probability-one events.

### 3. Four independent centres force zero local mass

We now give a deterministic argument. Let $\mu$ be a locally finite
nonnegative Borel measure, let $G$ have full $\mu$ measure, and suppose
that (11) holds with $\mu$ in place of $\Lambda$. Let $U$ be an open
ball of radius $r<R/2$. If $\mu(U)=0$, there is nothing to prove.
Suppose instead that $\mu(U)>0$.

Assume, for contradiction, that $G\cap U$ contains four affinely
independent points $x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3$. Any
$\psi\in C_c^\infty(U)$ is supported in $B(x_i,R)$ for each $i$,
because two points in $U$ are at distance less than $2r<R$.
Subtracting (11) at $x_i$ and $x_0$ gives
\[
 (x_i-x_0)\cdot\int\nabla\psi(y)\,\mu(dy)=0,\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]
The three vectors $x_i-x_0$ form a basis of $\mathbb R^3$, so
\[
          \int\partial_j\psi(y)\,\mu(dy)=0
 \quad(j=1,2,3;\ \psi\in C_c^\infty(U)).                           \tag{12}
\]

For clarity, (12) implies that $\mu=c\,dy$ on $U$, for a constant
$c\geq0$. Here is a direct justification. Let $\eta$ be a nonnegative
smooth mollifier supported in the unit ball with integral one, and put
$h_\varepsilon(z)=\int\eta_\varepsilon(z-y)\,\mu(dy)$. On the
concentric ball $U_\varepsilon$ with radius $r-\varepsilon$, its
gradient is zero by (12). Differentiation under the integral is valid
because all relevant kernels have compact support and $\mu$ is finite
on bounded sets. Thus $h_\varepsilon$ is constant, say
$c_\varepsilon$, on the connected ball $U_\varepsilon$.

Fix a nonnegative $\theta\in C_c^\infty(U)$ with $\int\theta\,dy=1$.
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon$,
$c_\varepsilon=\int\theta h_\varepsilon\,dy$, and this tends to
$c=\int\theta\,d\mu$. Indeed, integrating first in $z$ expresses the
left side as integration against $\mu$ of a mollified $\theta$; these
converge uniformly to $\theta$ and have support in a common compact
subset of $U$. The same argument for any $\psi\in C_c^\infty(U)$
therefore gives
$\int\psi\,d\mu=c\int\psi\,dy$. Equality on smooth compactly supported
tests determines the locally finite measures on $U$, proving the claim.

Use this conclusion in (11) at $x_0$, with a test supported in $U$.
Integration by parts in ordinary Lebesgue measure gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0
 &=c\int_U\bigl((y-x_0)\cdot\nabla\psi(y)+2\psi(y)\bigr)\,dy\\
 &=c(-3+2)\int_U\psi(y)\,dy
 =-c\int_U\psi(y)\,dy.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Choosing a test with nonzero integral gives $c=0$. This contradicts
$\mu(U)>0$.

There are consequently no four affinely independent points in $G\cap U$
when $\mu(U)>0$. Its affine span has dimension at most two: if its affine
span had dimension three, three successive independent difference vectors
would supply precisely such a quartet. The set $G\cap U$, and hence
$\mu|_U$, is therefore carried by an affine plane. The conclusion also
allows a line or a point. No classification theorem for uniform measures
is being invoked.

In particular, in both the zero-mass and positive-mass cases,
\[
                       \mu^{\otimes4}(U^4\cap\{V>0\})=0.           \tag{13}
\]
No measurable selection of the carrying plane is needed for (13).
The set on its left is fixed and Borel.

### 4. Contradiction with S1

Apply the deterministic conclusion on the probability-one event obtained
in Section 2. For each fixed ball of radius $r<R/2=M/8$, (13) holds.
Taking expectation as a nonnegative integral proves (2).

Choose four small open balls $U_1,\ldots,U_4$ inside one such $U$, centred
at the vertices of a nondegenerate tetrahedron. By continuity, their
radii can be chosen so that
\[
                    0<v_-\leq V(\mathbf x)\leq v_+<\infty
 \quad\text{on }U_1\times\cdots\times U_4.
\]
This product has positive twelve-dimensional Lebesgue measure, consists
entirely of distinct noncoplanar points, and has diameter less than $M$.
S1 and (3) would require
\[
 \Gamma_4(U_1\times\cdots\times U_4)
 =a_4\int_{U_1\times\cdots\times U_4}V(\mathbf x)^{-1}\,d\mathbf x
 \in(0,\infty).
                                                                    \tag{14}
\]
Equation (2) instead makes it zero. This is a contradiction on a set
uniformly away from coplanarity; no assumption or manipulation involving
an infinite fourth moment enters.

There is a further consequence of the full no-singular-mass requirement.
By local planarity, for each such $U$,
\[
 \Lambda^{\otimes4}(U^4\cap\{V=0\})=\Lambda(U)^4
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
If $\Gamma_4$ is absolutely continuous, the left side has expectation
zero because $\{V=0\}$ is Lebesgue-null. Hence
$\mathbb E\Lambda(U)^4=0$, which forces $\Lambda(U)=0$ almost surely.
A countable cover by balls of radius less than $M/8$ gives
$\Lambda=0$ almost surely. That contradicts (1) with $a_3>0$.
This argument also excludes singular directing measures; diffuse
surface measures do not evade it.

The proof establishes the theorem and its Cox consequence for every
$M>0$, every $a_3,a_4>0$, and therefore every prescribed positive
intensity. No rescaling or intensity threshold is involved.

## Checks and limitations

- The result is restricted to Cox processes. An arbitrary point process
  need not admit the nonnegative random directing measure used in the
  zero-square identity. The proof cannot be transferred to general
  factorial measures by replacing $\Lambda$ with the point count:
  the resulting square has repeated-point contributions, whereas
  the third factorial measure counts only distinct points.
- The full third identity, including absence of singular third mass
  on collinear configurations, is essential to (8). A same-carrier,
  connected or cumulant identity does not justify that equation.
- The angular singularity is integrable with its actual spherical
  Jacobian; (6) proves the bound needed for all signed rearrangements.
  No subtraction of infinite integrals occurs.
- Local finiteness gives finite realised integrals against each compactly
  supported test. The required averaged square is then proved integrable
  from the third density, without assuming finite fourth moments.
- The countable test-function argument handles the simultaneous
  almost-sure assertion. The deterministic argument uses all translated
  tests only after continuity has supplied that extension.
- The proof of local planarity uses the affine independence of four
  centres and a self-contained constant-measure lemma. It assumes no
  density or smoothness for the realised directing measure.
- The final contradiction may be taken on a product set with volume
  bounded away from zero. It remains valid if singular fourth mass on
  coplanar configurations were permitted, provided the positive
  reciprocal fourth law on noncoplanar configurations were retained.
- No exact-target construction has been produced. General S1 existence
  remains unresolved by this submission. The separate finite-fourth-moment
  comparison was already excluded by the supplied reviewed geometric
  obstruction; no finite-moment assumption is added here.
