## Exact claim and scope

For every independently prescribed \(\lambda>0\) and \(L>0\), this submission constructs one stationary, isotropic, simple, almost surely locally finite process on all of \(\mathbb R^3\), with intensity exactly \(\lambda\). Its full ordered factorial measures through order four have globally finite-almost-everywhere Lebesgue densities and no singular components. It has finite third count moments on bounded sets and infinite fourth count moments on every nonempty open ball.

Its **full pair density** satisfies the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\rho^{(2)}(x_1,x_2)=\frac{3\lambda^2L}{D(x_1,x_2)}
       \quad(0<D(x_1,x_2)<2L).}                                      \tag{1}
\]
Thus choosing \(L=M/2\) meets S1's complete pair requirement for every prescribed \((\lambda,M)\), without a small-intensity restriction or a lower distance cutoff.

The **same-parent fourth density**, explicitly distinguished from the full fourth density, satisfies
\[
\boxed{c_4(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
       =\frac{8\lambda^4L^3}{3\pi\,V(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)}
       \quad(V>0).}                                                  \tag{2}
\]
This is undamped and holds on the entire noncoplanar configuration space. The actual full third and fourth densities are given below, including every immigrant partition. Neither equals S1's requested reciprocal law. Both failures are proved.

The model has Poisson latent parents, one sterile offspring generation, and children conditionally independent and uniform on a sphere whose radius also controls the offspring count. Parents are **not observed points**. This is a two-type spatial branching/Poisson-family construction, also representable as a Cox process driven by a random sum of surface measures. It departs from the usual linear Hawkes model and from the root-observed models of v01–v02. No temporal projection is involved.

A second theorem excludes the simultaneous full pair and triangle laws for the larger class of Poisson latent-parent families with arbitrary radius mixing and arbitrary radius-dependent offspring counts, provided the children, conditional on count and radius, are independent and uniform on their common sphere. This is a model-class obstruction only. It neither excludes dependent offspring directions nor general branching or general stationary processes.

## 1. The actual whole-space law

Let \(\sigma_{z,R}\) denote uniform probability measure on the sphere with center \(z\in\mathbb R^3\) and radius \(R>0\). Define
\[
q_L(R)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac1{16\pi L^3R},&0<R\leq L,\\[4pt]
\displaystyle\frac{L^3}{16\pi R^7},&R>L,
\end{cases}
\qquad
m_{\lambda,L}(R)=
\begin{cases}
16\pi\lambda L^{3/2}R^{3/2},&0<R\leq L,\\
16\pi\lambda R^3,&R>L.
\end{cases}                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Both are positive measurable functions. At each point \((z,R)\) of a Poisson random measure with intensity
\[
dz\,q_L(R)\,dR,                                                       \tag{4}
\]
independently generate \(K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(m_{\lambda,L}(R))\) children with independent positions of law \(\sigma_{z,R}\). Children have no descendants. The observed process \(X\) is the union of all children; the latent parent \(z\) is not included.

The measure in (4) is sigma finite, though its radius marginal has infinite mass near zero. This does not leave the construction formal. Empty families may be deleted. The resulting nonempty-parent process is Poisson with intensity
\[
dz\,q_L(R)(1-e^{-m_{\lambda,L}(R)})\,dR.                              \tag{5}
\]
Its spatial parent rate is finite and strictly positive, because
\[
0<\nu:=\int_0^\infty q_L(R)(1-e^{-m_{\lambda,L}(R)})\,dR
 \leq\int_0^\infty q_L(R)m_{\lambda,L}(R)\,dR=\lambda.                 \tag{6}
\]
One can therefore define the law using (5) alone: mark each parent by its radius and use a Poisson offspring count conditioned to be positive. Every family is finite almost surely. The zero-truncated description gives exactly the same factorial weights:
\[
q_L(R)(1-e^{-m(R)})
 \mathbb E[(K)_n\mid R,K>0]=q_L(R)m(R)^n.                            \tag{7}
\]

Here and below \((K)_n=K(K-1)\cdots(K-n+1)\). Formula (7) permits the simpler untruncated notation without changing the law.

For every bounded Borel \(B\),
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3}\sigma_{z,R}(B)\,dz=|B|,
\]
by translation and Tonelli. Also
\[
q_L(R)m_{\lambda,L}(R)=
\begin{cases}
\lambda L^{-3/2}R^{1/2},&R\leq L,\\
\lambda L^3R^{-4},&R>L.
\end{cases}
\]
The two integrals are \(2\lambda/3\) and \(\lambda/3\). Thus
\[
\mathbb EX(B)=\lambda|B|.                                           \tag{8}
\]
This calculation integrates all parents on the whole space, including arbitrarily distant centers of large spheres. It does not assume bounded family support uniformly in the mark.

Finite expected counts imply almost-sure finiteness of \(X(B)\). A countable exhaustion by bounded balls proves simultaneous local finiteness. The nonempty parents are countable almost surely, and every family is finite. Conditional on their centers, radii and counts, every child has an atomless surface distribution and the children are independent. For any pair of distinct children, the conditional probability of a collision is zero, whether or not they have the same parent. A countable union proves simplicity.

Spatial translations preserve (4) and translate every family; rotations preserve (4) and the uniform sphere laws. Hence \(X\) is stationary and isotropic. Equations (3)–(8) hold for all \(\lambda,L>0\), with no relation between them.

## 2. Same-parent measures by elementary coarea

Write \(c_n\) for the density of the ordered \(n\)-tuple measure contributed by one parent, after integration over all parent locations and marks. Before asserting the existence of a density, that measure is defined by
\[
\mathcal C_n(dx_1\cdots dx_n)
 =\int_{\mathbb R^3}\int_0^\infty
 q_L(R)m(R)^n\prod_{i=1}^n\sigma_{z,R}(dx_i)\,dR\,dz.                \tag{9}
\]
This is a nonnegative measure identity. No finiteness of a fourth local moment is assumed. In particular \(c_1=\lambda\).

The following calculations also apply with \(q_L(R)m(R)^n\,dR\) replaced by a nonnegative radius measure times its conditional \(n\)-th factorial count moment.

For clarity, the notation
\[
\sigma_{z,R}(dx)
 =\frac{\delta(|x-z|^2-R^2)}{2\pi R}\,dx                            \tag{10}
\]
is a shorthand for the coarea identity
\[
\int h(x)\delta(|x-z|^2-R^2)\,dx
 =\frac1{2R}\int_{|x-z|=R}h(x)\,dS(x).
\]
Its total mass is \(2\pi R\), so (10) has mass one. All calculations below can equivalently be performed against a nonnegative test function, using this identity and the indicated linear changes of coordinates. Delta notation is not an additional probabilistic assumption.

### 2.1 Pairs

Fix distinct \(x_1,x_2\), distance \(D\). Subtracting the two squared-radius constraints confines \(z\) to the perpendicular-bisector plane, with Jacobian \(2D\). On that plane the remaining constraint is
\[
|w|^2+D^2/4=R^2,\qquad w\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
The integral of its delta function in \(w\) is \(\pi\) when \(R>D/2\), and zero when \(R<D/2\). The two normalizations in (10) give
\[
c_2(D)=\frac1{8\pi D}
             \int_{D/2}^\infty\frac{q_L(R)m(R)^2}{R^2}\,dR.          \tag{11}
\]
The value at the threshold can be chosen arbitrarily here, since the radius measure in (9) has a density.

### 2.2 Triples

Let \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) be noncollinear, with area \(A>0\) and circumradius \(r>0\). Their possible equidistant centers form the line perpendicular to the triangle plane through its circumcenter. Subtract the first squared-radius equation from each of the other two. If \(u=x_2-x_1\), \(v=x_3-x_1\), the two linear gradients have cross-product magnitude
\[
4|u\times v|=8A.
\]
Writing the remaining center coordinate as \(t\), the first constraint becomes
\[
r^2+t^2=R^2.
\]
For \(R>r\), integration over \(t\) contributes \(1/\sqrt{R^2-r^2}\); this includes both signs of \(t\). Therefore
\[
c_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)
 =\frac1{64\pi^3A}
   \int_r^\infty
      \frac{q_L(R)m(R)^3}{R^3\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}\,dR.                  \tag{12}
\]

### 2.3 Quadruples

For a noncoplanar quadruple, there is a unique circumcenter \(z_*\) and circumradius \(R_*>0\). Let \(E\) have columns \(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1\), so \(|\det E|=6V\). Subtracting the first squared-radius constraint from the other three gives linear constraints on \(z\), with absolute determinant
\[
8|\det E|=48V.
\]
After setting \(z=z_*\), integration of
\(\delta(R_*^2-R^2)\) over positive \(R\) contributes \(1/(2R_*)\).
The four factors in (10) consequently yield
\[
c_4(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
 =\frac{q_L(R_*)m(R_*)^4}{1536\pi^4R_*^5V}.                        \tag{13}
\]
In particular the order-four coefficient depends on the radius weight at the actual circumsphere, not on a freely chosen cutoff.

### 2.4 No residual singular components

For each fixed center and positive radius, two independent surface-uniform children coincide with probability zero. Three are collinear with probability zero: after two distinct positions are fixed, their line meets the sphere in at most two points. Four are coplanar with probability zero: after a noncollinear triple is fixed, its plane meets the sphere in a circle, which has surface area zero.

Integrating these zero conditional probabilities with any of the nonnegative parent/count weights in (9) still gives zero. On the complementary open configuration sets, the preceding coarea calculations yield densities. In the order-four calculation the continuously varying radius is essential: a fixed-radius family by itself would impose a circumsphere-radius constraint.

Thus (11)–(13), with arbitrary values assigned on the indicated null degeneracy sets, are densities of the whole same-parent measures. No singular mass has been discarded. Their finiteness for the concrete law will be proved below.

## 3. All genealogies and immigrant partitions

The only observed vertices are sterile children. Every ordered tuple from one family therefore consists of \(n\) distinct siblings. Its conditional factorial weight is \(m(R)^n\), already present in (9). There is no observed-root contribution and no descendant genealogy missing from this law.

For the full process, an ordered tuple has a unique partition \(\pi\) of its labels according to latent parent. Order the blocks, for example by their least labels. The factorial measure of the corresponding distinct Poisson parents is the product of their intensity measures. Integrating each block's children then proves
\[
\rho^{(n)}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_n}\prod_{B\in\pi}c_{|B|}(x_B),
 \qquad 1\leq n\leq4.                                               \tag{14}
\]
This uses all parent allocations, not just nearby parents. It follows first for finite parent/mark restrictions; the nonnegative terms increase to the whole-space measures, so Tonelli and monotone convergence justify the limit. There is no factorial for ordering the blocks a second time.

In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho^{(2)}_{12}&=c_{2,12}+\lambda^2,\\
\rho^{(3)}_{123}&=c_{3,123}
                  +\lambda(c_{2,12}+c_{2,13}+c_{2,23})+\lambda^3,\\
\rho^{(4)}_{1234}&=c_{4,1234}
       +\lambda\sum_{|I|=3}c_{3,I}
       +\sum_{\{ij\mid kl\}}c_{2,ij}c_{2,kl}
       +\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}c_{2,ij}+\lambda^4.                       \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
\]
There are four triple blocks, three pairings, and six individual pairs. Together with the all-in-one and all-singleton partitions, these are all fifteen fourth-order partitions.

We use \(c_n\) to mean the nonnegative same-parent measure density; in Poisson-family terminology it is the factorial-cumulant coefficient where that terminology is defined. No assumption of finite ordinary fourth cumulants or finite fourth local moments is made. The target concerns the full \(\rho^{(n)}\) in (14)–(15).

## 4. Evaluation of the concrete densities

### 4.1 Exact full pair law at arbitrary intensity

The weight in (11) is
\[
\frac{q_L(R)m(R)^2}{R^2}
=\begin{cases}
16\pi\lambda^2,&R\leq L,\\
16\pi\lambda^2L^3R^{-3},&R>L.
\end{cases}                                                        \tag{16}
\]
Its total integral is \(24\pi\lambda^2L\). If \(0<D<2L\), (11) becomes
\[
c_2(D)
 =\frac{24\pi\lambda^2L-8\pi\lambda^2D}{8\pi D}
 =\frac{3\lambda^2L}{D}-\lambda^2.
\]
Adding the independent-parent contribution in (15) proves (1). If \(D\geq2L\), direct integration gives
\[
c_2(D)=\frac{4\lambda^2L^3}{D^3},\qquad
\rho^{(2)}(D)=\lambda^2+\frac{4\lambda^2L^3}{D^3}.                  \tag{17}
\]
The two expressions agree at \(2L\). All \(c_2\) values are nonnegative; in the first branch \(3L/D-1>1/2\). In particular,
\[
0\leq c_2(D)\leq\frac{3\lambda^2L}{D}\quad(D>0).                    \tag{18}
\]

The large-distance branch in (17) approaches \(\lambda^2\); it is not a global reciprocal pair law. No S0 conclusion is obtained.

The subtraction of \(\lambda^2\) in the first branch is the result of the positive radius-tail integral (11). No signed family intensity or deletion of a full-density term has been used.

### 4.2 Exact same-parent reciprocal volume

On both branches of (3),
\[
q_L(R)m(R)^4=4096\pi^3\lambda^4L^3R^5.                             \tag{19}
\]
Substitution into (13) proves exactly (2). In contrast with v02, there is no Gaussian damping factor to remove by a limit. The absence of damping follows from the actual normalized sphere law and the positive mark weights (3).

Set
\[
b_4=\frac{8\lambda^4L^3}{3\pi}.
\]
The full fourth density is \(b_4/V\) plus every positive lower-block term in (15), not just \(b_4/V\).

### 4.3 Exact third-order expression and a uniform bound

The weight in (12) equals
\[
\frac{q_L(R)m(R)^3}{R^3}
=256\pi^2\lambda^3
\begin{cases}
L^{3/2}R^{1/2},&R\leq L,\\
L^3R^{-1},&R>L.
\end{cases}
\]
Define, for \(r>0\),
\[
G_L(r)=
L^{3/2}\mathbf1_{\{r<L\}}\int_r^L
          \frac{R^{1/2}}{\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}\,dR
 +L^3\int_{\max(r,L)}^\infty
          \frac{dR}{R\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}.                              \tag{20}
\]
Then the complete same-parent triangle density is
\[
c_3=\frac{4\lambda^3}{\pi A}\,G_L(r).                               \tag{21}
\]

These are finite integrals. If \(r\geq L\),
\[
G_L(r)=\frac{\pi L^3}{2r}.                                         \tag{22}
\]
If \(0<r<L\), the second integral in (20) is
\(\arcsin(r/L)/r\leq\pi/(2L)\).
In the first integral put \(s=\sqrt{R^2-r^2}\). Its integrand becomes
\((r^2+s^2)^{-1/4}\,ds\), and hence its integral is at most
\(\int_0^L s^{-1/2}ds=2\sqrt L\). Therefore
\[
0<G_L(r)\leq(2+\pi/2)L^2,\qquad
0<c_3\leq (8/\pi+2)\frac{\lambda^3L^2}{A}.                           \tag{23}
\]

For a triangle with side lengths \(a,b,c\), \(r=abc/(4A)\). In the branch \(r\geq L\), (21)–(22) give the particularly useful exact identity
\[
c_3=\frac{8\lambda^3L^3}{abc}.                                     \tag{24}
\]

## 5. Global density regularity and the local moment class

For distinct pairs, (16)–(18) are finite. For noncollinear triples, (20)–(23) are finite. For noncoplanar quadruples, (2) is finite, each pair is distinct and each face is noncollinear. Every term of (15) is therefore finite there. The exceptional coincidence, collinearity and coplanarity sets are Lebesgue null. Section 2.4 and the partition formula prove that all full measures through order four are globally absolutely continuous, with finite-almost-everywhere densities, even outside the target's diameter domains.

Their fourth density need not be integrable over bounded sets. This does not introduce a singular measure: the identity is an integral of a density, and it assigns zero to every Lebesgue-null set.

For completeness, the bounds in (18) and (23) imply finite third moments on every bounded set without assuming a finite third *family-size* moment. If \(B\) has finite diameter \(H\) and volume \(|B|\), then
\[
\int_{B^2}\frac{dx_1dx_2}{D}\leq2\pi H^2|B|.
\]
For the triangle integral, fix \(x_1,x_2\), write \(D=|x_2-x_1|\), and use cylindrical coordinates about their line. The transverse factor in area is \(A=D s/2\). Inside the ball of radius \(H\) about \(x_1\), the integral of \(1/s\) is at most \(4\pi H^2\), so integration over \(x_3\) gives at most \(8\pi H^2/D\). Consequently
\[
\int_{B^3}\frac{dx_1dx_2dx_3}{A}
\leq16\pi^2H^4|B|.                                                 \tag{25}
\]
Using (15), (18), (23) and (25) gives finite factorial moments of orders one, two and three on \(B\). The identity
\[
X(B)^3=(X(B))_3+3(X(B))_2+X(B)
\]
proves \(\mathbb EX(B)^3<\infty\).

The unbounded radii matter here. An integrated third family-size moment need not be finite, and none was used. Spatial integration of the actual tuple densities is what establishes local finiteness of these moments.

For a nonempty open ball \(B\), choose a noncollinear triple of points in its interior. In a small cylinder around an interior point of their plane,
\[
V=A|t|/3,\qquad dx_4=d\sigma\,dt.
\]
Thus \(\int dt/|t|\) diverges. The argument applies to a positive-measure set of triples in a smaller ball inside \(B\), so Tonelli gives
\[
\int_{B^4}\frac{dx_1dx_2dx_3dx_4}{V}=\infty.
\]
Since \(c_4=b_4/V\) is a nonnegative part of \(\rho^{(4)}\),
\[
\mathbb E(X(B))_4=\mathbb EX(B)^4=\infty.                            \tag{26}
\]
Both expectations in (26) are infinite; the displayed equality is not a general identity between the two random variables.

These conclusions match the necessary finite-third/infinite-fourth local moment distinction in S1. They do not prove its three reciprocal identities.

## 6. The two exact-target failures of the concrete law

### 6.1 Triangle failure

Fix any triple of distinct collinear points with diameter below \(M\). Near this triple all three side lengths stay bounded away from zero. Along noncollinear approaches, \(A\to0\), so \(r=abc/(4A)\to\infty\). Eventually \(r\geq L\), and (24) is uniformly bounded. The pair densities also remain uniformly bounded there by (18).

The full third density in (15) is consequently uniformly bounded near this collinear triple, while \(a_3/A\) diverges for every \(a_3>0\). Almost-everywhere equality cannot avoid this contradiction: there are positive-measure open sets of noncollinear configurations arbitrarily close to the selected triple, still of diameter below \(M\), and the uniform bound applies throughout them. For small enough area, \(a_3/A\) exceeds that bound. Thus the triangle law fails on every \(E_3(M)\).

### 6.2 Fourth-order failure despite the exact singular coefficient

Fix a coplanar quadruple with distinct points, all four faces noncollinear, and diameter below \(M\); a sufficiently small square suffices. In a neighbourhood of this configuration, all pair distances and all face areas are bounded away from zero. Equations (18) and (23) bound all lower-block terms in (15). Therefore the full density obeys
\[
V\rho^{(4)}\longrightarrow b_4                                    \tag{27}
\]
along every noncoplanar approach to that quadruple.

If \(\rho^{(4)}=a_4/V\) almost everywhere on \(E_4(M)\), sequences of noncoplanar configurations outside the exceptional null set and approaching the chosen quadruple force \(a_4=b_4\). But (15) gives, on every noncoplanar configuration,
\[
\rho^{(4)}\geq b_4/V+\lambda^4>b_4/V,                              \tag{28}
\]
a contradiction. Thus this model also fails the full fourth identity on every \(E_4(M)\).

Unlike the v02 obstruction, the leading coefficient in (27) is independent of configuration. Here the remaining strictly positive partition terms are the decisive obstruction. Removing them would change the observed process.

## 7. A larger spherical-family obstruction

The preceding construction is not the only sphere model that fails. The following theorem does not assume Poisson offspring counts, the particular weights (3), any fourth moment, or any fourth-order formula.

**Theorem.** Consider a homogeneous Poisson process of latent spatial parents with independently marked finite families. Conditional on a radius \(R>0\) and finite offspring count \(K\), the \(K\) observed children are independent and uniform on the common sphere centered at their parent. Allow arbitrary radius mixing, arbitrary dependence of \(K\) on \(R\), and zero offspring. Assume the resulting process has finite positive intensity \(\lambda\). Then for no \(M>0\) and positive finite \(a_2,a_3\) can its two full densities satisfy simultaneously
\[
\rho^{(2)}=a_2/D\quad\hbox{on }E_2(M),\qquad
\rho^{(3)}=a_3/A\quad\hbox{on }E_3(M),                              \tag{29}
\]
with the global absolute-continuity requirement of S1.

**Proof.** Write the radius intensity measure as \(\mu(dR)\), and
\(k_j(R)=\mathbb E[(K)_j\mid R]\), permitting extended values.
Finite intensity is \(\lambda=\int k_1(R)\mu(dR)\). If \(\mu\) has infinite total mass, deleting zero families gives a finite nonempty-parent rate at most \(\lambda\), just as in (6).

The pair and triple coarea calculations show that the same-parent pair density depends only on \(D\), while the triple density has the form
\[
c_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)=\frac{F(r)}{A},\quad
F(r)=\frac1{64\pi^3}
 \int_{(r,\infty)}
     \frac{k_3(R)}{R^3\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}\,\mu(dR).                     \tag{30}
\]
These are identities of nonnegative measures, with densities interpreted almost everywhere. An atom at \(R=r\) is harmless for this formulation: for fixed \(R\), the set of triangles whose circumradius equals \(R\) has conditional measure zero and is a Lebesgue-null hypersurface. The usual density can diverge on it. Nonnegative integration preserves absence of a singular component; the coarea formula off the threshold yields (30) for almost every \(r\). Equivalently the same formula follows by first restricting away from thresholds and exhausting the parameter domain.

The independent-parent terms give
\[
\rho^{(2)}=c_2+\lambda^2,\qquad
\rho^{(3)}=c_3+\lambda\sum_{\rm edges}c_2+\lambda^3.                 \tag{31}
\]
Assuming (29), all quantities being subtracted below are finite almost everywhere on the target domains. Substituting the pair identity into the triple identity yields
\[
F(r)=a_3-\lambda a_2 A
        \left(\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c\right)+2\lambda^3A             \tag{32}
\]
for almost every triangle of diameter below \(M\).

We make the use of almost-everywhere identities precise. Translation, orientation, and nondegenerate side lengths give smooth local coordinates for triples, with positive Jacobian away from degeneracies. The side lengths in turn have smooth inverse coordinates
\[
(r,\alpha,\beta),\qquad
0<\alpha,\beta,\quad \alpha+\beta<\pi,
\]
via
\[
a=2r\sin\alpha,\quad b=2r\sin\beta,\quad
c=2r\sin(\alpha+\beta).
\]
The inverse uses the cosine rule and \(r=abc/(4A)\), so this is a smooth change of coordinates on the open triangle domain. The a.e. pair identities transfer to a.e. triples by these same coordinates. For \(0<r<M/2\), every such triangle has diameter below \(M\). Fubini applied to (32) thus gives, for almost every such \(r\), equality for almost every \((\alpha,\beta)\).

Choose one such \(r>0\). Its \(F(r)\) is finite. The right side of (32) is continuous in the two angles, so it must equal that constant for **all** angles in the open triangle domain. We may therefore evaluate it on right triangles with legs \(2r\sin\theta,2r\cos\theta\), hypotenuse \(2r\), \(0<\theta<\pi/2\).

Put \(s=\sin\theta\), \(t=\cos\theta\). On these triangles,
\[
A=2r^2st,\qquad
A(1/a+1/b+1/c)=r(s+t+st).
\]
Writing \(p=s+t\), so \(st=(p^2-1)/2\), equation (32) requires
\[
F(r)=a_3-\lambda a_2r\,p
        +\frac{-\lambda a_2r+4\lambda^3r^2}{2}(p^2-1)              \tag{33}
\]
throughout the nontrivial interval \(1<p<\sqrt2\).
The right side is a quadratic polynomial in \(p\). For it to be constant, its linear coefficient must vanish. That coefficient is \(-\lambda a_2r<0\), a contradiction. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

This theorem is stronger than a failure caused by the chosen Poisson productivity. It says that changing the offspring count law or radius mixing alone cannot complete the pair-plus-triangle target within the stated sphere family.
