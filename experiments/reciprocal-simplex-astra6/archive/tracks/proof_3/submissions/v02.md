## Exact claim and scope

S1 still asks for every independently prescribed $\lambda_0,M>0$ for a
stationary simple locally finite process on all of $\mathbb R^3$ of
intensity $\lambda_0$, with full factorial densities finite almost
everywhere and no singular parts, satisfying
\[
 \rho^{(2)}=\frac{a_2}{D},\qquad
 \rho^{(3)}=\frac{a_3}{A},\qquad
 \rho^{(4)}=\frac{a_4}{V}
 \quad\text{on }E_2(M),E_3(M),E_4(M),
 \tag{1}
\]
for finite positive configuration-independent constants. There are no
lower geometric cutoffs and no finite-fourth-moment requirement.

This submission constructs a different branching family from v01.
Immigrants have a continuous mark that jointly controls their Poisson
offspring mean and the thickness of their spatial offspring distribution.
Children are sterile. Thus this is a two-type marked Poisson branching
model with one generation, not the one-type iid count-independent spatial
model excluded in v01. No equivalence with a temporal conditional-intensity
Hawkes model is asserted.

For every prescribed $\lambda_0>0$ and any independently chosen length
$L>0$, the explicit model below is stationary, isotropic, simple and
locally finite, has intensity exactly $\lambda_0$, has finite third
moments on bounded sets and infinite fourth moments on every open ball,
and has full densities through order four finite almost everywhere with
no singular parts. In particular one may choose $L=M$ for any prescribed
pair $(\lambda_0,M)$.

Writing
\[
 \bar x=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^4x_i,\qquad
 S_4=\sum_{i=1}^4(x_i-\bar x)(x_i-\bar x)^T,
 \]
the **four-children same-family contribution**, rather than the full
fourth density, is exactly
\[
 C_4(\mathbf x)
 =\frac{c}{24\pi^3L^6}
       \frac{\exp[-\operatorname{tr}(S_4)/L^2]}{V(\mathbf x)}
 \quad (V>0),
 \qquad c=\frac{63\lambda_0}{32\pi}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Every additional term is given below. The full fourth density retains this
reciprocal-volume singularity near any coplanar quadruple whose four
triangular faces are nondegenerate. Its limiting coefficient depends on
the shape through the exponential in (2). We prove that the resulting
full fourth law cannot equal $a_4/V$ throughout $E_4(M)$.

We also prove a new obstruction for the larger radial-productivity family
that preserves (2): either its full pair density is infinite at every
positive separation, or
\[
 D\rho^{(2)}(D)\longrightarrow0\quad\text{as }D\downarrow0.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus no member of that larger marked family can satisfy S1's pair law.
The result permits a broad count–geometry dependence and is outside the
hypotheses of the accepted v01 theorem. It is still a family-specific
obstruction, not general non-existence for S1.

## Argument

### 1. Marked reproduction and an actual process on the whole space

For $v\in\mathbb R^3$, put
\[
 a(v)=1+|v|^2,\qquad Q_v=I+vv^T,\qquad \det Q_v=a(v).
 \tag{4}
\]
For a fixed $L>0$ define the probability density
\[
 f_{v,L}(y)=\frac{a(v)^{1/2}}{\pi^{3/2}L^3}
       \exp\left[-\frac{|y|^2+(v\cdot y)^2}{L^2}\right].
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed $Q_v$ is positive definite, with eigenvalues $1,1,a(v)$.
Diagonalising it gives
$\int\exp[-y^TQ_vy/L^2]\,dy=\pi^{3/2}L^3a(v)^{-1/2}$.
The normalisation in (5) is therefore exact.

Take a Poisson process of marked immigrants $(z,v)$ on
$\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^3$ with intensity
\[
 dz\,\mu(dv),\qquad \mu(dv)=c\,a(v)^{-11/2}\,dv.
 \tag{6}
\]
At each immigrant observe the root $z$, sample
$K\mid v\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(a(v))$, and put its $K$ children at
$z+Y_1,\ldots,z+Y_K$, where conditional on $v,K$ the displacements are
independent with density (5). Children have no offspring. Families are
independent conditional on the marked immigrant process.

This is a measurable probability construction on countable parents and
finite child arrays. The total immigrant rate is finite because
$\int a^{-11/2}\,dv<\infty$. Every family is finite almost surely, but
its unconditional fourth size moment will be infinite.

For $p>3/2$, polar integration and the substitution $t=r^2$ give
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}(1+|v|^2)^{-p}\,dv
 =2\pi\int_0^\infty t^{1/2}(1+t)^{-p}\,dt
 =\pi^{3/2}\frac{\Gamma(p-3/2)}{\Gamma(p)}.
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \int a^{-11/2}\,dv=\frac{64\pi}{315},\qquad
 \int a^{-9/2}\,dv=\frac{32\pi}{105}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Translating each child's normalised density through all parent locations
and applying Tonelli gives
\[
 \mathbb EX(B)
 =|B|\int(1+a(v))\,\mu(dv)
 =\frac{32\pi c}{63}|B|.
 \tag{9}
\]
Set $c=63\lambda_0/(32\pi)$. This choice works for every independently
prescribed $L>0$. Roots have intensity $2\lambda_0/5$ and children
$3\lambda_0/5$; neither component was omitted from (9).

Finite expectation of every bounded count, followed by a countable
exhaustion by balls, proves almost-sure local finiteness despite the
unbounded Gaussian displacements. Conditional on marks and counts,
children have diffuse independent displacements. Root–root coincidences
have probability zero by Poisson simplicity; all other pairs have a
diffuse displacement after conditioning on the remaining variables.
Countably many pairs prove simplicity. Translating all roots translates
the configuration, proving stationarity. Rotating both $z$ and $v$
preserves (6), and $f_{Rv,L}(Ry)=f_{v,L}(y)$, proving isotropy.

### 2. Finite third moments and the exact full factorial measures

Let $T=1+K$ be the size of a family. Conditional on $v$,
\[
 \mathbb E[T^3\mid v]=1+7a(v)+6a(v)^2+a(v)^3.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently $\int\mathbb E[T^3\mid v]\,\mu(dv)<\infty$:
the largest-power term has integrable weight $a^{-5/2}$.
For a realised family $C$ and $N_z=C(B-z)$,
\[
 N_z^j\leq T^{j-1}N_z,\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}N_z^j\,dz\leq |B|T^j,\qquad j=1,2,3.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus the parent-integrated family raw moments $\Psi_j(B)$ are finite.
Expanding the raw cube of the Poisson family sum yields
\[
 \mathbb EX(B)^3
 =\Psi_3(B)+3\Psi_2(B)\Psi_1(B)+\Psi_1(B)^3<\infty.
 \tag{12}
\]
One first restricts immigrants to a bounded spatial set and then increases
it to the whole space; monotone convergence proves (12). This is the
marked version of v01's accepted cluster-moment calculation and requires
no bound on the cluster radius.

For an $n$-tuple write
\[
 \bar x_n=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j,\qquad
 S_n=\sum_{j=1}^n(x_j-\bar x_n)(x_j-\bar x_n)^T.
 \]
For a selected root label $i$, write
\[
 T_{n,i}=\sum_{j\ne i}(x_j-x_i)(x_j-x_i)^T,\qquad
 E_{n,i}=\operatorname{tr}T_{n,i}.
 \tag{13}
\]
A same-family ordered distinct tuple contains either only children or
exactly one root. There are no other genealogies in this model.

The children-only contribution is
\[
 C_n(\mathbf x)
 =\int \mu(dv)\,a(v)^n
       \int_{\mathbb R^3}\prod_{j=1}^n f_{v,L}(x_j-z)\,dz.
 \tag{14}
\]
Completing the square in $z$ gives the exact expression
\[
 C_n(\mathbf x)=
 \frac{c\,e^{-\operatorname{tr}(S_n)/L^2}}
      {n^{3/2}\pi^{3(n-1)/2}L^{3(n-1)}}
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}
       a(v)^{\,3(n-4)/2}e^{-v^TS_nv/L^2}\,dv.
 \tag{15}
\]
The coefficient $a(v)^n$ in (14) is the Poisson factorial moment
$\mathbb E[(K)_n\mid v]$. The square-completion identity is
\[
 \sum_j(x_j-z)^TQ_v(x_j-z)
 =n(z-\bar x_n)^TQ_v(z-\bar x_n)+\operatorname{tr}(Q_vS_n).
 \]
Its integrated Gaussian factor is
$\pi^{3/2}L^3 n^{-3/2}a(v)^{-1/2}$, which gives every factor in (15).

The root-containing contribution is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_n(\mathbf x)
 &=\sum_{i=1}^n\int\mu(dv)\,a(v)^{n-1}
          \prod_{j\ne i}f_{v,L}(x_j-x_i)\\
 &=\frac{c}{\pi^{3(n-1)/2}L^{3(n-1)}}
    \sum_{i=1}^n e^{-E_{n,i}/L^2}
       \int_{\mathbb R^3}
       a(v)^{(3n-14)/2}e^{-v^TT_{n,i}v/L^2}\,dv.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
There are precisely $n$ possible root labels. Equations (14)–(16)
are nonnegative integral identities, including when an integral is
infinite. Put $c_n=C_n+R_n$. For $n=1$, (15) and (16) give the child
and root intensities in (9), respectively, so $c_1=\lambda_0$.

The full measures are
\[
 \rho^{(n)}(\mathbf x)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P([n])}\prod_{I\in\pi}c_{|I|}(x_I).
 \tag{17}
\]
Every observed tuple has a unique immigrant partition. Conditional on
distinct immigrants, family marks and offspring are independent, and
the ordered Poisson immigrant factorial measure is the product of (6).
Ordering blocks by their least label avoids an extra block-order
factorial. Equations (14) and (16) count all possible selections within
each block. Tonelli now proves (17) for every nonnegative measurable
test, without a finite-fourth-moment assumption.

In particular the full fourth density is
\[
 \rho^{(4)}
 =C_4+R_4
  +\lambda_0\sum_{|I|=3}c_3(I)
  +\sum_{ij\,|\,kl}c_2(ij)c_2(kl)
  +\lambda_0^2\sum_{i<j}c_2(ij)
  +\lambda_0^4.
 \tag{18}
\]
The sums have four triples, three pairings and six pairs. This accounts
for all fifteen immigrant partitions and both within-family
genealogies. Each component is a Lebesgue density, so the full measure
has no singular part. Extended nonnegative products use $0\cdot\infty=0$,
equivalently increasing bounded truncations of their factors.

### 3. Exact reciprocal-volume contribution and finite-a.e. full densities

For four noncoplanar points, let $E$ be the matrix with columns
$x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1$. Then
\[
 S_4=E\left(I-\tfrac14\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\right)E^T,\qquad
 \det S_4=\frac{(\det E)^2}{4}=9V^2.
 \tag{19}
\]
The middle $3$-by-$3$ matrix has eigenvalues $1,1,1/4$.
Thus $\sqrt{\det S_4}=3V$. At $n=4$ the power of $a(v)$ in (15)
is zero, and
\[
 \int e^{-v^TS_4v/L^2}\,dv
 =\frac{\pi^{3/2}L^3}{\sqrt{\det S_4}}.
 \]
Substitution proves (2), including its coefficient.

For noncoplanar quadruples, every $T_{4,i}$ is positive definite.
Its integrand in (16) is
$a(v)^{-1}e^{-v^TT_{4,i}v/L^2}$ and is bounded by an integrable
positive-definite Gaussian. Hence $R_4<\infty$ there.
At order two the weights in (15) and (16) are $a^{-3}$ and $a^{-4}$,
both integrable on $\mathbb R^3$. Thus $c_2$ is bounded globally.
At order three, the root weight $a^{-5/2}$ is integrable. For a
noncollinear triple the matrix $S_3$ has two positive eigenvalues.
After an orthogonal change of coordinates, the children integrand
can be bounded by
\[
 (1+|w|^2+t^2)^{-3/2}e^{-\delta |w|^2},
 \qquad w\in\mathbb R^2,\ t\in\mathbb R,\ \delta>0.
 \]
The integral in $t$ is $2/(1+|w|^2)$, and the remaining integral is
finite. Therefore $c_3$ is finite on noncollinear triples.
Every face of a noncoplanar quadruple is noncollinear, so all terms
in (18) are finite there. The degenerate sets are Lebesgue-null.
Equations (15)–(18) consequently give full densities finite almost
everywhere globally. Their values on null sets can be set to zero
without adding or removing singular mass.

For any bounded ball $B$, $\operatorname{tr}S_4$ is bounded on $B^4$,
so the exponential in (2) has a strictly positive lower bound there.
The reciprocal-volume integral over noncoplanar quadruples in $B^4$
is infinite: at a fixed noncollinear base, the fourth vertex's
transverse height gives $V=A|t|/3$ and an integral of $1/|t|$.
Since $\rho^{(4)}\geq C_4$ as full measures,
\[
 \mathbb E(X(B))_4=\mathbb EX(B)^4=\infty.
 \tag{20}
\]
This proves the claimed moment class and reconciles it with finite-a.e.
densities. The fourth divergence occurs near ordinary coplanarity,
not only near point coincidences.

### 4. The full coplanar singularity and its failure to give the exact fourth law

Let $\mathbf q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4)$ be a coplanar quadruple with every
triangular face noncollinear. We claim that, in some neighbourhood of
$\mathbf q$, the remainder
\[
 B_4(\mathbf x)=\rho^{(4)}(\mathbf x)-C_4(\mathbf x)
 \tag{21}
\]
defined by the nonnegative terms in (18) is uniformly bounded on
noncoplanar configurations. The subtraction notation in (21) is only
used where both densities are finite; (18) defines the remainder
without any subtraction of infinite quantities.

For each selected root $i$, the matrix $T_{4,i}$ at $\mathbf q$ has
rank two and two strictly positive eigenvalues. By continuity, its
two largest eigenvalues stay bounded below by one fixed positive
number in a sufficiently small neighbourhood. The third is
nonnegative. Orthogonal diagonalisation and radiality of $a(v)$
bound its integral in (16), uniformly, by a constant of the form
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^2}\int_{\mathbb R}
       \frac{e^{-\delta|w|^2}}{1+|w|^2+t^2}\,dt\,dw
 =\pi\int_{\mathbb R^2}
       \frac{e^{-\delta|w|^2}}{\sqrt{1+|w|^2}}\,dw<\infty.
 \tag{22}
\]
The diagonalising coordinates may vary with the configuration:
the bound is unchanged because the weight is radial.

Each face remains noncollinear with its two positive scatter
eigenvalues bounded away from zero. The bound for $C_3$ in Section 3
is therefore also uniform on all four faces. The integrable weights
already give global bounds for $R_3$ and $c_2$. All terms in (18)
other than $C_4$ are consequently uniformly bounded, proving the claim.

Put $A_*=c/(24\pi^3L^6)$. For any noncoplanar approach to $\mathbf q$,
\[
 V(\mathbf x)\rho^{(4)}(\mathbf x)
 \longrightarrow
 A_*\exp[-\operatorname{tr}S_4(\mathbf q)/L^2].
 \tag{23}
\]
This limit is a proved property of the **full** fourth density.
It follows from (2), the continuity of $S_4,V$, and the uniform bound
on (21), rather than by discarding the other positive terms.

The coefficient in (23) prevents the exact fourth identity in S1.
Consider the coplanar squares
\[
 \mathbf q_s=((0,0,0),(s,0,0),(0,s,0),(s,s,0)),
 \qquad 0<s<M/\sqrt2.
 \]
They have four nondegenerate faces, diameter $\sqrt2s<M$, and
$\operatorname{tr}S_4(\mathbf q_s)=2s^2$.
If $\rho^{(4)}=a_4/V$ almost everywhere on $E_4(M)$, take a sequence
of noncoplanar configurations approaching $\mathbf q_s$ outside its
null exceptional set. Such sequences exist because every sufficiently
small neighbourhood contains a positive-measure open subset of $E_4(M)$.
Equation (23) would require
\[
 a_4=A_*e^{-2s^2/L^2}
 \quad\text{for every such }s.
 \tag{24}
\]
Two distinct values of $s$ contradict (24). Thus this explicit model
fails even the fourth identity by itself, for every finite $L>0$ and
every $M>0$. Its correct moment class and reciprocal-volume singularity
are partial progress, not exact fourth-law attainment.

The concrete model also has a bounded pair density. Using
$\int a^{-3}dv=\pi^2/4$ and $\int a^{-4}dv=\pi^2/8$ in (15)–(16),
\[
 \rho^{(2)}(x_1,x_2)
 \leq \lambda_0^2+
       \frac{c\sqrt\pi}{L^3}
       \left(\frac14+\frac1{8\sqrt2}\right).
 \tag{25}
\]
This already excludes its exact reciprocal-distance law.
The next result shows why merely changing the radial productivity
profile, while preserving the same exact four-child calculation, does
not repair the pair law.

### 5. A new obstruction for arbitrary radial productivity in this marked family

Let $b:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty)$ be any measurable, finite-valued
function. Keep (4)–(5), but give an immigrant with mark $v$ a Poisson
number of sterile children with mean $b(|v|)$, and replace (6) by
\[
 \mu_b(dv)=c\,b(|v|)^{-4}a(v)^{-3/2}\,dv.
 \tag{26}
\]
Assume
\[
 0<J_\lambda=
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}
       \{b(|v|)^{-4}+b(|v|)^{-3}\}a(v)^{-3/2}\,dv
 <\infty.
 \tag{27}
\]
Then (26) has finite immigrant rate, and exactly the preceding
construction proves stationarity, simplicity, local finiteness and
intensity $\lambda=cJ_\lambda$. It is an actual marked branching
process. No higher moment is assumed. If desired, any positive
intensity is attained by taking $c=\lambda_0/J_\lambda$.

The four-child coefficient still cancels:
$\mu_b(dv)b(|v|)^4\det(Q_v)^{3/2}=c\,dv$.
Thus the expression for $C_4$ in (2) remains exactly true, with $c$
normalised by (27) instead of the special relation in (9).
The concrete model is the choice $b(r)=1+r^2$.

We prove that every model (26)–(27) fails the exact pair law, regardless
of its fourth-order remainder. Write $D=|x_2-x_1|$, $d=D/L$,
and for a unit vector $e$ define
\[
 I_\beta(d;\gamma)=
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}
       \frac{b(|v|)^{-\beta}}{1+|v|^2}
       e^{-\gamma d^2(v\cdot e)^2}\,dv,
 \qquad \gamma>0,\quad \beta=2,3.
 \tag{28}
\]
Radiality makes this independent of $e$. The exact same-family pair
terms, obtained by selecting two children or a root and a child, are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_{2,b}(D)
 &=\frac{c}{2^{3/2}\pi^{3/2}L^3}
       e^{-d^2/2}I_2(d;1/2),\\
 R_{2,b}(D)
 &=\frac{2c}{\pi^{3/2}L^3}
       e^{-d^2}I_3(d;1).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{29}
\]
The full pair density is $\rho_b^{(2)}(D)=\lambda^2+C_{2,b}(D)+R_{2,b}(D)$.
All terms are nonnegative extended densities.

Put
\[
 F(u)=\int_{-1}^1e^{-u^2t^2}\,dt.
 \]
For $0<u\leq1$, $2e^{-1}\leq F(u)\leq2$.
For $u\geq1$, integrating first over $|t|\leq1/u$ and then over
the entire real line gives
$2e^{-1}/u\leq F(u)\leq\sqrt\pi/u$.
Hence $F(u)$ is bounded above and below by positive constants times
$\min(1,u^{-1})$. Polar coordinates in (28) give
\[
 I_\beta(d;\gamma)
 =2\pi\int_0^\infty
      \frac{r^2}{1+r^2}b(r)^{-\beta}
      F(\sqrt\gamma\,rd)\,dr.
 \tag{30}
\]
Define the nonnegative integral
\[
 J_2=\int_0^\infty \frac{r}{1+r^2}b(r)^{-2}\,dr.
 \tag{31}
\]
The part near zero is always finite because $b\geq1$.

If $J_2=\infty$, the lower bound for $F$ on the tail
$r\geq1/(\sqrt\gamma d)$ proves
$I_2(d;\gamma)=\infty$ for every $d>0$.
Therefore the full pair density is infinite at every positive
separation. It cannot equal the finite-a.e. density required by S1.

If $J_2<\infty$, the upper bound for $F$ gives a finite constant
$C_\gamma$, independent of $d,r$, such that
\[
 d I_\beta(d;\gamma)
 \leq C_\gamma\int_0^\infty
       \frac{r}{1+r^2}b(r)^{-\beta}
       \min(rd,1)\,dr.
 \tag{32}
\]
For $\beta=2$ the weight without the minimum is integrable by (31).
For $\beta=3$ it is dominated by the same weight since $b\geq1$.
At every fixed $r$, $\min(rd,1)\to0$ as $d\downarrow0$.
Dominated convergence therefore proves
\[
 d I_2(d;1/2)\to0,\qquad d I_3(d;1)\to0.
 \tag{33}
\]
It also establishes finiteness of both integrals for every $d>0$.
Substitution into (29), with $D=Ld$, proves (3).

If the full pair law were $a_2/D$ almost everywhere for $0<D<M$,
then $D\rho_b^{(2)}(D)=a_2$ for almost every such radius.
Radial almost-everywhere equality follows from the full configuration
identity by translation coordinates and polar integration. It is
incompatible with the limit zero in (3) and $a_2>0$.
This proves the obstruction for every radial measurable productivity
profile satisfying (27), without a hidden third- or fourth-moment
assumption.

The proof is different from v01's fixed-kernel argument. Each mark
here changes the shape of the offspring density and its reproduction
mean together; the four-child density is finite on noncoplanar
configurations and has the required type of geometric divergence.
The new obstruction comes from the radial integral of the actual
pair density in (30), not from factoring an infinite offspring
moment out of one fixed spatial kernel.
