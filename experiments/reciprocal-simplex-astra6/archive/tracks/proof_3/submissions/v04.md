## Exact claim and scope

**Theorem.** For every \(\lambda,M>0\) satisfying
\[
\lambda M^3\leq\frac1{64\pi},                                      \tag{1}
\]
there is a stationary, isotropic, simple, almost surely locally finite point process \(X\) on all of \(\mathbb R^3\), of intensity exactly \(\lambda\), whose full ordered factorial measures of orders two through four are globally absolutely continuous with finite-almost-everywhere densities. It has finite third count moments on every bounded set and infinite fourth count moments on every nonempty open ball. On the entire domains in question, without lower geometric cutoffs,
\[
\boxed{\rho^{(2)}=\frac{2\lambda^2M}{D}\quad(0<D<M),\qquad
\rho^{(3)}=\frac{4\lambda^3M^2}{A}
       \quad(\operatorname{diam}<M,\ A>0).}                         \tag{2}
\]
The full fourth density is explicitly given in Section 6. Its same-parent term is
\[
c_4=\frac{\lambda^4M^3}{48\pi V}\quad(V>0),                         \tag{3}
\]
but the full fourth density is not a constant divided by \(V\) on any \(E_4(M)\).

Both identities in (2) are **full densities**. They include all immigrant partitions. This improves the v03 result by introducing genuine dependence between two offspring positions in a three-point family; merely changing the old sphere radius/count law could not do so.

Condition (1) is a limitation of the positive construction proved here. It does not follow as a necessary condition for S1, and it is not an adopted amendment. S1 prescribes \(\lambda_0\) and \(M\) independently without such a restriction and also requires the full fourth identity. Thus this theorem is partial progress only.

The law is a superposition of four explicitly defined independent Poisson-family types: the heavy-tailed spherical families from v03, dependent three-point families, two-point families, and singleton families. Some types include a point at their parent location, while the spherical type has latent unobserved parents. Every observed vertex is specified below. This is a spatial branching/cluster construction outside the standard homogeneous linear Hawkes class; it is not claimed to be a Cox process. No time projection is used.

## 1. The spherical component used as a frozen dependency

Set
\[
\eta=\lambda/2,\qquad L=M/2.
\]
We use the actual law of v03, Sections 1–5, at intensity \(\eta\) and length \(L\). Its proof and the independent review are identified above. For completeness, the law and every property needed below are recorded here.

For \(R>0\), put
\[
q_L(R)=
\begin{cases}(16\pi L^3R)^{-1},&R\leq L,\\
L^3/(16\pi R^7),&R>L,\end{cases}
\quad
m_{\eta,L}(R)=
\begin{cases}16\pi\eta L^{3/2}R^{3/2},&R\leq L,\\
16\pi\eta R^3,&R>L.\end{cases}                                     \tag{4}
\]
Spatial parents have marked intensity \(dz\,q_L(R)dR\). A parent has a Poisson number of children with mean \(m_{\eta,L}(R)\), independently uniform on its sphere of radius \(R\); the parent is unobserved, and the children are sterile.

An ordinary finite-rate version deletes empty families. Its marked parent intensity is \(dz\,q_L(R)(1-e^{-m(R)})dR\), and its count is a positive-conditioned Poisson variable. The parent rate is at most
\(\int q_Lm\,dR=\eta\), and is positive. Its factorial family weights are still \(q_Lm^n\,dR\). Translation integration gives expected observed mass \(\eta|B|\) on every bounded \(B\), because the two radius ranges contribute \(2\eta/3\) and \(\eta/3\). This proves whole-space local finiteness; atomless conditional child laws and countability prove simplicity.

Write \(s_n\) for the spherical component's same-parent densities. Its \(s_1=\eta\), and the needed formulas are
\[
s_2(D)=
\begin{cases}
3\eta^2L/D-\eta^2,&0<D<2L,\\
4\eta^2L^3/D^3,&D\geq2L,
\end{cases}                                                       \tag{5}
\]
\[
s_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)=\frac{4\eta^3}{\pi A}\,G_L(r),                    \tag{6}
\]
where \(r\) is the triangle circumradius and
\[
G_L(r)=L^{3/2}\mathbf1_{\{r<L\}}
 \int_r^L\frac{R^{1/2}\,dR}{\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}
 +L^3\int_{\max(r,L)}^\infty\frac{dR}{R\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}.
                                                                         \tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
0\leq s_2(D)\leq\frac{3\eta^2L}{D},\qquad
0<s_3\leq(2+8/\pi)\frac{\eta^3L^2}{A}
       \leq\frac{3}{16}\frac{\lambda^3M^2}{A},                    \tag{8}
\]
where \(2+8/\pi<6\). At order four,
\[
s_4=\frac{8\eta^4L^3}{3\pi V}
    =\frac{\lambda^4M^3}{48\pi V}.                                \tag{9}
\]

These identities are genuine measures, not just formulas outside a presumed singular component. The v03 coarea proof starts with the probability surface measure
\(\delta(|x-z|^2-R^2)dx/(2\pi R)\). The pair and triple center Jacobians are \(2D\) and \(8A\). For a quadruple, the center Jacobian is \(48V\) and the positive radius derivative is \(2R_*\). Thus its fourth density is
\[
\frac{q_L(R_*)m(R_*)^4}{1536\pi^4R_*^5V}.
\]
Since \(q_Lm^4=4096\pi^3\eta^4L^3R^5\), this is (9). Conditional degeneracy probabilities vanish, and the coarea maps cover the complements. These facts exclude residual singular mass. The displayed densities are finite on distinct pairs, noncollinear triples and noncoplanar quadruples respectively.

For the bound in (8), the substitution \(t=\sqrt{R^2-r^2}\) bounds the first integral in (7) by \(2\sqrt L\); the second is at most \(\pi/(2L)\) when \(r<L\), and equals \(\pi/(2r)\) when \(r\geq L\). Hence \(G_L(r)\leq(2+\pi/2)L^2\). This supplies the exact bound used in the new positivity proof below.

The inherited facts about this component are used only with the continuous radius density (4). Section 8 separately addresses the requested clarification for arbitrary radius measures.

## 2. New dependent-family kernels

Let
\[
a_2=2\lambda^2M,\qquad a_3=4\lambda^3M^2.
\]
All kernels below are symmetric and translation invariant; they will be defined as zero on coincidence or collinearity sets when a formula is undefined. Such choices do not change the resulting measures.

Define the intended extra same-parent pair kernel
\[
w_2(x,y)=\mathbf1_{\{0<D<M\}}
 \left(\frac{a_2}{D}-\lambda^2-s_2(D)\right)
 =\mathbf1_{\{0<D<M\}}
 \lambda^2\left(\frac{13M}{8D}-\frac34\right).                      \tag{10}
\]
Define a three-point family kernel on every noncollinear triple by
\[
F_3(x,y,z)=\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{diam}<M\}}
 \left[
 \frac{a_3}{A}
 -\lambda a_2\left(\frac1{D_{xy}}+\frac1{D_{xz}}+\frac1{D_{yz}}\right)
 +2\lambda^3-s_3(x,y,z)
 \right].                                                        \tag{11}
\]
Its pair marginal is
\[
J_2(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}F_3(x,y,z)\,dz,                         \tag{12}
\]
and the additional two-point family kernel is
\[
F_2(x,y)=w_2(x,y)-J_2(x,y).                                       \tag{13}
\]
The point of (13) is that three-point families also contribute pairs. The construction cannot use \(w_2\) as the two-point family kernel and then add triples without accounting for those extra pairs.

### 2.1 Positivity and integrability of the triple kernel

For a triangle of diameter less than \(M\),
\[
\frac{A}{D_{ij}}\leq M/2,\qquad A\leq M^2/2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\lambda a_2 A\sum_{\rm edges}D_{ij}^{-1}
\leq3\lambda^3M^2.
\]
Combining this with (8) in (11) gives
\[
F_3\geq\frac{13}{16}\frac{\lambda^3M^2}{A}+2\lambda^3>0
\quad\hbox{on }\{\operatorname{diam}<M,\ A>0\}.                    \tag{14}
\]
For an upper bound, omit the two nonpositive terms in (11) and use \(2A\leq M^2\). This yields
\[
0\leq F_3\leq
\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{diam}<M,\ A>0\}}
\frac{5\lambda^3M^2}{A}.                                         \tag{15}
\]

Fix distinct \(x,y\), distance \(D<M\). In cylindrical coordinates for \(z\) about their line, \(A=Ds/2\), with transverse radius \(s\). The support in (11) lies in the ball of radius \(M\) about \(x\). Bounding that ball by the cylinder of axial length \(2M\) and radius \(M\) gives
\[
\int_{\operatorname{diam}(x,y,z)<M}\frac{dz}{A}
\leq\frac{8\pi M^2}{D}.
\]
Hence
\[
0\leq J_2(x,y)\leq
\mathbf1_{\{0<D<M\}}\frac{40\pi\lambda^3M^4}{D}.                   \tag{16}
\]
In particular \(J_2\) is measurable, finite off the diagonal, and zero when \(D\geq M\). Symmetry of \(F_3\) makes it symmetric in \(x,y\). Its integral at fixed \(x\) is finite, since \(1/D\) is locally integrable in three dimensions.

### 2.2 Positivity of the two-point family kernel

For \(0<D<M\), (10) implies
\[
w_2(x,y)\geq\frac78\frac{\lambda^2M}{D}.
\]
Under (1), (16) is bounded by \((5/8)\lambda^2M/D\). Thus
\[
\boxed{\frac14\frac{\lambda^2M}{D}
       \leq F_2(x,y)\leq w_2(x,y)
       \leq\frac{13}{8}\frac{\lambda^2M}{D}\quad(0<D<M),}            \tag{17}
\]
and \(F_2=0\) outside that domain. The construction uses only nonnegative kernels.

Let
\[
Z_2=\int_{\mathbb R^3}F_2(0,u)\,du,\qquad
Z_3=\int_{(\mathbb R^3)^2}F_3(0,u,v)\,du\,dv.                       \tag{18}
\]
The preceding bounds give \(0<Z_2,Z_3<\infty\). Tonelli and (12)–(13) show
\[
Z_2+Z_3=R_2,\qquad
R_2:=\int_{\mathbb R^3}w_2(0,u)\,du
     =\frac{9\pi}{4}\lambda^2M^3.                                 \tag{19}
\]
For the last evaluation, the two terms in (10) integrate to
\((13\pi/4)\lambda^2M^3\) and \(\pi\lambda^2M^3\).

Define the singleton intensity
\[
\zeta=\lambda-\eta-Z_2-Z_3/2
     =\lambda/2-R_2+Z_3/2.                                       \tag{20}
\]
By (1) and (19),
\[
\zeta\geq\lambda/2-R_2
\geq\frac{119}{256}\lambda>0.                                    \tag{21}
\]
Thus intensity matching also uses a positive rate.

## 3. Definition of all four family types

Take the following four independent parent processes and independent marks across different parents.

1. **Spherical families:** use (4), equivalently its finite-rate nonempty-parent realization, with intensity of observed points \(\eta=\lambda/2\).
2. **Three-point families:** take spatial Poisson parents of rate \(Z_3/6\). Given a parent \(z\), draw \((U,V)\) with probability density \(F_3(0,U,V)/Z_3\) on \(\mathbb R^6\), and emit the three observed points \(z,z+U,z+V\).
3. **Two-point families:** take spatial Poisson parents of rate \(Z_2/2\). Given a parent \(z\), draw \(U\) with density \(F_2(0,U)/Z_2\), and emit the two observed points \(z,z+U\).
4. **Singleton families:** use a Poisson process of rate \(\zeta\), emitting its parent location as one observed point.

There are no further descendants. In type 2, the two offsets generally are dependent; their joint density is specified in full by (11). This dependence is the substantive departure from the previous iid geometries.

Equations (18) and (21) prove positivity and normalization of every law. Translation and rotation invariance of the kernels prove stationarity and isotropy. Each of the last three types has bounded family size and finite parent rate. The spherical type has the whole-space finite-intensity realization from Section 1.

The expected number in a bounded set \(B\), integrating every parent over the whole space, is
\[
\bigl(\eta+3Z_3/6+2Z_2/2+\zeta\bigr)|B|
=\lambda|B|.                                                      \tag{22}
\]
Therefore the superposed process is locally finite almost surely. Within two-/three-point families, the offset laws have Lebesgue densities, so collisions have probability zero. The spherical families are simple by Section 1. For different families, parent coordinates have diffuse spatial laws; conditioning on offsets, or using the Poisson factorial measure for distinct parents, gives zero probability of equal observed positions. Countability of all parents and their finite families proves simplicity. These arguments define one process on all of \(\mathbb R^3\).

## 4. Complete same-parent contributions

The anchored three-point law has same-parent third density exactly \(F_3\). To see the coefficient, an ordered selection of all three observed points has six possible orders. Each such order has density \(F_3/Z_3\) after integration of its parent coordinate: symmetry and translation invariance transform its ordered anchor/offset representation into that same expression. The linear transformation between anchored coordinate representations has absolute Jacobian one. Multiplying by parent rate \(Z_3/6\) and summing the six orders gives \(F_3\), with no omitted root label.

Its same-parent pair density is \(J_2\). Indeed integrating the third ordered coordinate of a size-three family factorial measure leaves exactly one possible remaining point for every ordered pair. Thus the marginal of its third measure equals its pair measure, giving (12). Its one-point intensity is \(Z_3/2\), as also follows from its count of three. It contributes no order-four same-parent tuples.

Likewise, the two possible orders of a two-point family, multiplied by rate \(Z_2/2\), give same-parent pair density \(F_2\); its intensity is \(Z_2\), with no same-parent tuples of order three or four. Singletons contribute only their intensity. The spherical type has only sibling selections because its parents are unobserved.

Combining all types, write \(c_n\) for the total same-parent density. We have the exact measure identities
\[
c_1=\lambda,\qquad
c_2=s_2+F_2+J_2=s_2+w_2,\qquad
c_3=s_3+F_3,\qquad
c_4=s_4.                                                         \tag{23}
\]
Each term is a nonnegative measure. In particular the subtraction in the definition of \(F_2\) has been justified by (17) before using it as a family law.

## 5. Full measures and simultaneous attainment through order three

Merge the four independent parent processes into one marked Poisson parent process. An ordered observed tuple is uniquely partitioned by its parent. Applying the factorial measure of distinct Poisson parents and integrating the family marks gives
\[
\rho^{(n)}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_n}\prod_{B\in\pi}c_{|B|}(x_B),
\qquad 1\leq n\leq4.                                             \tag{24}
\]
Finite restrictions of the parent regions and the nonempty spherical marks prove this first without convergence issues; nonnegative monotone convergence gives the whole-space identity. Only the same-parent genealogies in Section 4 are possible within this defined law, and all between-parent partitions occur in (24).

In particular,
\[
\rho^{(2)}_{12}=c_{2,12}+\lambda^2,\qquad
\rho^{(3)}_{123}=c_{3,123}+\lambda\sum_{\rm edges}c_{2,ij}+\lambda^3,
                                                                         \tag{25}
\]
and
\[
\rho^{(4)}=
c_4+\lambda\sum_{|I|=3}c_{3,I}
\sum_{\{ij\mid kl\}}c_{2,ij}c_{2,kl}
\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}c_{2,ij}+\lambda^4.                             \tag{26}
\]
The fourth line contains the \(1+4+3+6+1=15\) partitions. In particular no root-containing or lower-block term is hidden in the notation.

On \(0<D<M\), (10) and (23) give
\[
c_2=a_2/D-\lambda^2.
\]
On every noncollinear triangle of diameter below \(M\), (11) and (23) give
\[
c_3=a_3/A-\lambda a_2\sum_{\rm edges}D_{ij}^{-1}+2\lambda^3.         \tag{27}
\]
Substitution into (25) proves both full identities in (2). These identities hold throughout the nondegenerate open domains for the displayed versions of the densities, hence also as measures and almost-everywhere statements.

## 6. Exact full fourth density and its failure of S1

Globally, the full fourth density is (26), with all \(c_n\) defined by (5)–(13) and (23). On \(E_4(M)\) each face and each pair belongs to the corresponding domain in (27). Expanding every term in (26) gives
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\rho^{(4)}
={}&\frac{b_4}{V}
+\lambda a_3\sum_{|I|=3}\frac1{A_I}
+a_2^2\sum_{\{ij\mid kl\}}\frac1{D_{ij}D_{kl}}\\
&-2\lambda^2a_2\sum_{i<j}\frac1{D_{ij}}
+6\lambda^4,\qquad
b_4=\frac{\lambda^4M^3}{48\pi}.
\end{aligned}}                                                    \tag{28}
\]
This is an exact full formula, not an approximation. The apparent negative pair term in this expanded expression is compatible with positivity: (26) is its nonnegative partition representation. In obtaining the coefficient, every edge belongs to two triangular faces, and the three pairings use all six edges once. The constant terms are \(8+3-6+1=6\), in units of \(\lambda^4\).

The lower-block remainder in (26) is at least \(\lambda^4\) everywhere on noncoplanar configurations. It is also uniformly bounded in a neighbourhood of any coplanar quadruple with all pair distances and all face areas positive. This boundedness is local to such a neighbourhood, not global. It follows from (8), (15), (17), and the positivity of those distances and areas.

Choose such a quadruple, for example a small square, with diameter below \(M\). Along every noncoplanar approach to it,
\[
V\rho^{(4)}\longrightarrow b_4.                                  \tag{29}
\]
An a.e. identity \(\rho^{(4)}=a_4/V\) on \(E_4(M)\) would force \(a_4=b_4\): every neighbourhood contains a positive-measure open set of eligible noncoplanar configurations, so a sequence can avoid the exceptional null set. But (26) gives
\[
\rho^{(4)}\geq b_4/V+\lambda^4>b_4/V,
\]
contradicting that identity. Thus the exact full fourth requirement fails, even though the two lower full laws have now been repaired.

The same argument explains the limitation of this repair method. Adding independent Poisson families of size at most three leaves the old same-parent fourth density unchanged. If the added lower-order densities remain locally bounded near the selected ordinary coplanar configuration, their full partition remainder cannot repair an already fixed coefficient \(b_4/V\). This observation is confined to that construction mechanism; it is not an obstruction for arbitrary point processes or arbitrary changes to the four-point family law.

## 7. Global regularity and moments

The spherical component has the global density and null-set properties recorded in Section 1. The added anchored families have joint offset densities, and every coordinate marginal used in Section 4 is a Lebesgue density after parent integration. They add no singular factorial measure. The full partition representation preserves absolute continuity, because its block measures act on disjoint coordinate blocks.

For every distinct pair, (16)–(17) make the new pair terms finite. For a noncollinear triple, (15) makes \(F_3\) finite. A noncoplanar quadruple has distinct pairs and noncollinear faces, so every term of (26) is finite there. Coincidence, collinearity and coplanarity sets are null and carry zero measure. Consequently all full factorial measures through order four have globally finite-almost-everywhere densities, including outside the target diameter domains.

There are global bounds of the form
\[
c_2(x,y)\leq C_2/D,\qquad
c_3(x,y,z)\leq C_3/A,                                             \tag{30}
\]
for finite constants depending on \(\lambda,M\): combine (8), (10), (15), and (23). For a bounded set \(B\) of diameter \(H\),
\[
\int_{B^2}D^{-1}\leq2\pi H^2|B|,\qquad
\int_{B^3}A^{-1}\leq16\pi^2H^4|B|.                                \tag{31}
\]
The second inequality follows from the same cylindrical calculation used for (16), followed by the pair integral. Equations (24)–(25) and (30)–(31) give finite first, second and third factorial count moments. Thus
\[
\mathbb EX(B)^3<\infty
\]
by \(N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N\).

For every nonempty open ball \(B\), \(\int_{B^4}V^{-1}=\infty\). Indeed, for noncollinear base triples in a smaller interior ball, a small cylinder for the fourth point lies within \(B\); its transverse coordinate gives \(V=A|t|/3\) and the divergent integral \(\int dt/|t|\). Tonelli applies to the nonnegative integrand. From (3) and (26),
\[
\mathbb E(X(B))_4=\infty,\qquad \mathbb EX(B)^4=\infty.              \tag{32}
\]
No fourth-moment assumption has entered the construction. The fourth measure is a density integral with infinite local mass; this is different from singular mass or a density infinite on a positive-measure set.

## 8. Clarification requested by the v03 review

The arbitrary-radius statement preceding v03's coarea calculations is to be read as follows.

For **pairs and triples**, integration over parent centers at each fixed radius already gives absolutely continuous measures, with no antipodal or great-circle threshold mass. Therefore arbitrary nonnegative radius mixing preserves those measure identities, allowing extended densities.

For **quadruples**, the displayed evaluation formula at the circumsphere radius requires an absolutely continuous *fourth-factorial-weighted radius measure*. The concrete density \(q_L(R)m(R)^4dR\) has this property. An atom at \(R_0\) carrying positive fourth factorial weight can instead produce singular fourth same-parent mass on the set of noncoplanar quadruples whose circumsphere radius is \(R_0\). This set is Lebesgue null: in nondegenerate shape-and-scale coordinates its radius is a positive constant times the scale, so a specified radius fixes one scalar coordinate. Conditional sphere samples give positive mass there when four children can be selected.

It would be too strong to demand that the entire radius law have no atoms: an atom whose count law never has four children contributes no fourth factorial weight. The correct distinction is the weighted measure needed at the relevant order. The v03 pair/triangle obstruction used only the first statement. The concrete spherical component used in this submission has a radius density, so its fourth formula is unaffected.

The coordinator's transcription note concerns a missing plus sign in the review's display of the right-triangle polynomial. The frozen v03 equation (33) and its polynomial argument are unchanged and were correct. No revised mathematical premise is inferred from that note.
