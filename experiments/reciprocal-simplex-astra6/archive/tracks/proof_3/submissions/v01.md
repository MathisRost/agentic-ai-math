## Exact claim and scope

S1 asks for every independently prescribed pair $\lambda_0,M>0$ for one
stationary, simple, locally finite process on $\mathbb R^3$ of intensity
$\lambda_0$, with full factorial densities finite almost everywhere and no
singular components, such that
\[
 \rho^{(2)}=\frac{a_2}{D},\qquad
 \rho^{(3)}=\frac{a_3}{A},\qquad
 \rho^{(4)}=\frac{a_4}{V}
 \quad\text{on }E_2(M),E_3(M),E_4(M),
 \tag{1}
\]
respectively. Here $a_2,a_3,a_4$ are finite positive constants chosen with the
process, $E_n(M)$ contains every nondegenerate $n$-tuple of diameter less than
$M$, and $D,A,V$ have the normalisations in S1. All tuples are ordered and
pairwise distinct. No fourth-moment assumption or lower geometric cutoff is
part of this target.

We prove the following narrower impossibility theorem.

**Theorem.** Consider homogeneous Poisson immigrants on $\mathbb R^3$ with
rate $\nu>0$. Each immigrant is an observed point and starts an independent
branching family. Every individual has an independent copy of a finite
nonnegative integer offspring count $K$. Conditional on $K=k$, its $k$
child displacements are independent with one fixed Lebesgue probability
density $f$ on $\mathbb R^3$, independent of $K$ and of all other reproduction
events. All individuals in all generations are observed.

No process in this family with finite positive intensity can satisfy S1,
for any $\lambda_0,M>0$. Indeed, it cannot satisfy even the fourth-order
identity in (1), with its finite-almost-everywhere density requirement,
for any $a_4>0$.

The theorem allows arbitrary displacement tails and singularities of the
integrable density $f$, arbitrary offspring-count tails, and parameters
depending on both $\lambda_0$ and $M$. It does not impose a fourth moment.
Its proof separates the two exhaustive possibilities:

1. If $\mathbb E K^4<\infty$, finite intensity forces subcriticality, and all
   bounded-window fourth count moments are finite.
2. If $\mathbb E K^4=\infty$, the four direct children of a single immigrant
   already produce a fourth factorial density infinite on a set of positive
   Lebesgue measure inside every ball to the fourth power.

The first possibility contradicts the reciprocal-volume integral. The second
contradicts finiteness almost everywhere of the prescribed density itself.

The Poisson-offspring special case, with $K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(m)$ and
offspring intensity kernel $h=mf$, is the spatial linear Hawkes branching
model considered here. It has no temporal conditional-intensity assumption.
For non-Poisson $K$, the theorem concerns a broader Galton–Watson spatial
branching model, not an ordinary linear Hawkes model.

This is a failure of a specified construction family. Count-dependent
displacement laws, shared random spatial kernels coupled to reproduction,
and more general marked or interacting models are outside this theorem.
No assertion of their feasibility or impossibility is made. In particular,
the theorem supplies no authority to restrict S1.

## Argument

### 1. The local moments required by S1

The fourth-order difficulty is present at every intensity and every positive
diameter threshold. Let $B$ be a nonempty open ball of diameter less than $M$.
For a noncollinear triple $(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in B^3$, let its area be $A>0$,
its affine plane be $P$, and a unit normal be $n$. Choose
\[
 0<\delta<\operatorname{dist}(x_1,B^c)/\sqrt2.
\]
The cylinder
\[
 x_4=z+tn,\qquad z\in P,\quad |z-x_1|<\delta,\quad |t|<\delta
\]
lies in $B$. For fixed base triple these orthogonal coordinates have
Jacobian one, and $V=A|t|/3$. Consequently,
\[
 \int_{B\cap\{V>0\}}\frac{dx_4}{V}
 \geq \frac{3\pi\delta^2}{A}
       \int_{0<|t|<\delta}\frac{dt}{|t|}
 =\infty.
 \tag{2}
\]
Almost every base triple is noncollinear. Tonelli therefore gives
\[
 \int_{B^4\cap\{V>0\}}\frac{d\mathbf x}{V}=\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
Every tuple in this integral has diameter less than $M$. Under S1, its full
fourth factorial measure is thus infinite, and
\[
 \mathbb E(X(B))_4=\mathbb E X(B)^4=\infty.
 \tag{4}
\]
Here $(N)_r$ denotes a falling factorial, zero for integers $N<r$.
Every open ball contains a smaller one of diameter below $M$, so the
infinite-fourth-moment conclusion holds on every nonempty open ball.
Changing density values on the coplanar null set cannot change (3).

For contrast, S1 implies finite third count moments on every bounded set.
For a bounded set $Q$ of diameter $L<M$, nonnegative integration gives
\[
 \int_{Q^2}D^{-1}\,d\mathbf x\leq 2\pi L^2|Q|,
 \qquad
 \int_{Q^3}A^{-1}\,d\mathbf x\leq16\pi^2L^4|Q|.
 \tag{5}
\]
For the second bound, put $u=x_2-x_1$, $v=x_3-x_1$, and use cylindrical
coordinates around the line spanned by $u$. At fixed $u\ne0$, enlarging
$|v|\leq L$ to $|z|\leq L$, transverse radius $s\leq L$, gives
\[
 \int_{|v|\leq L}|u\times v|^{-1}\,dv
 \leq 4\pi L^2/|u|.
 \]
Integrating $u$ over its radius-$L$ ball and multiplying by the area factor
two yields (5). The pair estimate is the radial integral of $|u|^{-1}$.
These calculations include coincidence and collinearity limits.

The full first, second and third measures and
$N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N$ now imply $\mathbb E X(Q)^3<\infty$.
Cover any bounded set by finitely many such cubes. For nonnegative numbers,
$(\sum_{i=1}^q b_i)^3\leq q^2\sum_{i=1}^q b_i^3$, so finite third moments
extend to that bounded set. None of this upgrades the fourth moment:
the divergence (4) remains an actual requirement on any S1 construction.

### 2. A whole-space spatial branching process

Let $p_k=\mathbb P(K=k)$ and $m=\mathbb E K$. Use the rooted tree whose
vertices are finite words in positive integers: the root is the empty word,
and an individual with $K=k$ has children numbered $1,\ldots,k$.
Independent reproduction variables and displacements on this countable
label space define the family measurably. Conditional on the realised tree,
each edge has an independent displacement with density $f$.
The root is at zero and each other vertex is at the sum of the
displacements along its ancestral path. Denote this random counting
configuration by $C$, and its total number of individuals by $T$.

Place independent copies of $C$ at the points $z$ of a homogeneous Poisson
process $\Pi$ of rate $\nu$. Define the potentially extended count
\[
 X(B)=\sum_{z\in\Pi} C_z(B-z).
 \tag{6}
\]
For a realised family, Tonelli gives the elementary translation identity
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}C(B-z)\,dz=|B|T.
 \tag{7}
\]
The expected size of generation $j$ is $m^j$, with the usual extended
interpretation if $m=\infty$. For finite $m$ this follows by conditioning
on the previous generation and induction; for infinite $m$ already the
first generation has infinite mean. Thus
\[
 \mathbb E X(B)=\nu |B|\sum_{j\geq0}m^j.
 \tag{8}
\]
The Poisson first-moment identity used here follows from the expectation
of its count on a set, first for simple functions of the parent position
and mark, and then by monotone approximation. All integrands are
nonnegative. Equation (8) does not assume finite cluster size or moments.

A finite positive prescribed intensity forces $m<1$ in this representation.
For $m\geq1$ the right side of (8) is infinite whenever $|B|>0$.
For $m<1$,
\[
 \mathbb E T=\frac1{1-m}<\infty,\qquad T<\infty\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad \lambda=\frac{\nu}{1-m}.
 \tag{9}
\]
In particular, $\nu=\lambda_0(1-m)$ realises any prescribed $\lambda_0>0$
for this model, before reciprocal identities are imposed. There is no
small-intensity restriction in (9).

Finite expectation in (8) implies that $X(B)$ is finite almost surely for
each bounded $B$. A countable exhaustion by balls proves simultaneous
local finiteness. This argument also handles unbounded offspring
displacements; no finite enlargement of $B$ is being assumed.
Translating all parents translates all observations, so (6) is stationary.

The process is simple. For two distinct vertices in one finite tree,
their positional difference includes at least one edge displacement with
coefficient $1$ or $-1$ which is independent of the other involved edge
displacements. Conditioning on the latter makes equality of the two
positions have probability zero. For vertices from different families,
two roots are distinct almost surely; every other case has a diffuse
independent edge displacement available after conditioning on the roots
and all other edge variables. There are only countably many parents and
vertex pairs. A countable union proves absence of every coincidence.

### 3. All genealogies and all immigrant partitions

We give the full factorial measures explicitly; the theorem does not rely
on treating a reproduction kernel as a product density.

Let $\mathcal T$ be the countable collection of finite rooted ordered trees.
For $\tau\in\mathcal T$, write $d_v$ for the number of children of vertex
$v$, and
\[
 P_\tau=\prod_{v\in\tau}p_{d_v}.
 \tag{10}
\]
This is the probability of that ordered family tree. Since $m<1$ implies
$T<\infty$ almost surely, $\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T}P_\tau=1$.
No ordering factor is omitted: child numbers are part of the probability
construction and the Poisson choice, when used, already contains its
factor $1/k!$ in $p_k$.

Choose an ordered $r$-tuple of distinct vertices
$\mathbf v=(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$ of $\tau$. Give all vertices spatial
coordinates $y_v$, fix $y_{v_i}=x_i$, and define
\[
 G_{\tau,\mathbf v}(\mathbf x)
 =
 \int_{(\mathbb R^3)^{\,\tau\setminus\{v_1,\ldots,v_r\}}}
       \prod_{\substack{w\in\tau\\w\ne\mathrm{root}}}
       f(y_w-y_{\operatorname{par}(w)})
       \prod_{u\notin\{v_1,\ldots,v_r\}}dy_u.
 \tag{11}
\]
The root coordinate is integrated in (11) exactly when the root is
unobserved. The integral is extended and nonnegative. Its measurability
follows by Tonelli. The change from the root position and edge
displacements to all vertex positions is triangular with absolute
Jacobian one. Thus (11) is exactly the marginal density of the chosen
vertices when the root position is integrated against Lebesgue measure.

Define the same-immigrant factorial densities
\[
 c_r(\mathbf x)
 =\nu\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T}P_\tau
       \sum_{\mathbf v\in\tau_{\ne}^r}
       G_{\tau,\mathbf v}(\mathbf x),\qquad r\geq1.
 \tag{12}
\]
For $r=1$, integration of each translated vertex gives
$c_1=\nu\mathbb ET=\lambda$. For general $r$, (12) integrates over
every realised finite family tree and every ordered selection of
observed vertices. It therefore includes ancestor–descendant, sibling,
cousin and all other genealogical relations, including unobserved
ancestors and descendants.

For every $n\geq1$, the full factorial expectation measure has the extended
Lebesgue density
\[
 \rho^{(n)}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 =
 \sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P([n])}
       \prod_{I\in\pi}c_{|I|}(x_I).
 \tag{13}
\]
Products of extended nonnegative factors use $0\cdot\infty=0$; equivalently,
truncate each factor at a finite level and increase that level. This is
also the convention supplied by the nonnegative product integrals.
To prove this as a measure identity, assign each ordered observed tuple to
the immigrants of its vertices. This gives one set partition $\pi$.
Order its blocks by their least element. The distinct immigrants assigned
to those blocks have Poisson factorial measure
$\nu^{|\pi|}\,dz_1\cdots dz_{|\pi|}$. This identity follows from independent
Poisson counts on disjoint sets and
$\mathbb E(N)_q=\theta^q$ for $N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\theta)$;
finite partitions and monotone extension give it for all nonnegative
tests. Independent family marks give one factor (12) per block. Summing
the partitions proves (13), with no additional block-order factorial.
Every rearrangement is a nonnegative sum or integral, so it remains
valid when a higher factorial measure is not boundedly finite.

In particular, with sums over the six pairs, four triples, and three
partitions into two pairs, respectively,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \rho^{(2)}(12)
 &=\lambda^2+c_2(12),\\
 \rho^{(3)}(123)
 &=\lambda^3+\lambda\sum_{i<j}c_2(ij)+c_3(123),\\
 \rho^{(4)}(1234)
 &=\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}c_2(ij)
   +\sum_{ij\,|\,kl}c_2(ij)c_2(kl)
   +\lambda\sum_{|I|=3}c_3(I)+c_4(1234).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
These are full densities. Equations (11)–(13) also show that there is no
singular component: every summand integrates to zero on a Lebesgue-null
set. They do not assert finiteness almost everywhere in the infinite
offspring-moment case; that distinction will be decisive.

### 4. Finite fourth cluster size forces finite fourth local moments

We first establish a fact for arbitrary Poisson cluster processes with
finite random clusters. It does not require the internal branching
structure, a displacement density, bounded support, or independent
positions within a cluster.

Suppose $\mathbb ET^4<\infty$ and use (6). For a bounded Borel set $B$,
put $N_z=C(B-z)$. Since $0\leq N_z\leq T$,
\[
 N_z^r\leq T^{r-1}N_z,\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}N_z^r\,dz\leq |B|T^r
 \quad(1\leq r\leq4).
 \tag{15}
\]
Define
\[
 \Psi_r(B)=\nu\int_{\mathbb R^3}\mathbb E N_z^r\,dz.
 \]
It follows that $0\leq\Psi_r(B)\leq\nu|B|\mathbb ET^r<\infty$.
Restrict the parents temporarily to a bounded region. Expanding the
fourth power of their sum and grouping its four slots by immigrant
gives
\[
 \mathbb E X(B)^4
 =\Psi_4+4\Psi_3\Psi_1+3\Psi_2^2
       +6\Psi_2\Psi_1^2+\Psi_1^4.
 \tag{16}
\]
For the restricted parent process the same formula uses the restricted
integrals. Increasing the parent regions to all of $\mathbb R^3$
and applying monotone convergence proves (16) as written.
Its five finite terms prove finite fourth local moments.
This bound accounts for every allocation to parents, including different
parents, and remains valid with unbounded cluster radii.

We next show that, for the subcritical branching model,
\[
 \mathbb E K^4<\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb ET^4<\infty.
 \tag{17}
\]
Let $T_\ell$ be the total population through generation $\ell$, including
the root. Then $T_0=1$ and
\[
 T_\ell\ \stackrel{d}{=}\
 1+\sum_{i=1}^K T_{\ell-1,i},
 \tag{18}
\]
where conditional on $K$ the copies on the right are independent and
independent of $K$. Put $M_r(\ell)=\mathbb E T_\ell^r$.
For $S=\sum_{i=1}^K T_{\ell-1,i}$, grouping the $a$ factors in $S^a$
by child index gives
\[
 \mathbb E S^a
 =
 \sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P([a])}
       \mathbb E(K)_{|\pi|}
       \prod_{I\in\pi}M_{|I|}(\ell-1).
 \tag{19}
\]
At each finite depth these moments are finite under
$\mathbb EK^4<\infty$, by induction using (18)–(19).
The only occurrence of $M_r(\ell-1)$ in
$\mathbb E(1+S)^r$ is the single-block term at $a=r$, with coefficient
$m$. All other terms involve moments of strictly lower positive order
and finite offspring factorial moments of order at most $r$.

The bound $M_1(\ell)\leq(1-m)^{-1}$ follows from (9). Inductively,
uniform boundedness of the lower moments supplies a finite constant
$C_r$, independent of $\ell$, such that
\[
 M_r(\ell)\leq mM_r(\ell-1)+C_r,\qquad r=2,3,4.
 \tag{20}
\]
Because $m<1$, iteration bounds the right side by
$M_r(0)+C_r/(1-m)$. Finally $T_\ell\uparrow T$,
so monotone convergence proves (17).

Equations (15)–(20) show that the finite-fourth-offspring branch of the
theorem cannot satisfy (4). They also show that the same-immigrant and
full factorial measures through order four are boundedly finite in this
case, and hence their densities in (12)–(13) are finite almost everywhere.
Thus their failure is the required reciprocal-volume identity, not
an absence of an actual spatial process.

### 5. Infinite fourth offspring moment gives infinite density on positive measure

Now suppose $m<1$ and $\mathbb EK^4=\infty$. For nonnegative integers,
$(k)_4$ is comparable to $k^4$ for large $k$, so
$\mathbb E(K)_4=\infty$. Consider only quadruples of distinct direct
children of one immigrant. This is a submeasure of the full fourth
factorial measure, regardless of later generations or other parents.

Conditional on the immigrant position $z$ and on $K=k$, its expected
ordered four-child measure has density
$(k)_4\prod_{i=1}^4 f(x_i-z)$. Therefore the submeasure has density
\[
 s_4(\mathbf x)
 =
 \nu\sum_{k\geq0}p_k(k)_4\,H_4(\mathbf x),
 \qquad
 H_4(\mathbf x)=
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}\prod_{i=1}^4 f(x_i-z)\,dz.
 \tag{21}
\]
Writing the expression as a nonnegative series avoids any convention
about $0\cdot\infty$: it is zero wherever $H_4=0$ and infinite wherever
$H_4>0$.

For any bounded Borel set $B$ with $|B|>0$, put
\[
 q_B(z)=\int_B f(x-z)\,dx.
 \]
We have $0\leq q_B(z)\leq1$ and
$\int q_B(z)\,dz=|B|$. Hence $q_B$ is positive on a set of positive
measure, and
\[
 0<
 \int_{B^4}H_4(\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x
 =\int_{\mathbb R^3}q_B(z)^4\,dz
 \leq |B|<\infty.
 \tag{22}
\]
Consequently $H_4>0$ on a positive-measure subset of $B^4$.
It is also finite almost everywhere there. The density $s_4$ is
infinite on that positive-measure subset. This argument needs only
$f\geq0$ and $\int f=1$; no boundedness, continuity or positive interior
of its support is assumed.

Choose $B$ to be an open ball of diameter less than $M$. Its coplanar
quadruples are a Lebesgue-null set, so $s_4=\infty$ on a positive-measure
set $S\subseteq B^4\cap\{V>0\}$. S1 prescribes there the finite function
$a_4/V$. The contradiction can be stated directly in measures:
the sets
\[
 S_j=S\cap\{a_4/V\leq j\},\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,
 \]
exhaust $S$, so some $S_j$ has positive measure. The four-sibling
submeasure of $S_j$ is infinite by (21), whereas the full S1 measure
of $S_j$ is at most $j|B|^4<\infty$. A submeasure cannot exceed the
full measure. This contradiction uses only the prescribed fourth law
inside its diameter domain.

All other genealogies and immigrant partitions contribute nonnegative
measures by (12)–(14); none can cancel the four-sibling term.
Together with Sections 2 and 4, this completes the theorem.

### 6. Poisson reproduction and finite-time spatial projection

For Poisson reproduction with mean $m$, every moment of $K$ is finite.
If $m<1$, Sections 2–4 give a genuine stationary spatial process of
intensity $\nu/(1-m)$, with finite fourth local count moments.
It therefore cannot satisfy S1. Taking $m$ arbitrarily close to one
does not change that conclusion for any one process with $m<1$.
At $m\geq1$, the same positive-rate immigrant construction has infinite
mean measure by (8). A sequence of increasingly large finite moments
does not supply a limiting S1 process without a separate existence
and full-measure proof.

The finite-moment conclusion also covers the following temporal route.
Construct a Poisson-offspring branching process on
$\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ with homogeneous space-time immigrants,
an absolutely continuous offspring displacement law supported at positive
time lags, and total offspring mean $m<1$. Retain points whose times lie
in a finite interval $I$ of positive length and project them onto
$\mathbb R^3$. Its count in $B$ is the space-time count in $B\times I$.

The whole-space construction, translation calculation (7), and bound (15)
hold in every Euclidean dimension with Lebesgue measure in that dimension.
Thus this projected count has finite fourth moment for every bounded $B$.
Its spatial intensity is $\nu_{\mathrm{st}}|I|/(1-m)$ and can be prescribed
by choosing $\nu_{\mathrm{st}}$. Spatial stationarity follows from
space-time translation invariance in the spatial coordinates.
Absolute continuity of the spatial edge marginals and countability
exclude spatial coincidences by the same pair argument used in Section 2.
Integrating the full space-time factorial measures over $I^n$ gives the
full projected factorial measures, with all partitions retained.
Despite these valid process properties, (4) excludes this route as well.

Projection of every time at positive homogeneous space-time intensity
has infinite expected count on each positive-volume spatial set, by
Tonelli. It does not meet the prescribed finite-intensity requirement.
These observations concern the stated subcritical Poisson branching
representation; no equivalence theorem for more general temporal or
marked Hawkes models is asserted.

## Checks and limitations

- The no-go theorem ranges over all fixed probability densities $f$ and
  all finite-a.s. offspring counts $K$; it does not assume $\mathbb EK^4$
  finite. Finite positive intensity itself forces $m<1$ in its specified
  immigrant representation.
- The finite-moment argument integrates over all parent locations. It
  requires no bounded support or finite influence radius.
- The infinite-moment argument counts only direct immigrant siblings.
  Their contribution is a genuine submeasure, so omitted positive
  genealogies can only strengthen the contradiction.
- Equation (22) establishes positive-measure positivity of the sibling
  kernel even for irregular $f$. A statement about one isolated
  configuration would not have been enough.
- Equations (11)–(14) include every finite family tree, every ordered
  vertex selection and every immigrant partition. The fourth-order
  coefficients count $1+4+3+6+1=15$ set partitions.
- The proof distinguishes an infinite integral of a finite-a.e. function
  $1/V$ from a function infinite on a positive-measure set. S1 permits
  the former and excludes the latter.
- The theorem does not cover reproduction laws whose spatial
  distribution depends on the offspring count, shared productivity and
  geometry marks, or arbitrary infinite-type models. A putative
  construction in any such extension must specify its actual law,
  prove its existence and full densities, and identify its departure
  from the present unmarked Hawkes family.
- A mixture of subcritical laws with parameters approaching criticality
  is not covered merely because each conditional law has finite
  moments. Uniform bounds or integrable mixtures would need proof.
  No existence claim about such a mixture is made.
- No numerical experiment, simulation, earthquake-data comparison or
  proof assistant was used. The mathematical result is a self-checked
  informal proof, pending fresh independent review.

## Reasoning summary and decision record

The investigation first makes the spatial process and its full measures
explicit. The essential compatibility check is local: S1 requires an
infinite fourth count moment, yet a density finite almost everywhere.
A subcritical branching model with finite fourth offspring moment
