## Exact claim and scope

For every $\lambda>0$ and $a_4\in(0,\infty)$, there is no stationary, simple, locally finite point process on $\mathbb R^3$ with intensity $\lambda$ whose full fourth factorial expectation measure has density $a_4/V$ on all noncoplanar configurations. Neither a pair law nor a triangle law is assumed. No finite fourth count moment, mixing, ergodicity, isotropy or process representation is assumed.

We prove a stronger tail theorem: it is already impossible for the restriction of the fourth factorial measure to **all noncoplanar configurations of diameter greater than any fixed finite threshold** to be bounded above by the measure with density $a_4/V$. The measure outside that domain is unrestricted and may have infinite bounded-set mass. Thus modifying only small-diameter configurations cannot repair the global fourth-order requirement.

This closes the mathematical scope question raised by R1-07. It does not prove that a finite upper diameter is a necessary or sufficient repair, or that a particular shape restriction is necessary. The original S0 nonexistence verdict and all four results in v01 are retained below with self-contained proofs.

## Argument

### 1. A fourth-order tail obstruction without moment assumptions

Write
\[
 V(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
 =\frac16\left|\det[x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1]\right|,
 \qquad
 d(\mathbf x)=\max_{i,j}\|x_i-x_j\|.
\]
For any process in this section, $\alpha^{(4)}$ is the extended nonnegative measure defined as the expectation of the sum over ordered, pairwise-distinct quadruples.

**Theorem 1.** Let $X$ be a stationary, simple, locally finite point process on $\mathbb R^3$ with intensity $0<\lambda<\infty$. There do not exist finite constants $a>0$ and $R_0\geq0$ such that, for every Borel set
$E\subseteq\{\mathbf x:V(\mathbf x)>0,\ d(\mathbf x)>R_0\}$,
\[
 \alpha^{(4)}(E)\leq
 a\int_E\frac{d\mathbf x}{V(\mathbf x)}.
 \tag{1}
\]

This is domination of the restricted full measure, not merely a bound on one of its components. It leaves the measure on coplanar configurations and on configurations of diameter at most $R_0$ unrestricted. The exact fourth-order identity in S0 implies (1), for example with $R_0=0$ and $a=a_4$.

**Proof.** The argument counts occupied cubes, so all random variables to which we apply a fourth-power inequality are bounded.

Let $U=[0,1)^3$, $Q_z=z+U$ for $z\in\mathbb Z^3$, and
\[
 I_z=\mathbf1\{X(Q_z)>0\},\qquad
 p=\mathbb E I_0.
\]
Stationarity gives $\mathbb E I_z=p$ for every $z$. Since
$\mathbb E X(Q_0)=\lambda>0$, we have $p>0$.

For each positive integer $m$ and $u\in U$, let
$z_m(u)=(\lfloor mu_1\rfloor,\lfloor mu_2\rfloor,\lfloor mu_3\rfloor)$,
and define
\[
 f_m(u)=I_{z_m(u)},\qquad
 Y_m=\int_U f_m(u)\,du
 =\frac1{m^3}\sum_{z\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^3}I_z.
\]
The cells in this integral have volume $m^{-3}$, so the displayed equality is exact. We have $0\leq Y_m\leq1$ and $\mathbb E Y_m=p$. Jensen's inequality and bounded Fubini integration yield
\[
 p^4\leq\mathbb E Y_m^4
 =\int_{U^4}\mathbb E\left[\prod_{i=1}^4
 I_{z_m(u_i)}\right]d\mathbf u.
 \tag{2}
\]

For $\varepsilon>0$ put
\[
 B_\varepsilon=\{\mathbf u\in U^4:V(\mathbf u)\leq\varepsilon\},
 \qquad b_\varepsilon=|B_\varepsilon|,
 \qquad G_\varepsilon=U^4\setminus B_\varepsilon.
\]
The coplanar set $\{V=0\}$ has twelve-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. Indeed, the first two vertices coincide only on a null set; for each distinct pair the collinear third vertices lie on a line of three-dimensional measure zero; and for each noncollinear base triangle the coplanar fourth vertices lie in a plane of three-dimensional measure zero. Fubini proves the assertion. Since $|U^4|=1$, continuity from above of Lebesgue measure therefore gives
\[
 b_\varepsilon\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }\varepsilon\downarrow0.
 \tag{3}
\]

We next control the error from replacing a location $mu_i$ by an arbitrary point in its unit cube. If $\mathbf u,\mathbf w\in U^4$ and
$\max_i\|w_i-u_i\|\leq\delta$, then
\[
 |V(\mathbf w)-V(\mathbf u)|\leq3\delta.
 \tag{4}
\]
To verify (4), set $a_j=u_{j+1}-u_1$ and $b_j=w_{j+1}-w_1$ for $j=1,2,3$. All six vectors have norm at most $\sqrt3$, and
$\|b_j-a_j\|\leq2\delta$. Subtract the two determinants by changing their columns one at a time. Each of the three differences has absolute value at most
$(2\delta)(\sqrt3)^2=6\delta$ by the determinant bound
$|\det[v_1,v_2,v_3]|\leq\prod_j\|v_j\|$.
The absolute-value operation does not increase a difference; division by 6 gives (4).

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $\mathbf u\in G_\varepsilon$. For any choice
$y_i\in Q_{z_m(u_i)}$, the normalised points $w_i=y_i/m$ lie in $U$ and satisfy
$\|w_i-u_i\|\leq\sqrt3/m$. Thus, when $m>6\sqrt3/\varepsilon$, (4) gives
\[
 V(\mathbf y)=m^3V(\mathbf w)>
 \frac{\varepsilon m^3}{2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
This statement holds for every point of the product of the four cubes. In particular, those cubes must be distinct: if two were identical, we could choose the corresponding $y_i$ equal, contradicting (5). They are therefore disjoint because our cubes are half-open.

Hadamard's determinant bound also gives $V(\mathbf y)\leq d(\mathbf y)^3/6$.
Consequently (5) implies
$d(\mathbf y)>(3\varepsilon)^{1/3}m$. Increasing $m$ further if necessary ensures that the entire product of these four cubes lies in the domain of (1).

For these distinct cubes, every point tuple contributing to their count product is pairwise distinct. Hence (1) and (5) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\left[\prod_{i=1}^4 I_{z_m(u_i)}\right]
 &\leq\mathbb E\left[\prod_{i=1}^4 X(Q_{z_m(u_i)})\right]\\
 &=\alpha^{(4)}\left(\prod_{i=1}^4Q_{z_m(u_i)}\right)
 \leq\frac{2a}{\varepsilon m^3}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{6}
\]
The last bound uses the fact that each cube has volume one. The product-count expectation is allowed to be extended before applying (1); the bound proves it finite on the selected products. We never require a finite fourth moment for the count in their union.

On $B_\varepsilon$ the product of the four indicators is at most one. Splitting (2) over $B_\varepsilon$ and $G_\varepsilon$, and using $|G_\varepsilon|\leq1$, gives
\[
 p^4\leq b_\varepsilon+\frac{2a}{\varepsilon m^3}
 \tag{7}
\]
for all sufficiently large integers $m$. By (3), choose a fixed $\varepsilon>0$ with $b_\varepsilon<p^4/2$. Letting $m\to\infty$ in (7) gives
$p^4\leq b_\varepsilon<p^4/2$, a contradiction. $\square$

All expectations in (2) concern bounded indicators. Large or infinite factorial moments near coplanarity are discarded using the Lebesgue measure of a set of normalised cube locations, not by making an integrability assumption on the actual point counts. On the remaining products, all configurations are uniformly separated from coplanarity after rescaling. This is why the local divergence of $1/V$ does not prevent the global contradiction.

### 2. The reviewed pair obstruction

We retain the following stronger-than-S0 result from v01. A simple locally finite process with homogeneous mean measure $\lambda\,dx$, $\lambda>0$, cannot have full pair measure
$\alpha^{(2)}(dx\,dy)=a_2\|y-x\|^{-1}dx\,dy$, with $0<a_2<\infty$.
Stationarity beyond the homogeneous mean measure is unnecessary.

For $B_R=B(0,R)$, $v=4\pi/3$, and $N_R=X(B_R)$, the full measure identity and nonnegative integration give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(N_R)_2
 &=a_2\int_{B_R^2}\frac{dx\,dy}{\|y-x\|}\\
 &\leq a_2|B_R|\int_{\|h\|<2R}\frac{dh}{\|h\|}
 =8\pi a_2vR^5.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
\]
Here $(N)_k$ denotes the falling factorial, zero for nonnegative integer $N<k$.
The diagonal is null and the full density assumption excludes additional singular mass. The finite bound (8) together with $\mathbb E N_R=\lambda vR^3$ proves a finite second moment. Thus
\[
 \lambda^2v^2R^6
 =(\mathbb E N_R)^2
 \leq\mathbb E N_R^2
 \leq8\pi a_2vR^5+\lambda vR^3,
\]
which is impossible as $R\to\infty$. The relevant moment is derived from the density, not assumed.

The v01 tail strengthening also remains valid. Suppose instead that a stationary process of positive finite intensity has the following property: for every $\eta>0$, its full pair measure on all sufficiently distant pairs is dominated by $\eta\,dx\,dy$. For the above unit-cube indicators, put $S_m=\sum_{z\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^3}I_z$, and $n=m^3$. The common occupancy probability $p$ is positive. For a fixed $\eta$, only at most $C_\eta n$ ordered cube pairs have separation too small to use the tail bound, where $C_\eta<\infty$ does not depend on $m$. All other distinct cube pairs satisfy
\[
 \mathbb E(I_zI_w)
 \leq\alpha^{(2)}(Q_z\times Q_w)\leq\eta.
\]
Indeed, a centre separation exceeding the tail threshold plus $\sqrt3$ makes every pair of points in the two cubes lie beyond that threshold. For the remaining products use the bound one. It follows that
\[
 n^2p^2\leq\mathbb E S_m^2
 \leq(1+C_\eta)n+\eta n^2.
\]
First let $m\to\infty$ at fixed $\eta$, then let $\eta\downarrow0$. This contradicts $p>0$. In particular, a reciprocal pair law beyond any fixed lower-distance threshold remains impossible regardless of arbitrary short-range factorial moments. This theorem asserts no mixing or pointwise limit for the pair density.

### 3. The reviewed triangle obstruction

Independently of the pair law, no simple locally finite process with homogeneous mean measure $\lambda\,dx$, $\lambda>0$, has the unrestricted full third factorial density $a_3/A$ with finite $a_3>0$.

To prove the needed local integrability, fix $x_1\in B_R$ and set
$u=x_2-x_1$, $w=x_3-x_1$. Their norms are at most $2R$, and
$A=\|u\times w\|/2$. Spherical integration, with $\theta$ the angle between $u$ and $w$, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{B_R^3}\frac{d\mathbf x}{A}
 &\leq |B_R|
 \int_{\|u\|,\|w\|<2R}\frac{2\,du\,dw}{\|u\times w\|}\\
 &=|B_R|\,2(4\pi)(2\pi)
 \left(\int_0^{2R}r\,dr\right)^2
 \int_0^\pi d\theta
 =64\pi^3vR^7.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
The angular volume element cancels the reciprocal $\sin\theta$; the radial integrals handle coincidences. The exceptional collinear set is null by the line-fibre argument used above. Tonelli justifies the nonnegative calculation before its finiteness is known.

The full measure identity gives
$\mathbb E(N_R)_3\leq64\pi^3a_3vR^7$. Since
$(N)_3\geq(N-2)_+^3$ for every nonnegative integer $N$, Jensen's inequality for the convex function $t\mapsto(t-2)_+^3$ yields
\[
 (\lambda vR^3-2)_+^3
 \leq\mathbb E(N_R)_3
 \leq64\pi^3a_3vR^7.
 \tag{10}
\]
Division by $R^9$ and the limit $R\to\infty$ give a contradiction.

### 4. The reviewed local fourth-moment obstruction remains separate

Suppose the reciprocal-volume density $a_4/V$ is prescribed on all noncoplanar quadruples in a nonempty open ball $B$, with $a_4>0$. Then
\[
 \mathbb E(X(B))_4=\infty,\qquad \mathbb E X(B)^4=\infty.
 \tag{11}
\]

For completeness, fix a noncollinear base triple in $B$, with area $A>0$, affine plane $P$, and unit normal $\nu$. Take
$0<\delta<\operatorname{dist}(x_1,B^c)/\sqrt2$. The cylinder
$x_4=z+t\nu$, $z\in P$, $\|z-x_1\|<\delta$, $|t|<\delta$, lies in $B$.
In these orthonormal coordinates $dx_4=d\mathcal H^2(z)\,dt$ and
$V=A|t|/3$. Therefore
\[
 \int_{B\cap\{V>0\}}\frac{dx_4}{V}
 \geq\frac{3\pi\delta^2}{A}
 \int_{0<|t|<\delta}\frac{dt}{|t|}
 =\infty.
 \tag{12}
\]
Almost every base triple is noncollinear. Tonelli and the full fourth measure identity imply (11), using $N^4\geq(N)_4$ for the last implication.

The divergence occurs on noncoplanar configurations; changing the density only on the exactly coplanar null set cannot remove it. On a bounded-diameter replacement, a sufficiently small ball still satisfies its diameter condition, so (11) persists if there is no additional geometric restriction.

Equation (11) by itself is only a finite-moment-class obstruction. A finite integer-valued random variable with probabilities proportional to $k^{-5}$ has finite first three moments and infinite fourth moment. This is a count example, not a spatial construction. Theorem 1 supplies a different, global argument which applies even when (11) is allowed.

## Consequences for S0 and possible amendments

Each unrestricted reciprocal law, separately, now has a general obstruction under the process assumptions stated here:

| Required law | General obstruction | Extra finite-moment assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Full pair density $a_2/D$ | Large-ball count inequality; also arbitrary-short-range tail theorem | None |
| Full triangle density $a_3/A$ | Large-ball third factorial inequality | None |
| Full fourth density $a_4/V$ | Theorem 1, using bounded occupancy and nondegenerate large configurations | None |

The pair and triangle direct arguments only require homogeneous positive finite mean measure. Theorem 1 uses stationarity to give the unit cubes one positive occupancy probability. No law at either lower order enters Theorem 1.

If the stationary positive-intensity class and full-density interpretation are kept, the all-scale fourth identity must therefore change, just as the all-scale pair and triangle identities must. In particular, leaving the fourth reciprocal law on every sufficiently large noncoplanar configuration cannot work, even if all smaller-diameter configurations are made unrestricted. Arbitrary singular mass on coplanar configurations would not help with Theorem 1's chosen products either.

This new result provides independent evidence for reconsidering fourth-order large-diameter requirements. It does not identify a unique replacement. A common finite diameter cap is one way to remove the hypotheses of all three general obstructions, but a hard cap is not proved necessary, maximal or sufficient. Requirements on selected unbounded domains, with a sufficiently unrestricted complement, are not classified by these proofs.

The provisional question proposed in v01 remains a precise possible question: **for every independently prescribed $\lambda_0>0$ and $M\in(0,\infty)$**, is there a stationary simple locally finite process of intensity $\lambda_0$, with full factorial densities and positive finite constants $a_2,a_3,a_4$, such that
\[
 \rho^{(n)}=a_n/\Delta_n
 \quad\text{a.e. on }\{0<\Delta_n,\ \operatorname{diam}<M\},
 \qquad
 (\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)=(D,A,V)?
\]
The densities outside these domains would be unspecified, no singular components would be permitted anywhere, and bounded-set higher moments could be infinite. There would be no positive lower distance, altitude or shape cutoff. The constants and process could depend on both prescribed parameters. This question has **not** been answered or adopted. Theorem 1 uses arbitrarily large configurations and hence does not decide it.

If finite fourth count moments are imposed, (11) rules out that provisional question without additional changes near coplanarity. That local implication still must not be transferred to the extended-moment class. Likewise, no positive lower altitude, positive lower distance, particular shape threshold, or numerical cutoff is proved necessary for that broader class. A construction valid only at low intensity for fixed prescribed $M$ would not settle the displayed independent quantifiers.

S0 remains the active frozen statement. The original nonexistence verdict is preserved; the new theorem is an extension of its evidence, not a silent target change.

## Checks and limitations

- The normalised small-volume set is measured in the fixed compact cube $U^4$; its measure tends to zero because coplanarity is null. This does not assert that the reciprocal-volume integral on that set is finite.

## Reasoning summary and decision record

The local reciprocal-volume divergence prevents a useful upper bound on the fourth moment of the count in a whole ball. We instead average unit-cube occupancy indicators, whose fourth powers remain bounded. Positive stationary intensity gives a positive mean occupancy. After expressing the fourth power as an integral over normalised cube locations, we discard a set of small Lebesgue measure near coplanarity. On its complement, the tetrahedral volume of every corresponding point tuple grows as $m^3$, so the required fourth factorial measure forces the occupancy product expectation to vanish. This contradicts the positive fourth-power lower bound.

We retained all reviewed v01 claims and their scope qualifications. The fourth-order domain issue is now addressed by a separate general theorem, rather than by treating a moment divergence as a local-finiteness contradiction. No replacement target has been adopted.
