## Exact claim and scope

For every prescribed $\lambda_0>0$, S0 has no solution. Theorem 1 proves this
using only its first and second factorial measures. It does not require mixing,
ergodicity, isotropy, a cluster representation, or a fourth moment assumption.
Indeed, translation invariance in law is unnecessary once the constant first
moment measure is assumed.

The local geometry gives a different conclusion. The reciprocal-distance and
reciprocal-area kernels are locally integrable on their full configuration
spaces. The reciprocal-volume kernel has an infinite integral in every
nonempty ball to the fourth power. Its divergence persists near coplanar
quadruples with all edges and all triangular faces bounded away from
degeneracy. Consequently, imposing the fourth identity on those neighbourhoods
is incompatible with finite fourth local count moments. **This local result
alone does not exclude S0's broader moment class.**

Theorem 4 provides an explicit stationary, simple, locally finite process of
any prescribed positive intensity whose third local count moments are finite,
whose fourth local moments are infinite on every ball, and whose full
factorial measures nevertheless have densities finite almost everywhere.
It is a counterexample to a possible inference from infinite fourth moments
to non-local-finiteness or to the absence of genuine diffuse densities. It
does not satisfy the reciprocal-simplex identities.

We use the S0 conventions
\[
 D=|x_2-x_1|,\qquad
 A=\frac12|(x_2-x_1)\times(x_3-x_1)|,\qquad
 V=\frac16|\det(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1)|.
\]
All tuples are ordered. The notation $(N)_k=N(N-1)\cdots(N-k+1)$ denotes a
falling factorial, with $(N)_k=0$ for integer $0\leq N<k$.

## Argument

### Theorem 1: a global obstruction at order two

**Statement.** For any $\lambda>0$ and $a>0$, there is no simple, almost surely
locally finite point process on $\mathbb R^3$ with first moment measure
$\lambda\,dx$ and full second factorial measure
\[
 \alpha^{(2)}(dx\,dy)=a|x-y|^{-1}\,dx\,dy .
\]
The identity is required Lebesgue-almost everywhere off the diagonal, with
no additional singular component.

**Proof.** Let $B_R$ be the ball of radius $R$ about the origin, let
$v=|B_1|>0$, and put
\[
 J_2=\int_{B_1^2}|u-v'|^{-1}\,du\,dv'.
\]
This number is positive and finite. For finiteness, at each $u\in B_1$ the
difference $v'-u$ lies in $B_2$, and
$\int_{B_2}|z|^{-1}dz=4\pi\int_0^2 r\,dr<\infty$.
The change of variables $x=Ru$, $y=Rv'$ therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E X(B_R)=\lambda vR^3,\qquad
 \mathbb E(X(B_R))_2=aJ_2R^5 .
\]
These are identities of nonnegative integrals from the full factorial
measure, and show in particular that the second count moment is finite.
The elementary identity $N^2=(N)_2+N$ and
$\mathbb E N^2\geq(\mathbb E N)^2$ now imply, for every $R>0$,
\[
 \lambda^2v^2
 \leq \frac{\lambda v}{R^3}+\frac{aJ_2}{R}.
\]
The right side tends to zero as $R\to\infty$, a contradiction. This proves
the theorem and excludes S0 for each prescribed positive intensity. $\square$

The mechanism is a comparison of a count with its own mean. The first
moment grows as volume, whereas the prescribed second factorial moment
grows more slowly than the square of volume. No assumption about distant
independence enters.

### Lemma 2: local integrability at orders two and three

**Statement.** For every bounded Borel set $B\subset\mathbb R^3$,
\[
 \int_{B^2} D^{-1}\,d\mathbf x<\infty,\qquad
 \int_{B^3} A^{-1}\,d\mathbf x<\infty,
\]
where the kernels are assigned zero on their degenerate null sets.

**Proof.** Choose $L>0$ such that $|x-y|\leq L$ for all $x,y\in B$.
The pair integral is at most
$|B|\int_{|u|\leq L}|u|^{-1}du=2\pi L^2|B|$.

For the triple integral, fix $x_1$, and write $u=x_2-x_1$ and
$v=x_3-x_1$. For $u\ne0$, use cylindrical coordinates
$v=z e+w$, where $e=u/|u|$, $w\perp e$, and $s=|w|$. Then
$|u\times v|=|u|s$. Enlarging the ball $|v|\leq L$ to the cylinder
$|z|\leq L$, $0\leq s\leq L$, we obtain
\[
 \int_{|v|\leq L}\frac{dv}{|u\times v|}
 \leq \frac1{|u|}
       \int_{-L}^{L}\int_0^L\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\,ds\,dz
 =\frac{4\pi L^2}{|u|}.
\]
The factor $s$ in three-dimensional volume cancels the reciprocal
transverse distance. Since $A^{-1}=2|u\times v|^{-1}$,
\[
 \int_{B^3}A^{-1}\,d\mathbf x
 \leq 2|B|(4\pi L^2)(2\pi L^2)
 =16\pi^2L^4|B|<\infty .
\]
Tonelli's theorem permits each rearrangement because the integrands are
nonnegative. The resulting finite bound establishes integrability. $\square$

In particular, the first three S0 measure identities, if considered locally,
would imply finite third count moments on every bounded $B$, since
\[
 N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N.
\]
This observation does not overcome Theorem 1's separate global contradiction.
It identifies the local moment class associated with the proposed kernels.

### Theorem 3: coplanarity creates a local fourth-moment obstruction

**Statement.** Let $B$ be any nonempty open ball in $\mathbb R^3$. Then
\[
 \int_{B^4\cap\{V>0\}} V^{-1}\,d\mathbf x=\infty.
 \tag{1}
\]
There is a subset responsible for this divergence on which every pairwise
distance and every triangular face area has a strictly positive lower bound
and the diameter has a finite upper bound. These constants may depend on $B$.

Consequently, if a simple, locally finite process has
$\alpha^{(4)}(d\mathbf x)=a_4V^{-1}d\mathbf x$ on all nondegenerate
configurations in $B^4$, with $a_4>0$, then
$\mathbb E(X(B))_4=\mathbb E X(B)^4=\infty$. More generally it suffices
that this equality hold on the positive-measure family used below. Additional
nonnegative measure elsewhere cannot repair the divergence.

**Proof.** First work near the planar square
\[
 q_1=(0,0,0),\quad q_2=(1,0,0),\quad
 q_3=(0,1,0),\quad q_4=(1,1,0).
\]
Its six distances and four face areas are all positive. Take $x_1,x_2,x_3$
in sufficiently small open balls around $q_1,q_2,q_3$. Write
\[
 C=|(x_2-x_1)\times(x_3-x_1)|,\quad
 e=\frac{x_2-x_1}{|x_2-x_1|},\quad
 n=\frac{(x_2-x_1)\times(x_3-x_1)}{C},\quad
 f=n\times e .
\]
These depend continuously on the chosen base triple, and its neighbourhoods
can be chosen so that $1/2\leq C\leq2$.
Let $p=x_2+x_3-x_1$, and parameterise the fourth vertex by
\[
 x_4=p+s e+t f+h n,\qquad |s|,|t|,|h|<\delta.
 \tag{2}
\]
For a fixed sufficiently small $\delta>0$, all four vertices remain near the
square uniformly over the base neighbourhoods. Continuity therefore supplies
positive lower bounds for all six distances and all four face areas, as
well as a finite upper diameter bound.

For each fixed base triple, $(s,t,h)\mapsto x_4$ is an isometry with
Lebesgue Jacobian one. Since $p-x_1$, $e$, and $f$ lie in the base plane,
\[
 V(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=\frac{C|h|}{6}.
\]
Thus the integral over the fourth vertices in (2), with $h\ne0$, equals
\[
 \frac{6}{C}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\!\!ds
             \int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\!\!dt
             \int_{0<|h|<\delta}\frac{dh}{|h|}
 =\infty.
\]
The product of the three base neighbourhoods has positive nine-dimensional
Lebesgue measure. Tonelli therefore gives divergence of the full integral on
this family. This remains valid for any density equal to $a_4/V$ almost
everywhere: null exceptional configurations have zero integral, including
when the full integral is infinite.

Translate and shrink the square and its neighbourhoods into the given ball
$B$. Under a spatial scaling by $r>0$, the product-volume element scales
by $r^{12}$ and $V^{-1}$ by $r^{-3}$; the positive finite factor $r^9$
does not change divergence. This proves (1) and its stated geometric
strengthening.

Finally,
$\mathbb E(X(B))_4=\alpha^{(4)}(B^4)=\infty$ under the prescribed identity.
Since $N^4\geq(N)_4$, the fourth raw count moment is also infinite.
Almost-sure finiteness of $N$ has not been contradicted. $\square$

The divergence is logarithmic in transverse height. Deleting only the
exactly coplanar set leaves it unchanged. A lower edge-distance threshold
or a lower bound on face areas also leaves such a family whenever its
thresholds admit a neighbourhood of the chosen planar square. This is not
a claim that arbitrary numerical thresholds always admit that square:
the relevant condition is that the retained domain contain the exhibited
family. Restricting diameter from above does not help when all scales
below that bound remain admitted.

The degenerate sets themselves are Lebesgue-null. Conditional on a distinct
pair, the third point lies on its line only on a three-dimensional null set;
conditional on a noncollinear triple, the fourth point lies in its plane
only on a three-dimensional null set. Fubini, together with the null pair
diagonal, proves these assertions in the full product spaces. Under S0's
full measure identities, such sets have zero factorial expectation measure.
For every bounded ball the corresponding nonnegative integer tuple count is
therefore zero almost surely. A countable exhaustion by balls shows that
there would almost surely be no exactly collinear triples or exactly
coplanar quadruples anywhere. This absence is compatible with an infinite
expected count in each neighbourhood of coplanarity and supplies no repair
to (1).

### Theorem 4: infinite fourth moments do not themselves exclude the broad class

**Statement.** For every $\lambda>0$ there exists a stationary, simple, almost
surely locally finite point process $Y$ on $\mathbb R^3$ such that:

1. $\mathbb E Y(B)=\lambda|B|$ for bounded Borel $B$.
2. $\mathbb E Y(B)^3<\infty$ for every bounded Borel $B$.
3. $\mathbb E Y(B)^4=\infty$ for every nonempty open ball $B$.
4. Every full factorial expectation measure of positive finite order has a
   Lebesgue density finite on pairwise distinct tuples, after choosing values
   arbitrarily on the diagonals. There is no singular component.

**Construction and proof.** Let $N$ be a positive integer with
\[
 \mathbb P(N=n)=c n^{-5}\quad(n\geq1),\qquad
 c=\left(\sum_{n\geq1}n^{-5}\right)^{-1}.
\]
Then $m=\mathbb EN\in(0,\infty)$, $\mathbb EN^3<\infty$, and
$\mathbb E(N)_4=\infty$ by comparison respectively with convergent
$p$-series and the harmonic series. Take a homogeneous Poisson process of
parents of intensity $\kappa=\lambda/m$. Independently at each parent $z$,
sample $N$, then place $N$ independent offspring uniformly in
$B(z,r_N)$, with $r_n=1/(10n)$. Let $f_n$ be the uniform probability
density on $B(0,r_n)$.

Every cluster is finite almost surely and has radius at most $1/10$.
Only parents within distance $1/10$ of a bounded observation set can
contribute. That parent count is finite almost surely, so $Y$ is locally
finite. Conditional on parents and marks, offspring positions are diffuse;
a countable union over pairs proves simplicity. Translation covariance of
the construction gives strict stationarity. Integrating each offspring
density over the parent position, using Tonelli, gives
$\mathbb E Y(B)=\kappa\mathbb EN\,|B|=\lambda|B|$.

For bounded $B$, dominate $Y(B)$ by
$Z=\sum_{j=1}^M N_j$, where $M$ is the Poisson count of parents in a
bounded region containing the $1/10$ enlargement of $B$. If its mean is
$\theta<\infty$, expansion into equal and distinct parent indices gives
\[
 \mathbb EZ^3
 =\theta\mathbb EN^3
  +3\theta^2(\mathbb EN^2)(\mathbb EN)
  +\theta^3(\mathbb EN)^3<\infty.
\]
This establishes assertion 2.

For a ball $B$ of radius $R>0$, let $B'$ be its concentric ball of radius
$R/2$. Choose $n_0$ so that $r_n<R/2$ for $n\geq n_0$. Each parent in
$B'$ with such a mark contributes its entire cluster to $B$. Counting
only ordered quadruples within those clusters yields
\[
 \mathbb E(Y(B))_4
 \geq \kappa |B'|\sum_{n\geq n_0}c n^{-5}(n)_4=\infty.
\]

For completeness, the full density, including every parent allocation, is
explicit. For any nonempty block $I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}$ of size $j$, set
\[
 K_j(x_I)=\kappa\sum_{n\geq1}c n^{-5}(n)_j
                \int_{\mathbb R^3}\prod_{i\in I}f_n(x_i-z)\,dz.
 \tag{3}
\]
For each $k\geq1$, the full factorial density is
\[
 \rho_Y^{(k)}(x_1,\ldots,x_k)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P(\{1,\ldots,k\})}
       \prod_{I\in\pi}K_{|I|}(x_I).
 \tag{4}
\]
To justify the measure identity, assign the ordered distinct offspring
labels to their parent clusters. The resulting equivalence classes give
exactly one set partition $\pi$. Within a block of size $j$, conditional
on mark $n$, there are $(n)_j$ ordered choices and the joint density is the
product in (3). Different blocks use distinct parents. The factorial
expectation measure of $q$ distinct Poisson parents is
$\kappa^q dz_1\cdots dz_q$: for disjoint boxes this follows from independent
Poisson counts and $\mathbb E(M)_j=\theta^j$, and partitioning boxes and
monotone extension gives the identity for nonnegative measurable tests.
Integrating the independent marks now gives (4). All summands and
integrands are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies without an assumption of
finite high moments. The resulting identity covers the whole factorial
expectation measure.

Here $K_1=\kappa m=\lambda$. If $j\geq2$ and the points in the block
are pairwise distinct, their diameter $d$ is positive. The integral in
(3) vanishes unless $d\leq2r_n=1/(5n)$. Only finitely many integers $n$
can then contribute, and each individual integral is finite, for example
bounded by $\|f_n\|_\infty^{j-1}$. Consequently every factor in every term
of the finite sum (4) is finite on pairwise distinct tuples. Each component
measure has a density, so their nonnegative sum has no singular component.
The diagonals are null and their values may be set to zero. This proves
assertion 4 and the theorem. $\square$

This example is deliberately used only to test a logical implication. Its
densities are not the reciprocal-simplex densities, and it gives no
existence result for a domain-restricted version of S0. It shows that even
finite-almost-everywhere full densities and finite third local moments do
not upgrade almost-sure local finiteness to finite fourth local moments.

### Logical consequences and possible replacement questions

Theorem 1 excludes the unrestricted full pair identity at every positive
intensity. Any proposed repair retaining that identity at all separations,
constant positive intensity, and full second measure equality still fails.
Restricting only the fourth-order domain therefore cannot repair S0.
The proof does not show that an upper distance cutoff is the only repair,
or that any particular finite domain is sufficient. For example, leaving
large-separation values unprescribed avoids the premise of this argument;
changing the density outside a neighbourhood of the diagonal is a different
choice. A cumulant interpretation would change the mathematical question
and requires explicit authority under the run rules.

Theorem 3, separately, says that retaining the reciprocal-volume law on the
displayed near-coplanar families requires infinite fourth local moments.
If a replacement explicitly requires finite fourth local moments, those
families cannot remain unchanged. A positive-volume or altitude restriction
is a possible choice; changing the density on such neighbourhoods is another.
A positive threshold is not proved necessary: the precise integrability
condition is that the retained portion of $V^{-1}$ have finite local
integrals, and domains may approach the singular set while narrowing in
other coordinates. Excluding the exactly degenerate null set is insufficient.
In S0's broad moment class this theorem alone justifies **no compulsory
lower altitude cutoff and no compulsory finite-moment assumption**.

A precise replacement question removing only the general obstruction
proved here is the following **proposal, not an adopted statement**:

> For every prescribed $\lambda_0>0$ and every prescribed $M\in(0,\infty)$,
> where $M$ is specified independently of $\lambda_0$, does there exist a
> stationary, simple, locally finite process of intensity $\lambda_0$ whose
> full second, third and fourth factorial measures are Lebesgue absolutely
> continuous, and constants $a_2,a_3,a_4>0$, such that
> $\rho^{(n)}=a_n/\Delta_n$ almost everywhere on
> $\{0<\Delta_n,\ \operatorname{diam}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)<M\}$, for $n=2,3,4$?
> Outside those domains the full densities are unprescribed. No fourth local
> moment assumption is added; the identities would force infinite fourth
> moments on every sufficiently small ball.

This proposal retains exact equality, full densities, all three orders and
the prescribed intensity. It changes the global geometric domain only.
No existence, sufficiency, maximality, or uniqueness claim is made. The
all-intensity, independently prescribed $M$ quantifiers are explicit
additional research obligations, not consequences of the obstruction.
For a separate finite-fourth-moment replacement one could also prescribe
$0<\varepsilon_2<M$, $0<\varepsilon_3<M^2$, and
$0<\varepsilon_4<M^3$ and impose the identities only where
$\operatorname{diam}<M$ and $\Delta_n>\varepsilon_n$, provided each chosen
domain is nonempty. That is a different proposed problem, and its extra
moment requirement is not forced on the broad S0 class.
