## Exact claim and scope

S1 still asks for every independent pair $\lambda,M>0$ and all three exact **full** factorial densities $a_2/D,a_3/A,a_4/V$ on the respective nondegenerate diameter-$M$ domains $E_2(M),E_3(M),E_4(M)$. No geometric lower cutoff or finite-fourth-moment assumption is available. This submission does not prove S1.

The new theorem concerns the entire geometric domains, including arbitrarily small and arbitrarily flat configurations, but retains a restriction on intensity and an approximation at order four.

**Theorem.** Suppose $\lambda,M>0$ satisfy
\[
 2\pi t\leq\frac12,\qquad
 8\pi^3t^2+12\pi^2t\leq\frac12,\qquad t=\lambda M^3.
 \tag{1}
\]
For every prescribed $\eta\in(0,1)$ there is a stationary isotropic simple locally finite Poisson-cluster process $X$ on $\mathbb R^3$ of intensity exactly $\lambda$ and positive finite constants $a_2,a_3,a$ such that
\[
 \rho^{(2)}=\frac{a_2}{D}\quad\text{on }E_2(M),\qquad
 \rho^{(3)}=\frac{a_3}{A}\quad\text{on }E_3(M),
 \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 (1-\eta)\frac{a}{V}
 \leq \rho^{(4)}
 \leq (1+\eta)\frac{a}{V}
 \quad\text{on }E_4(M).
 \tag{3}
\]
These are statements about the full densities, almost everywhere. The full factorial measures at orders two, three and four are globally absolutely continuous, with densities finite almost everywhere and no singular component. Counts have finite third moments on every bounded Borel set and infinite fourth moments on every nonempty open ball. Every individual parent emits a finite cluster, but cluster population is unbounded and depends on the cluster's geometry.

The constants $a_2,a_3$ depend on $\lambda,M$; $a$ and the process may also depend on $\eta$. There is no exact fourth-order identity in this theorem, and (1) does not cover all prescribed intensities. Approximation, even uniform over all shapes and arbitrarily accurate, is **partial progress only**.

The principal new ingredient is an explicit infinite mixture of finite cylindrical clusters. Its fourth same-parent density is calculated exactly by a one-dimensional integral and an elementary polar-volume identity. Its lower factorial contributions can be made arbitrarily small without changing that fourth density. Finite two- and three-point clusters then correct the full lower-order identities exactly.

## Argument

### 1. Parent partitions and the notation repair

We use the finite-mark framework of v01: a Poisson random measure on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathcal C$ with intensity $dz\,\nu(dC)$, where $\mathcal C$ consists of finite nonempty simple point configurations and
\[
 \int |C|\,\nu(dC)<\infty.
 \tag{4}
\]
A parent $(z,C)$ emits $z+C$. Different independent streams can be combined by adding their mark measures. Empty clusters, if any, can be discarded.

For a partition $\pi=\{I_1,\ldots,I_q\}$ of tuple labels, ordered by the least label of each block, define its contribution to a nonnegative test $g$ **at parent level**:
\[
 \int\prod_{\ell=1}^q[dz_\ell\,\nu(dC_\ell)]
 \sum_{\substack{(y_i)_{i\in I_\ell}\in C_\ell^{|I_\ell|}\\
                    \text{distinct within each block}}}
 g\big((z_{\ell(i)}+y_i)_{i=1}^r\big).
 \tag{5}
\]
The sums for all blocks are taken inside the integral. The underlying measures in (5) are $\sigma$-finite, so nonnegative integration is unambiguous. The factorial measure of distinct Poisson parents is this product measure; the partition of each observed tuple by its parents is unique. Summing (5) over all partitions therefore defines the full factorial expectation measure.

Only when the image same-parent measures $K_j$ are $\sigma$-finite do we also write their ordinary product-measure version. In particular, if they have finite-almost-everywhere densities $k_j$, the density formula is
\[
 \rho^{(r)}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{\pi}\prod_{I\in\pi}k_{|I|}(\mathbf x_I).
 \tag{6}
\]
This resolves P1V01-03: no product of arbitrary non-$\sigma$-finite image measures is required.

For the construction below the densities are explicitly proved finite almost everywhere through order four, so (6) applies in the usual sense. With total intensity $\lambda$, it reads
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \rho^{(2)}&=\lambda^2+k_2,\\
 \rho^{(3)}&=\lambda^3+\lambda\sum_{i<j}k_2(ij)+k_3,\\
 \rho^{(4)}&=\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}k_2(ij)
       +\sum_{ij|kl}k_2(ij)k_2(kl)
       +\lambda\sum_{|J|=3}k_3(J)+k_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
There are six edges, three pairings, four faces, and the two remaining partitions at order four. Thus the formula includes all 15 terms.

### 2. Finite clusters in random cylinders

Fix a lateral radius $R>0$, a reference length $\ell>0$, a population parameter $T\geq1$, and $c>0$. Let $\sigma$ be uniform probability measure on the unit sphere. For $n\in S^2$, write $P_n=I-nn^\mathsf T$ and define the probability density
\[
 f_{\varepsilon,n}(y)
 =\frac{\mathbf1_{\{|P_ny|<R,\ |n\cdot y|<\varepsilon\}}}
        {2\varepsilon\pi R^2},
 \qquad \varepsilon>0.
 \tag{8}
\]
This is the uniform density in a cylinder of half-height $\varepsilon$.

Set
\[
 g_\ell(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon/\ell+\ell/\varepsilon,\qquad
 N_T(\varepsilon)=4+\left\lceil T g_\ell(\varepsilon)\right\rceil.
 \tag{9}
\]
A type $(\varepsilon,n)$ parent emits $N_T(\varepsilon)$ iid displacements with density (8). Its type-rate measure per unit parent volume is
\[
 \frac{c}{(N_T(\varepsilon))_4}\,
       \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(dn).
 \tag{10}
\]
Here $(N)_r$ denotes the falling factorial. Equation (10), together with the conditional iid law, defines an actual nonnegative measure on finite simple marks. Simplicity within a mark follows from its diffuse displacement density; each population in (9) is finite.

Let
\[
 m_r=c\int_0^\infty
       \frac{(N_T(\varepsilon))_r}{(N_T(\varepsilon))_4}
       \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon},
 \qquad r=1,2,3.
 \tag{11}
\]
These are factorial population rates, with $m_1$ the spatial intensity of this stream. Since $N_T(\varepsilon)-3\geq T g_\ell(\varepsilon)$, cancellation of falling factorials gives
\[
 \frac{(N_T)_r}{(N_T)_4}\leq
       T^{-(4-r)}g_\ell^{-(4-r)}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The substitution $u=\varepsilon/\ell$ gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty g_\ell(\varepsilon)^{-p}\frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}
 =\int_0^\infty\frac{u^{p-1}}{(1+u^2)^p}\,du.
 \tag{13}
\]
For $p=1,2,3$ the values are $\pi/2,1/2,\pi/16$, respectively, by $u=\tan\theta$ or direct integration. Hence
\[
 m_3\leq\frac{\pi c}{2T},\qquad
 m_2\leq\frac{c}{2T^2},\qquad
 m_1\leq\frac{\pi c}{16T^3}.
 \tag{14}
\]
The total parent rate is finite as well: its integrand is bounded by
$cT^{-4}g_\ell^{-4}\,d\varepsilon/\varepsilon$, whose integral is finite by its powers at zero and infinity. Its fourth factorial population rate is infinite, since cancellation in (10) leaves $c\,d\varepsilon/\varepsilon$.

Let $p_r$ denote the same-parent density contributed by this stream. The conditional iid calculation gives
\[
 p_r(\mathbf x)
 =c\int_0^\infty
       \frac{(N_T(\varepsilon))_r}{(N_T(\varepsilon))_4}
       \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}
       \int_{S^2}\int_{\mathbb R^3}
           \prod_{i=1}^r f_{\varepsilon,n}(x_i-z)\,dz\,\sigma(dn).
 \tag{15}
\]
All operations are nonnegative. Thus (15) proves a full measure identity for the same-parent contribution before any finiteness is asserted.

The stream is stationary and isotropic. Its finite intensity $m_1$ implies almost-sure local finiteness: the expected count in bounded $B$ is $m_1|B|$. Distinct-parent coincidences have zero expectation by the independent translation variables, as in v01. Moreover its third population moment rate is finite by
$N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N$ and (14). For bounded $B$,
\[
 \int dz\int C(B-z)^3\,\nu(dC)
 \leq |B|\int N(C)^3\,\nu(dC)<\infty.
 \tag{16}
\]
The finite Poisson number of clusters meeting $B$ and the expansion of its compound sum consequently give a finite third count moment. Bounded cluster radii are not needed.

### 3. Exact fourth-density calculation

For a fixed tuple $\mathbf x$, let
\[
 w_{\mathbf x}(n)=\max_i n\cdot x_i-\min_i n\cdot x_i
 \tag{17}
\]
be its width in direction $n$. Let $A_R(n;\mathbf x)$ be the two-dimensional area of the intersection, in $n^\perp$, of the radius-$R$ discs centred at $P_nx_i$.

Integrating the parent position separately in the normal and tangential coordinates gives, for each $r$,
\[
 \int\prod_{i=1}^r f_{\varepsilon,n}(x_i-z)\,dz
 =\frac{A_R(n;\mathbf x)(2\varepsilon-w_{\mathbf x}(n))_+}
        {(2\varepsilon\pi R^2)^r}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The normal intersection is an interval of the displayed length; the tangential intersection is exactly the specified disc intersection. These sets and their volumes are measurable, for example directly from their indicator integrals.

At order four, the factorial population factor in (15) cancels completely. If $w>0$, then
\[
 \int_0^\infty
 \frac{(2\varepsilon-w)_+}{(2\varepsilon)^4}
       \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}
 =\int_w^\infty\frac{s-w}{s^5}\,ds
 =\frac1{12w^3}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Consequently, on affinely independent quadruples,
\[
 p_4(\mathbf x)=
 \frac{c}{12(\pi R^2)^4}
 \int_{S^2}\frac{A_R(n;\mathbf x)}{w_{\mathbf x}(n)^3}\,\sigma(dn).
 \tag{20}
\]
This formula is exact and independent of $T$. Every nondegenerate tetrahedron has strictly positive minimum width, so its integrand is finite and bounded above by $\pi R^2$ times the reciprocal minimum width cubed.

The following elementary identity connects (20) with volume:
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \int_{S^2}w_{\mathbf x}(n)^{-3}\,\sigma(dn)=\frac1{2\pi V(\mathbf x)}.}
 \tag{21}
\]
Here is a proof with its normalisation. Translate $x_1$ to zero and put
$U=[x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_1]$. Let $S$ be the standard simplex with vertices $0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, and let $D_0=S-S$. The width is the support function of the centrally symmetric difference body $UD_0$. For any convex body $D$ containing zero in its interior, its polar
$D^\circ=\{y:\sup_{x\in D}y\cdot x\leq1\}$ has radial function equal to the reciprocal support function of $D$. Spherical coordinates therefore give
\[
 |D^\circ|=\frac13\int_{S^2}h_D(n)^{-3}\,dS(n).
 \tag{22}
\]
The polar of $D_0$ is
\[
 D_0^\circ=\{q:
   \max(0,q_1,q_2,q_3)-\min(0,q_1,q_2,q_3)\leq1\}.
 \]
Its volume is four. Indeed, the region where zero is the minimum is $[0,1]^3$, of volume one. Where $q_i=-u<0$ is the minimum, $0<u<1$ and each other coordinate independently ranges over $[-u,1-u]$; integrating gives volume one for each of the three possible $i$. Ties are null. Linear change of variables gives
\[
 |(UD_0)^\circ|=\frac4{|\det U|}
 =\frac{2}{3V}.
 \]
Using $d\sigma=dS/(4\pi)$ in (22) proves (21).

Always $0\leq A_R\leq\pi R^2$. If $\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf x)<M<R$, the disc of radius $R-M$ about $P_nx_1$ lies in every disc defining the intersection. Therefore
\[
 \pi(R-M)^2\leq A_R(n;\mathbf x)\leq\pi R^2.
 \tag{23}
\]
Equations (20)–(23) imply, with
\[
 a=\frac{c}{24\pi^4R^6},
 \tag{24}
\]
the global upper bound $p_4\leq a/V$ on nondegenerate tetrahedra, and the two-sided bound
\[
 (1-M/R)^2\frac{a}{V}\leq p_4\leq\frac{a}{V}
 \quad\text{on }E_4(M).
 \tag{25}
\]
Thus the same-parent fourth density is finite almost everywhere globally. The set of coplanar quadruples is Lebesgue-null. Since (15) is an integral of Lebesgue densities, there is no singular measure there; values of the final density on that null set can be assigned arbitrarily.

The factor $A_R(n;\mathbf x)$ has **not** been discarded from the exact formula. It depends on the configuration, so (20) is not an exact reciprocal-volume law. Equation (25) is a bound, with a radius-controlled error.

### 4. Uniform bounds on the lower contributions

We need lower-order bounds that remain valid near all degeneracies. They allow us to correct the pair and triangle densities without introducing negative cluster kernels.

For a pair of length $d>0$, its width is $d|n\cdot e|$. Under $\sigma$, $|n\cdot e|$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. From (18) and $A_R\leq\pi R^2$,
\[
 \int_{S^2}\int f_{\varepsilon,n}(x_1-z)
                         f_{\varepsilon,n}(x_2-z)\,dz\,\sigma(dn)
 \leq\frac1{2\varepsilon\pi R^2}
       \int_0^1(1-ds/(2\varepsilon))_+\,ds
 \leq\frac1{2\pi R^2d}.
 \tag{26}
\]
If $2\varepsilon\leq d$, the last integral is $\varepsilon/d$. Otherwise set $v=d/(2\varepsilon)\in(0,1)$ and use
$v(1-v/2)\leq1/2$. This proves (26) in both regimes.

For a noncollinear triangle, choose its longest edge as $u=de_1$, and write the third vector from its first endpoint as $v=be_1+he_2$. Then $h>0$, $|b|\leq d$, $h\leq d$, and $A=dh/2$. If its width is below $2\varepsilon$, necessarily
\[
 |n_1|<2\varepsilon/d,\qquad |n_2|<4\varepsilon/h.
 \tag{27}
\]
When $\varepsilon<h/16$, the right sides are at most $1/8$ and $1/4$. On the two sphere graphs over this rectangle, each area Jacobian $1/|n_3|$ is at most two. The rectangle has area $32\varepsilon^2/(dh)$. After including both graphs and normalising by $4\pi$, we obtain
\[
 \sigma\{w_{\mathbf x}(n)<2\varepsilon\}
 \leq \frac{16\varepsilon^2}{\pi A}.
 \tag{28}
\]
When $\varepsilon\geq h/16$, the first strip alone gives the bound
$\min(1,2\varepsilon/d)\leq2\varepsilon/d$.

Using (18) at order three and the factor
$1/[4\varepsilon^2(\pi R^2)^2]$ after bounding its normal overlap by $2\varepsilon$, (28) gives $4/(\pi^3R^4A)$ in the first regime. The second regime gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi^2R^4\varepsilon d}
 \leq\frac4{\pi^2R^4A}.
 \]
Thus a bound valid in both regimes is
\[
 \int_{S^2}\int\prod_{i=1}^3f_{\varepsilon,n}(x_i-z)\,dz\,\sigma(dn)
 \leq\frac4{\pi^2R^4A}.
 \tag{29}
\]
This argument includes triangles with a very short edge or arbitrarily small altitude.

Combining (14)–(15) with (26) and (29), we have globally off the respective degeneracies
\[
 p_2\leq\frac{m_2}{2\pi R^2D}
       \leq\frac{c}{4\pi R^2T^2D},\qquad
 p_3\leq\frac{4m_3}{\pi^2R^4A}
       \leq\frac{2c}{\pi R^4TA},\qquad
 p_1=m_1\leq\frac{\pi c}{16T^3}.
 \tag{30}
\]
Increasing $T$ therefore makes all three lower contributions small while leaving (20) exactly unchanged.

### 5. Correcting the full pair and triangle densities

We now assume (1). Put
\[
 I=2\pi M^2,\qquad J=2\pi^2M^2,\qquad
 W=\frac{4\pi M^3}{3},\qquad
 a_2=\lambda/I,\qquad a_3=a_2/(4J).
 \tag{31}
\]
Define the same finite-domain residuals as in the accepted v01 construction:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_2&=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}(a_2/D-\lambda^2),\\
 q_3&=\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}
       \left(a_3/A-\lambda^3-\lambda\sum_{i<j}q_2(ij)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Write $K_2^0=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}/D$, $K_3^0=\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}/A$, with zero values on degeneracies. The v01 estimates, also recovered by $A\leq M^2/2$ and $A/D\leq M/2$, give
\[
 \frac{a_2}{2}K_2^0\leq q_2\leq a_2K_2^0,\qquad
 \frac{a_3}{2}K_3^0\leq q_3\leq a_3K_3^0.
 \tag{33}
\]
Indeed, the two relative subtraction bounds are exactly the two expressions in (1). Also, writing $\mathcal M$ for integration over one omitted coordinate,
\[
 \mathcal MK_3^0\leq J K_2^0,\qquad
 \int K_2^0(0,y)\,dy=I.
 \tag{34}
\]
For (34), with a fixed base edge of length $d$, cylindrical coordinates give
$2d^{-1}\int_{B(0,M)}\operatorname{dist}(z,\text{base line})^{-1}dz
=2\pi^2M^2/d$ as an upper bound. The triangle support forces the base edge into $E_2(M)$. These are finite nonnegative integrals.

Choose $T$ large enough that
\[
 \frac{c}{4\pi R^2T^2}\leq\frac{a_2}{8},\qquad
 \frac{2c}{\pi R^4T}\leq\frac{a_3}{4},\qquad
 \frac{\pi c}{16T^3}\leq\frac{\lambda^2W}{2}.
 \tag{35}
\]
Every right side is positive, so this is a finite choice for any fixed $c,R$. Take $\ell=M$ in (9), for definiteness.

Let the finite correction kernels be
\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_2&=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}(q_2-p_2),\\
 r_3&=\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}(q_3-p_3),\\
 F_3&=r_3,\qquad F_2=r_2-\mathcal Mr_3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
The restrictions in this formula are deliberate. The cylindrical stream has positive unprescribed densities outside the domains; we do not try to subtract them there.

Equations (30), (33)–(35) imply
\[
 \frac{a_3}{4}K_3^0\leq r_3\leq q_3,\qquad
 F_2\geq
       \left(\frac{a_2}{2}-\frac{a_2}{8}-a_3J\right)K_2^0
       =\frac{a_2}{8}K_2^0.
 \tag{37}
\]
Also $0\leq F_2\leq q_2$. These are nonnegative measurable symmetric invariant kernels with finite positive anchored integrals
$Z_j=\int F_j(0,\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y$.

Add independent size-$j$ Poisson cluster streams, $j=2,3$, of rates $Z_j/j!$. Each parent at $z$ emits $z,z+y_2,\ldots,z+y_j$ with probability density $F_j(0,\mathbf y)/Z_j$ for its offsets. Symmetry and translation invariance imply that its same-parent $r$-density is
$\mathcal M^{j-r}F_j/(j-r)!$. To verify the factor, there are $j!/(j-r)!$ ordered selections, each selected first point can become the translation anchor by a Jacobian-one change of variables, and the parent rate cancels $Z_j$ and $j!$. Selections omitting the original parent point are included.

It remains to supply exactly the missing intensity by singletons. Let
\[
 Q_2=\int q_2(0,y)\,dy=\lambda-\lambda^2W,\qquad
 P_2=\int\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}p_2(0,y)\,dy,\qquad
 R_3=\int r_3(0,y,z)\,dy\,dz.
 \tag{38}
\]
All are finite. The size-two and size-three streams have combined intensity
\[
 Z_2+Z_3/2=Q_2-P_2-R_3/2.
 \]
The cylindrical stream has intensity $m_1$. Add singleton parents of rate
\[
 \beta=\lambda^2W+P_2+R_3/2-m_1.
 \tag{39}
\]
Equation (35) gives $\beta\geq\lambda^2W/2>0$, and direct substitution shows that the total intensity is exactly $\lambda$.

The total same-parent second density equals $p_2+F_2+\mathcal MF_3=p_2+r_2$, hence equals $q_2$ on $E_2(M)$. The total third density is $p_3+F_3=p_3+r_3$, hence equals $q_3$ on $E_3(M)$. Substitution in the **full** formulas (7) proves (2). No approximation has been introduced at either of these orders.

### 6. Controlling the full fourth density

The finite correction streams contribute no same-parent fourth tuples. Thus $k_4=p_4$ globally. On $E_4(M)$ all lower blocks lie in their prescribed domains, so the full fourth density is exactly
\[
 \rho^{(4)}=p_4+B_4,
 \qquad
 B_4=\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}q_2(ij)
      +\sum_{ij|kl}q_2(ij)q_2(kl)
      +\lambda\sum_{|J|=3}q_3(J).
 \tag{40}
\]
In particular, the lower-partition terms have not been dropped. They are nonnegative.

The bounds $q_2\leq a_2/D$ and $q_3\leq a_3/A$, together with elementary determinant estimates, give
\[
 0\leq V B_4\leq C,
 \quad
 C=\frac{\lambda^4M^3}{6}
       +\lambda^2a_2M^2+\frac{a_2^2M}{2}
       +\frac{4\lambda a_3M}{3}.
 \tag{41}
\]
For the six-edge term use $V/D_{ij}\leq M^2/6$, for the three pairings use
$V/(D_{ij}D_{kl})\leq M/6$, and for the four faces use $V/A_J\leq M/3$. For example, the disjoint-edge bound follows from
$6V=|\det(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_3)|$.

We can now make all parameter choices explicitly. Given $\eta\in(0,1)$, choose
\[
 R\geq 2M/\eta,\qquad
 c=24\pi^4R^6\,C/\eta,
 \tag{42}
\]
and then choose $T$ satisfying (35). All quantities are positive and finite. By (24), $a=C/\eta$, and
$(1-M/R)^2\geq1-2M/R\geq1-\eta$. Combining (25), (40) and (41) gives precisely (3).

All streams are genuine finite-mark Poisson processes with finite total intensity. Their superposition is stationary, isotropic, simple and locally finite. The cylindrical stream has finite third local moments by (16); the bounded-population correction streams have finite local exponential moments. Their sum therefore has finite third local count moments. On the other hand, the lower bound in (3) forces infinite fourth moments on every ball by integrating near a small square crossing its base plane. This is the same nonnegative reciprocal-height integral as in v01, and it remains inside $E_4(M)$.

Finally, the same-parent densities $p_2,p_3,p_4$ are finite off their respective null degeneracy sets by (20), (25), and (30). The correction kernels and their required marginals are finite almost everywhere by (33)–(37). Formula (5), followed by (6), therefore proves global full absolute continuity and finite-almost-everywhere densities at orders two, three and four, including outside the prescribed domains. Every term is a density on disjoint coordinate blocks; no singular component is lost at coincidences, collinearity or coplanarity. This completes the theorem.

## What remains unresolved

This construction gives an actual population/geometry-coupled mark law and incorporates all parent partitions, but it does not realise the exact fourth residual required by S1.

The precise fourth-order gap is visible in (20) and (40). An exact solution by this route would require
\[
 \frac{c}{12(\pi R^2)^4}
       \int\frac{A_R(n;\mathbf x)}{w_{\mathbf x}(n)^3}\,\sigma(dn)
       +B_4(\mathbf x)
 =\frac{a_4}{V(\mathbf x)}
 \quad\text{a.e. on all of }E_4(M)
 \tag{43}
\]
for a finite constant $a_4$, or a different actual cluster law that supplies the required difference. The argument proves bounds on the two configuration-dependent terms in (43), not their exact cancellation. Positivity of a formal correction would not itself produce a law on finite configurations.

There is also still an independent quantifier gap: the lower-order correction uses (1). Dilation preserves $\lambda M^3$ and does not remove it.

Taking $\eta\downarrow0$ in the theorem is not a proof of S1. The explicit amplitude $a=C/\eta$ diverges, the cylinder radius and type-rate parameters change, and no fixed finite coefficient or convergence of fourth factorial measures is obtained. Finite intensity and convergence in law alone would not justify convergence of these non-uniformly-integrable fourth moments. This submission makes no limiting-existence claim.
