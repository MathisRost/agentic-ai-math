## Exact claim and scope

S1 prescribes **every independent pair** $\lambda>0$, $M>0$. It asks for one stationary simple locally finite process on $\mathbb R^3$ of intensity $\lambda$, with globally absolutely continuous full factorial measures and densities finite almost everywhere, such that
\[
 \rho^{(2)}=\frac{a_2}{D},\qquad
 \rho^{(3)}=\frac{a_3}{A},\qquad
 \rho^{(4)}=\frac{a_4}{V}
 \quad\text{on }E_2(M),E_3(M),E_4(M),
 \tag{1}
\]
respectively. Here $E_r(M)$ is the set of nondegenerate ordered configurations of diameter less than $M$, and $a_2,a_3,a_4$ are finite positive constants chosen with the process. All shapes in those domains are required. Infinite fourth local count moments are allowed.

This submission does **not** construct a process satisfying (1). It establishes the following partial results.

1. We derive the exact full partition equations for stationary Poisson clusters. For every prescribed $(\lambda,M)$ we exhibit positive constants for which the required same-parent residuals are nonnegative on the full S1 domains. This is a necessary algebraic feasibility check, not a process construction.
2. No such Poisson-cluster solution can have a uniformly bounded cluster population. More generally, a finite fourth population moment is impossible. The uniform-population result does not require bounded cluster radii.
3. The usual iid-offspring construction, with population independent of a fixed displacement law, cannot solve S1 even if its population has an infinite fourth moment. The obstruction also applies when the common displacement law is random but independent of population.
4. In the finite-cluster Poisson framework defined below, any S1 solution must satisfy
   \[
   \int (N(C))_4\,\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{diam}C\geq\delta\}}\,\nu(dC)=\infty
   \quad\text{for every }0<\delta<M/\sqrt2.
   \tag{2}
   \]
   Thus an infinite fourth moment arising solely from increasingly small clusters cannot supply the required fourth-order law near separated coplanar shapes.
5. We give a whole-space construction with the exact **pair and triangle** laws on their entire S1 domains whenever
   \[
   2\pi t\leq\frac12,\qquad
   8\pi^3t^2+12\pi^2t\leq\frac12,\qquad t=\lambda M^3.
   \tag{3}
   \]
   It has finite local exponential moments and therefore fails S1's fourth-order requirement. This partial theorem is rederived from the common seed, with its full measures and intensity checked explicitly.

Items 2–4 concern representation subclasses and do not establish general non-existence for S1. Neither the target nor its prescribed parameters are amended. S0 retains its separate historical non-existence verdict.

## Argument

### 1. The moment class forced by S1

We first record a consequence of the active target that will be used without adding a moment hypothesis.

**Lemma 1.** Every hypothetical S1 solution has finite third count moments on every bounded Borel set and infinite fourth count moments on every nonempty open ball.

**Proof.** Let $B$ be a ball of diameter less than $M$. The pair kernel is integrable:
\[
 \int_{B^2}D^{-1}\,d\mathbf x
 \leq |B|\int_{|u|<M}|u|^{-1}\,du
 =2\pi M^2|B|.
 \tag{4}
\]
For a pair of length $d>0$, write the third vertex in cylindrical coordinates about the line through the pair. Since $A=d\,r/2$ when $r$ is the transverse distance, nonnegative integration gives
\[
 \int_{\operatorname{diam}(x_1,x_2,z)<M,\ A>0}
       \frac{dz}{A(x_1,x_2,z)}
 \leq \frac{2\pi^2M^2}{d}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, enlarging to $B(x_1,M)$, the integral of $1/r$ is
$4\pi\int_0^M\sqrt{M^2-r^2}\,dr=\pi^2M^2$. Equations (4)–(5) imply a finite integral of $1/A$ over $B^3$. The full identities, together with
$N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N$, prove $\mathbb E X(B)^3<\infty$.

Any bounded set is covered by finitely many such balls. If there are $k$ balls, the deterministic inequality
$(\sum_{i=1}^k n_i)^3\leq k^2\sum_i n_i^3$ proves the asserted finiteness for its count. This also shows that the unprescribed long-distance third density cannot create an infinite third count moment on a bounded set.

For the fourth moment, place a sufficiently small square and neighbourhoods of its first three vertices inside the given ball, with the whole configuration diameter strictly below $M$. For each nearby base triangle, choose the fourth vertex near the remaining square vertex and write it as $p+s e+t f+h n$, where $e,f$ span the base plane and $n$ is its unit normal. All six edges and all four face areas remain bounded away from zero on a suitably small family. Its base area $A$ is positive, the fourth-coordinate Jacobian is one, and
\[
 V=A|h|/3.
\]
The fourth-point integral contains a positive finite factor times
$\int_{0<|h|<\varepsilon}dh/|h|=\infty$. The base family has positive measure. Tonelli and the full fourth identity give $\mathbb E(X(B))_4=\infty$, and hence $\mathbb E X(B)^4=\infty$. Scaling the family into any nonempty ball is legitimate. No finite fourth moment was assumed. $\square$

The last calculation is the supplied reviewed local obstruction, repeated to identify exactly where it is used. Its divergence persists with ordinary separated vertices and nondegenerate triangular faces.

### 2. A precise Poisson-cluster framework

Let $\mathcal C$ be the standard measurable space of finite nonempty simple configurations in $\mathbb R^3$. Write $N(C)=|C|$. Let $\nu$ be a measure on $\mathcal C$ such that
\[
 0<\int N(C)\,\nu(dC)=\lambda<\infty.
 \tag{6}
\]
Since $N\geq1$, $\nu(\mathcal C)\leq\lambda$. Take a Poisson random measure on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathcal C$ with intensity $dz\,\nu(dC)$, and let each atom $(z,C)$ contribute the translated configuration $z+C$. Thus all observed points belong to the specified cluster; the parent itself is observed exactly when $0\in C$.

The resulting superposition is defined on the whole space. For bounded Borel $B$, Tonelli gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}\int_{\mathcal C} C(B-z)\,\nu(dC)\,dz
 =|B|\int N(C)\,\nu(dC)=\lambda|B|.
 \tag{7}
\]
It follows that its count on $B$ is finite almost surely. A countable exhaustion gives local finiteness simultaneously on all bounded sets. In fact, the number of parent clusters meeting $B$ is a Poisson variable of mean at most the integral in (7), because $\mathbf1_{\{C(B-z)>0\}}\leq C(B-z)$.

Within each mark the points are distinct. Coincidences between different parents have probability zero: for each ordered pair of selected mark points, the equality $z+y=z'+y'$ is a Lebesgue-null condition on the independent translation variables. Applying nonnegative integration and a countable exhaustion shows that there are almost surely no such coincidences. The process is simple and, by translation invariance of $dz$, stationary. Its exact intensity is (7).

For $r\geq1$, define the same-parent factorial measure
\[
 K_r(H)=
 \int dz\int\nu(dC)
 \sum_{y_1,\ldots,y_r\in C}^{\ne}
       \mathbf1_H(z+y_1,\ldots,z+y_r).
 \tag{8}
\]
It is a nonnegative measure and $K_1(dx)=\lambda\,dx$. Every observed ordered tuple has one unique partition according to its parents. Distinct parents in a Poisson process have the product factorial measure, so
\[
 \alpha^{(r)}(d\mathbf x)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_r}
       \prod_{I\in\pi}K_{|I|}(d\mathbf x_I).
 \tag{9}
\]
For completeness, the Poisson factorial identity follows first on disjoint finite-measure sets from independent Poisson counts and
$\mathbb E(Z)_j=(\mathbb EZ)^j$, then on nonnegative measurable tests by finite partitions and monotone extension. Order the blocks of a set partition by their least labels. This identifies each parent assignment exactly once and introduces no additional factorial. Independent marks then give (9). Every rearrangement is of nonnegative terms and is justified by Tonelli, even when an expectation is infinite.

Whenever the full measures have S1's finite-almost-everywhere densities, $K_r\leq\alpha^{(r)}$ implies that $K_r$ also has a finite-almost-everywhere density $k_r$. In particular, the full measures and these submeasures are $\sigma$-finite. We then obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \rho^{(2)}={}&\lambda^2+k_2(12),\\
 \rho^{(3)}={}&\lambda^3+\lambda\sum_{i<j}k_2(ij)+k_3(123),\\
 \rho^{(4)}={}&\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}k_2(ij)
       +\sum_{ij|kl}k_2(ij)k_2(kl)
       +\lambda\sum_{|J|=3}k_3(J)+k_4(1234).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The sums at order four contain six edges, three pairings and four faces, respectively. Together with the all-singleton and single-block terms they account for all 15 partitions; orders one, two and three have 1, 2 and 5 partitions.

### 3. Nonnegative residuals exist algebraically at every intensity

For an S1 solution of this form, the same-parent densities on the specified domains must equal the following residuals:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_2(12)={}&a_2/D_{12}-\lambda^2,\\
 q_3(123)={}&a_3/A_{123}
       -\lambda a_2\sum_{i<j}D_{ij}^{-1}+2\lambda^3,\\
 q_4(1234)={}&a_4/V
       -\lambda a_3\sum_{|J|=3}A_J^{-1}
       -a_2^2\sum_{ij|kl}(D_{ij}D_{kl})^{-1}\\
       &\hspace{12mm}
       +2\lambda^2a_2\sum_{i<j}D_{ij}^{-1}
       -6\lambda^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
These expressions follow by successive substitution in (10); they concern the full densities, not a substitution of cumulants for the target. Every face and edge of an $E_4(M)$ configuration belongs to the corresponding lower-order domain.

There is no sign obstruction to (11) for any given $\lambda,M>0$. For example, choose
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_2&=2\lambda^2M,\\
 a_3&=3\lambda a_2M,\\
 a_4&=2\left(\frac43\lambda a_3M
                   +\frac12a_2^2M+\lambda^4M^3\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The elementary estimates
\[
 A/D\leq M/2,\quad V/A_J\leq M/3,\quad V\leq M^3/6,
 \quad V/(D_{ij}D_{kl})\leq M/6
 \tag{13}
\]
hold on the relevant domains. For the final estimate, using the pairing $12|34$,
$6V=|\det(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_1,x_4-x_3)|\leq D_{12}M D_{34}$; the other pairings follow by relabelling. Consequently
\[
 q_2\geq\frac{a_2}{2D},\qquad
 q_3\geq\frac{a_3}{2A},\qquad
 q_4\geq\frac{a_4}{2V}
 \tag{14}
\]
on the full respective domains. In the last inequality we discard the positive edge term in (11) and bound all three negative groups using (13).

Equations (11)–(14) do **not** define a cluster law. The unresolved condition is that all these measures arise from one nonnegative measure $\nu$ on actual finite configurations through (8), together with suitable outside-domain values. That condition couples different orders and contains substantially more information than pointwise nonnegativity.

### 4. Population obstructions

**Proposition 2.** A process of the form (6)–(8) with $N(C)\leq K$ for a fixed finite integer $K$ has finite local exponential moments. It therefore cannot satisfy S1.

**Proof.** For $\theta>0$ and $0\leq u\leq K$, convexity gives
\[
 e^{\theta u}-1\leq \frac{e^{\theta K}-1}{K}\,u.
\]
The exponential formula for a Poisson integral, obtained by finite-measure truncations followed by monotone convergence, and (7) yield
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\theta X(B)}
 =\exp\!\left\{\int dz\int\nu(dC)
                  [e^{\theta C(B-z)}-1]\right\}
 \leq \exp\!\left\{\lambda|B|
                   \frac{e^{\theta K}-1}{K}\right\}<\infty.
 \tag{15}
\]
No bound on the radius or diameter of $C$ is used. Lemma 1 gives the contradiction. $\square$

More generally, $\int N^4\,d\nu<\infty$ also implies finite fourth local count moments. Indeed,
\[
 \int dz\int C(B-z)^4\,\nu(dC)
 \leq |B|\int N(C)^4\,\nu(dC)<\infty,
 \tag{16}
\]
using $C(B-z)^4\leq N(C)^3 C(B-z)$. The number of contributing parents has finite mean by (7), and its compound Poisson sum has finite fourth moment by expansion into its four possible numbers of distinct parent indices. Thus finite fourth population moments are excluded as well.

**Proposition 3.** Consider homogeneous Poisson parents of rate $\kappa>0$, an almost surely finite nonnegative integer population $N$ with $0<\mathbb EN<\infty$, and iid offspring displacements with one probability density $f$, independent of $N$. This model cannot satisfy S1. The conclusion remains valid if $f$ is random, shared within the cluster, and independent of $N$.

**Proof.** If $\mathbb EN^4<\infty$, (16) and Lemma 1 apply. Otherwise $\mathbb E(N)_4=\infty$. Put
\[
 \beta_4(H)=
 \int dz\int \mathbf1_H(z+y_1,\ldots,z+y_4)
          \prod_{i=1}^4 f(y_i)\,d\mathbf y.
 \tag{17}
\]
For random $f$, average the displayed expression over its law. This is a nonzero, locally finite measure: on a set with its first coordinate in a bounded $B$, its mass is at most $|B|$, and
$\beta_4(B\times(\mathbb R^3)^3)=|B|$. Population independence and the ordered selection count give
\[
 K_4(H)=\kappa\,\mathbb E(N)_4\,\beta_4(H).
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus $K_4(H)=\infty$ whenever $\beta_4(H)>0$, and it is zero when $\beta_4(H)=0$. This measure is not $\sigma$-finite: any set of finite $K_4$-mass must have zero $\beta_4$-mass, and a countable cover by such sets would contradict $\beta_4\ne0$.

Under S1, $\alpha^{(4)}$ has a finite-almost-everywhere Lebesgue density and is $\sigma$-finite, as noted after (9). Its submeasure $K_4$ must be $\sigma$-finite as well, a contradiction. This handles the infinite-moment case without an assumption that the convolution in (17) is positive everywhere. $\square$

Population independence is essential to this proof. It gives no contradiction when the displacement law depends on $N$. In particular, it does not misclassify the common reviewed shrinking-cluster counterexample, whose displacement radii explicitly depend on population.

### 5. Large populations cannot be confined to arbitrarily small diameters

**Proposition 4.** Any S1 solution within (6)–(8) satisfies (2).

**Proof.** Fix $0<\delta<M/\sqrt2$ and choose a square side $s$ with
$\delta<s<M/\sqrt2$. Take small neighbourhoods of its first three vertices and the fourth-point coordinates used in Lemma 1. Shrink them so that every configuration in the resulting bounded measurable family $F$ has
\[
 \min_{i<j}D_{ij}>\delta,\qquad
 \min_{|J|=3}A_J>\eta,\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}<M
 \tag{19}
\]
for some $\eta>0$. Allow $0<|h|<\varepsilon$ with no lower height cutoff. The calculation in Lemma 1 gives
$\alpha^{(4)}(F)=a_4\int_F V^{-1}\,d\mathbf x=\infty$.

On $F$, every lower-order factor in (10) is bounded. In fact
$0\leq k_2\leq\rho^{(2)}\leq a_2/\delta$ and
$0\leq k_3\leq\rho^{(3)}\leq a_3/\eta$. Each of the 14 partition terms other than $k_4$ therefore has finite integral over bounded $F$. It follows from the nonnegative sum (10) that
\[
 K_4(F)=\infty.
 \tag{20}
\]
Let $B$ be a bounded set containing all the possible first coordinates in $F$. Any same-parent tuple in $F$ comes from a cluster of diameter at least $\delta$. For each ordered quadruple of that cluster, integrating the condition on its first translated point over $z$ gives at most $|B|$. Equation (8) consequently gives
\[
 K_4(F)
 \leq |B|\int (N(C))_4
           \mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{diam}C\geq\delta\}}\,\nu(dC).
 \tag{21}
\]
Combining (20) and (21) proves (2). All estimates concern nonnegative measures, so no subtraction of infinite quantities occurs. $\square$

In particular, suppose there is a deterministic sequence $r_n\to0$ such that every population-$n$ cluster has diameter at most $r_n$. For each fixed $\delta>0$, clusters with diameter at least $\delta$ then have population in a finite set. The integral in (2) is finite because $\nu(\mathcal C)<\infty$. Such a model cannot satisfy S1. This precisely explains why the supplied shrinking-ball example, despite its legitimate infinite fourth moment and finite-almost-everywhere densities, does not resolve the present problem.

A related local marginal obligation can be stated without a population moment assumption. Fix an open ball $B$ of diameter below $M$. For almost every noncollinear base triple $\mathbf x\in B^3$,
\[
 \int_B k_4(\mathbf x,z)\,dz=\infty.
 \tag{22}
\]
To see this, take a small in-plane patch for $z$ within $B$, away from the three lines joining base vertices, and then a small normal interval. On this patch all edge distances and face areas are bounded below. The lower partition terms in (10) are bounded there, whereas $a_4/V$ has an infinite reciprocal-height integral. Fubini makes this argument valid for almost every base triple.

In terms of actual clusters, the left side of (22) is the third same-parent measure weighted by the number of additional cluster points in $B$. This weight is finite for each configuration but has an infinite mean in the corresponding third-order sampling. More explicitly, the measure obtained from (8) at order three by weighting a selected triple by
\[
 C(B-z)-\sum_{i=1}^3\mathbf1_B(x_i)
\]
is the marginal $K_4(d\mathbf x\times B)$. A $K_3$-null set is null for this weighted measure, because the weighting is finite pointwise. In view of (22), $k_3>0$ almost everywhere on $B^3$ off collinearity. These facts make clear why a finite fourth-order kernel cannot simply be marginalised and subtracted from a finite third-order kernel as in a bounded-population construction.

### 6. An exact pair-and-triangle construction on the full lower-order domains

We now give the positive partial result claimed in (3). It uses at most three offspring per parent and therefore deliberately establishes only the first two identities of (1).

Let
\[
 K_2^0=\frac{\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}}D,\qquad
 K_3^0=\frac{\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}}A,\qquad
 I=2\pi M^2,\quad L=\frac{4\pi M^3}{3},\quad J=2\pi^2M^2.
\]
Assign zero to degenerate configurations. For a translation-invariant kernel $H$ write
$\mathcal MH(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1})=\int H(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1},z)\,dz$.
Equations (4)–(5), including the support condition on the base edge, give
\[
 \int K_2^0(0,y)\,dy=I,\qquad
 \mathcal MK_3^0\leq J K_2^0.
 \tag{23}
\]

Assume (3), and choose
\[
 a_2=\lambda/I,\qquad a_3=a_2/(4J).
\]
Define, on the entire appropriate configuration spaces,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_2&=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}(a_2/D-\lambda^2),\\
 q_3&=\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}
          \left(a_3/A-\lambda^3-\lambda\sum_{i<j}q_2(ij)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
The constants in this construction are independent of the separate algebraic choice (12). For pairs,
$\lambda^2D/a_2\leq2\pi t$. For triples, $A\leq M^2/2$ and $A/D_{ij}\leq M/2$ give
\[
 \frac{\lambda^3 A+\lambda A\sum q_2(ij)}{a_3}
 \leq \frac{\lambda^3M^2}{2a_3}
              +\frac{3\lambda a_2M}{2a_3}
 =8\pi^3t^2+12\pi^2t.
\]
Hence
\[
 \frac{a_2}2K_2^0\leq q_2\leq a_2K_2^0,\qquad
 \frac{a_3}2K_3^0\leq q_3\leq a_3K_3^0.
 \tag{25}
\]

Put
\[
 F_3=q_3,\qquad F_2=q_2-\mathcal Mq_3.
 \tag{26}
\]
Using (23)–(25),
\[
 \frac{a_2}{4}K_2^0
 =\left(\frac{a_2}{2}-a_3J\right)K_2^0
 \leq F_2\leq q_2,\qquad F_3\geq\frac{a_3}{2}K_3^0.
 \tag{27}
\]
Both kernels are nonnegative, symmetric, translation and rotation invariant, measurable, and have positive finite anchored integrals
\[
 Z_j=\int F_j(0,y_2,\ldots,y_j)\,dy_2\cdots dy_j,\quad j=2,3.
\]
Finiteness follows from (23)–(27). Positivity follows from the positive-measure domains of pairs and noncollinear triples.

Use independent Poisson parent streams of rates $Z_2/2!$ and $Z_3/3!$. A parent in stream $j$ at $z$ emits
$z,z+y_2,\ldots,z+y_j$, with offset density
$F_j(0,\mathbf y)/Z_j$. Thus in this explicit construction the parent location is one of the observed points. Add singleton parents of rate
\[
 \beta=\lambda^2L+\frac{Q_3}{2},\qquad
 Q_j=\int q_j(0,\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y.
 \tag{28}
\]
These are genuine normalised probability laws with nonnegative finite rates. All cluster diameters are below $M$.

For clarity, the normalisation at every order can be checked directly. A stream-$j$ cluster has $j!/(j-r)!$ ordered selections of $r$ points. Symmetry and translation invariance of $F_j$, followed by translating the selected first point to the origin, show that each selection has the same integrated joint density. Multiplying by the parent rate gives the same-parent $r$-density
\[
 \frac{\mathcal M^{j-r}F_j}{(j-r)!}.
 \tag{29}
\]
The change of anchor has Jacobian one. This calculation includes selections which do not contain the original parent point.

Since $Z_3=Q_3$, $Z_2=Q_2-Q_3$, and
$Q_2=a_2I-\lambda^2L=\lambda-\lambda^2L$, the non-singleton intensity is
\[
 Z_2+\frac{Z_3}{2}=Q_2-\frac{Q_3}{2}.
 \]
Adding (28) makes the total intensity exactly $\lambda$. Equations (26) and (29) give the same-parent densities
\[
 k_1=\lambda,\qquad k_2=F_2+\mathcal MF_3=q_2,\qquad
 k_3=F_3=q_3,\qquad k_r=0\ (r\geq4).
 \tag{30}
\]
Substitution in the full partition identities (10) proves
\[
 \rho^{(2)}=a_2/D\quad\text{on }E_2(M),\qquad
 \rho^{(3)}=a_3/A\quad\text{on }E_3(M).
 \tag{31}
\]

The process exists on all of $\mathbb R^3$, is stationary and isotropic, and is simple and locally finite by the construction and the arguments following (7). Proposition 2 with $K=3$ gives finite local exponential moments. At every finite order its entire factorial measure is the partition sum with block densities (30), so it has a finite-almost-everywhere density and no singular component. In particular, its actual fourth density is
\[
 \rho^{(4)}
 =\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}q_2(ij)
       +\sum_{ij|kl}q_2(ij)q_2(kl)
       +\lambda\sum_{|J|=3}q_3(J).
 \tag{32}
\]
It is not $a_4/V$ throughout $E_4(M)$ for any positive $a_4$, by Lemma 1 and its finite fourth moments.

Conditions (3) hold on a nonempty interval of positive $t$. For example, the second condition is equivalent to
\[
 0<t\leq
 \frac{\sqrt{9\pi+1}-3\sqrt\pi}{4\pi^{3/2}},
 \tag{33}
\]
and one can impose the first condition alongside it without using any numerical approximation. Spatial dilation leaves $\lambda M^3$ unchanged. Thus this result does not extend to all independently prescribed $(\lambda,M)$ by rescaling.

## Reasoning summary and decision record

The analysis separates an algebraic requirement from a probabilistic one. The partition equations admit nonnegative residuals at every prescribed intensity and diameter, but those residuals need to be factorial measures of one actual cluster distribution. The fourth residual has an infinite omitted-vertex integral near ordinary coplanar shapes, whereas the third measure remains locally finite. This prevents the finite-kernel subtraction used in the seed.

The population tests identify which extensions cannot repair that step. A uniform population bound forces finite exponential moments.
