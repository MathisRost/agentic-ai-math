## Exact claim and scope

S1 remains the question of simultaneous exact full densities $a_2/D,a_3/A,a_4/V$ on **all** nondegenerate configurations of diameter below an independently prescribed $M>0$, at every prescribed $\lambda>0$. Its global absolute-continuity and finite-almost-everywhere requirements, and its allowance for infinite fourth local moments, are unchanged.

This submission does not solve that question. It proves three new results about routes suggested by this track's v02 construction.

1. **Mixing finite radii cannot make the v02 fourth kernel exact.** No nonnegative mixture over finite cylinder radii of its inverse-width fourth kernels, supplemented by clusters of size at most three, can satisfy S1. This obstruction holds at every $\lambda,M>0$, whenever the proposed process and exact lower laws exist. The mixture need not initially have finite total fourth-amplitude weight.
2. **An expanding-radius repair has a different obstruction.** If cylinder radius tends to infinity as thickness tends to zero, and the type weights are adjusted to retain the inverse-width kernel, the resulting fourth same-parent density has one universal small-volume coefficient. Exact S1 requires different coefficients along two explicit degenerations. Hence this repaired family, again with size-at-most-three corrections, cannot solve S1 either. We specify actual finite mark laws in this family; it is not a formal kernel argument alone.
3. **A fixed-coefficient limit loses fourth factorial mass.** At the low intensities covered by v02, we construct processes $X_j$ of the same prescribed intensity and with the same exact pair and triangle laws such that their restrictions to every bounded window converge in total variation to the v01 finite-cluster process $X_0$. Nevertheless, for every nonzero nonnegative continuous test compactly supported inside $E_4(M)$,
   \[
   \lim_j\alpha^{(4)}_{X_j}(f)
   =\alpha^{(4)}_{X_0}(f)+a\int\frac{f}{V}\,d\mathbf x
   >\alpha^{(4)}_{X_0}(f),
   \tag{1}
   \]
   with one fixed finite $a>0$. Thus fourth-measure convergence fails even for these tests away from degeneracy.

The first two results exclude specified representation families. They do not exclude all dependent Poisson clusters or general stationary processes. The third establishes failure of a particular limiting route, not a universal prohibition on limits. No general obstruction justifying a target revision is claimed.

## Argument

### 1. Full partition terms and the two relevant boundary limits

Use the finite-mark Poisson framework and parent-level partition definition from v02, Section 1. All measure products below concern the finite-almost-everywhere block densities guaranteed in an S1 candidate or proved for the displayed constructions.

For a hypothetical Poisson-cluster S1 solution, the exact lower full laws force, on their respective domains,
\[
 q_2(ij)=a_2/D_{ij}-\lambda^2,\qquad
 q_3(ijk)=a_3/A_{ijk}
       -\lambda a_2\sum_{\{u,v\}\subset\{i,j,k\}}D_{uv}^{-1}
       +2\lambda^3.
 \tag{2}
\]
The same-parent fourth density $k_4$ must therefore obey
\[
 k_4+B_4=a_4/V
 \quad\text{a.e. on }E_4(M),
 \tag{3}
\]
where all 14 other parent partitions are
\[
 B_4=\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{i<j}q_2(ij)
       +\sum_{ij|kl}q_2(ij)q_2(kl)
       +\lambda\sum_{|J|=3}q_3(J).
 \tag{4}
\]
There are six edges, three pairings and four faces. Equation (4) is nonnegative for an actual candidate. The canonical functions in (2) and (4) are continuous on the open nondegenerate domains.

Whenever $k_4$ also has a continuous version on $E_4(M)$, (3) holds everywhere on that open set: a continuous difference that is zero almost everywhere must vanish identically. This observation justifies using the explicit paths below; no almost-everywhere identity is evaluated on an exceptional path without this step.

First fix $s>0$ with $\sqrt2s<M$ and consider the lifted square
\[
 x_1=0,\quad x_2=se_1,\quad x_3=se_2,\quad
 x_4=s(e_1+e_2)+he_3,\qquad h>0.
 \tag{5}
\]
For sufficiently small $h$, its diameter is below $M$. All its edges and face areas stay bounded below as $h\downarrow0$, while $V=s^2h/6$. Hence $B_4$ stays bounded and (3) requires
\[
 \lim_{h\downarrow0}Vk_4=a_4.
 \tag{6}
\]

For the second path choose $s,t>0$ with $\sqrt{4s^2+t^2}<M$ and let
\[
 x_1=0,\quad x_2=se_1,\quad
 x_3=2se_1+\delta e_2,\quad x_4=te_3,\qquad \delta>0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Its pair distances stay bounded below, and three of its face areas have positive limits. The face $123$ has
\[
 A_{123}=s\delta/2,\qquad V=s\delta t/6,\qquad V/A_{123}=t/3.
 \]
Using (2) in (4), every term of $VB_4$ tends to zero except the leading $a_3/A_{123}$ term in the face contribution. Thus
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}VB_4=\lambda a_3t/3,
 \qquad
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}Vk_4=a_4-\lambda a_3t/3.
 \tag{8}
\]
The difference between (6) and (8) is strictly positive. It is a consequence of the **full** third-plus-singleton partition, not a change of interpretation to cumulants.

In particular, a continuous fourth same-parent density with one common limit for $Vk_4$ along all bounded small-volume configurations cannot satisfy S1. Only this stated boundary property is excluded; (8) does not apply to an arbitrary proposed fourth kernel without its other hypotheses.

### 2. Normalised cylinder kernels

We recall exactly the part of the v02 calculation needed for the new arguments.

For an affinely independent quadruple $\mathbf x$, write
\[
 w_{\mathbf x}(n)=\max_i n\cdot x_i-\min_i n\cdot x_i,
 \]
and let $A_R(n;\mathbf x)$ be the intersection area of the radius-$R$ discs centred at the projections of its four vertices onto $n^\perp$. With $\sigma$ uniform probability measure on $S^2$, the v02 polar-volume identity is
\[
 \int_{S^2}w_{\mathbf x}(n)^{-3}\,\sigma(dn)=\frac1{2\pi V}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Its proof is the explicit polar-volume calculation in v02, equations (21)–(22): the polar of the difference body of the standard tetrahedron has volume four, affine change of variables contributes $1/(6V)$, and spherical integration contributes the factor three. No new external source is required.

Consequently
\[
 \mu_{\mathbf x}(dn)=2\pi V\,w_{\mathbf x}(n)^{-3}\,\sigma(dn)
 \tag{10}
\]
is a probability measure. Define
\[
 F_R(\mathbf x)=
 \int_{S^2}\frac{A_R(n;\mathbf x)}{\pi R^2}\,\mu_{\mathbf x}(dn).
 \tag{11}
\]
It satisfies $0\leq F_R\leq1$. If $d=\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf x)<R$, the intersection contains a disc of radius $R-d$, and hence
\[
 F_R(\mathbf x)\geq(1-d/R)^2.
 \tag{12}
\]
The exact v02 fourth same-parent kernel with amplitude $a>0$ is
$aF_R(\mathbf x)/V$.

For fixed $R>0$, $F_R$ is continuous on the affinely independent configuration space. Near any such tuple the width has a uniform positive lower bound on the compact sphere. Disc-intersection area is continuous in its centres: its indicator integral converges by dominated convergence because circle boundaries are null, with a common bounded envelope locally. The same holds when the projection plane varies, by a local orthonormal coordinate choice. Dominated convergence in (11) proves the assertion.

### 3. Obstruction for every positive mixture of finite radii

**Theorem 1.** Suppose a Poisson-cluster process has full factorial measures as required by S1 and its fourth same-parent density on $E_4(M)$ has the representation
\[
 k_4(\mathbf x)=\frac1V\int_{(0,\infty)}F_R(\mathbf x)\,\mathcal A(dR),
 \tag{13}
\]
for a nonnegative measure $\mathcal A$. Then it cannot satisfy all three S1 identities at any positive intensity.

This includes arbitrary nonnegative mixtures of the v02 cylinder streams, with any population parameters, supplemented by clusters of size at most three. More generally, it includes any representation whose fourth same-parent density has (13), regardless of how its lower blocks were produced.

**Proof.** First, (3) and nonnegativity imply $Vk_4\leq a_4$ almost everywhere. Fix $u\in(0,1)$ and choose $d_0<M$. On the positive-measure set $E_4(d_0)$, (12) gives
\[
 \int F_R(\mathbf x)\,\mathcal A(dR)
 \geq(1-u)^2\,\mathcal A([d_0/u,\infty)).
 \]
Thus $\mathcal A([d_0/u,\infty))\leq a_4/(1-u)^2$. Letting $d_0\downarrow0$, and then $u\downarrow0$, proves
\[
 \mathcal A((0,\infty))\leq a_4<\infty.
 \tag{14}
\]
This finiteness is derived from the target, not assumed.

The bound $F_R\leq1$ and (14) imply that the integral in (13) is continuous on nondegenerate configurations. Therefore (3) holds pointwise there by Section 1.

Fix the lifted square (5). As $h\downarrow0$, the probability measures (10) concentrate on the two unit normals $\pm e_3$ of the limiting plane. To check this, remove any neighbourhood of those normals. The noncollinear base $0,se_1,se_2$ has a width bounded below by a positive number on the remaining compact set of directions. Its contribution to (10) is then bounded by a constant times $V$, which tends to zero. Symmetry under $n\mapsto-n$ assigns equal masses to the two limiting normals.

Let $J_R(s)$ be the area of the intersection of four radius-$R$ planar discs centred at the corners of a square of side $s$. The continuity of the intersection area, together with the preceding concentration, gives
\[
 \lim_{h\downarrow0}F_R(\mathbf x(s,h))
 =\frac{J_R(s)}{\pi R^2}=:j_R(s).
 \tag{15}
\]
For every finite $R>0$ and $s>0$,
\[
 0\leq j_R(s)<1,\qquad \lim_{s\downarrow0}j_R(s)=1.
 \tag{16}
\]
The strict inequality holds because even the intersection of two equal discs with distinct centres has area strictly smaller than either disc. The limit follows by continuity as all four centres coalesce. These statements also cover radii too small for a nonempty four-disc intersection.

Dominated convergence with (14), (6) and (15) now gives, for every $0<s<M/\sqrt2$,
\[
 a_4=\int j_R(s)\,\mathcal A(dR).
 \tag{17}
\]
Letting $s\downarrow0$ in (17) yields $a_4=\mathcal A((0,\infty))$. Since $a_4>0$, the measure is nonzero. But (16) makes the integral in (17) strictly smaller than its total mass for each positive $s$, a contradiction. $\square$

An atom at an infinite radius is not part of the representation: each radius in (13) is a finite positive number and each underlying mark is finite. The theorem does not licence replacing such an atom by an improper infinite planar cluster. An expanding-radius law in which radius depends on thickness is a different possibility; it is treated next.

### 4. Expanding cylinders and a second exactness obstruction

Let $R:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be measurable, finite at every argument, and satisfy
\[
 R(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow\infty
 \quad\text{as }\varepsilon\downarrow0.
 \tag{18}
\]
Use the uniform cylinder density
\[
 f_{\varepsilon,n}(y)=
 \frac{\mathbf1_{\{|P_ny|<R(\varepsilon),\ |n\cdot y|<\varepsilon\}}}
      {2\varepsilon\pi R(\varepsilon)^2}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Fix $\ell>0$, $T\geq1$, $b>0$, and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_\ell(\varepsilon)&=\varepsilon/\ell+\ell/\varepsilon,\\
 N_T(\varepsilon)&=4+
 \left\lceil T g_\ell(\varepsilon)
          [1+(R(\varepsilon)/\ell)^6]\right\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
A type parent emits this many iid displacements with density (19). Its type-rate measure is
\[
 \frac{b[\pi R(\varepsilon)^2]^3}{(N_T(\varepsilon))_4}
       \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(dn).
 \tag{21}
\]
The factor in (21) compensates the radius change at fourth order.

These formulas define actual finite-cluster laws with finite intensity and finite third population moment rate. To verify this, for $r=0,1,2,3$ set $(N)_0=1$. With $u=(R/\ell)^6$ and $p=4-r$, cancellation of factorials gives
\[
 \frac{b\pi^3 R^6(N_T)_r}{(N_T)_4}
 \leq b\pi^3\ell^6 T^{-p}g_\ell^{-p}
          \frac{u}{(1+u)^p}
 \leq b\pi^3\ell^6 T^{-p}g_\ell^{-p}.
 \tag{22}
\]
The integrals of $g_\ell^{-p}\,d\varepsilon/\varepsilon$ are finite for $p=1,2,3,4$: after $u=\varepsilon/\ell$, their integrands are $u^{p-1}/(1+u^2)^p$, integrable at both endpoints. Thus the parent rate and first three factorial population rates are finite. The raw third moment rate follows from
$N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N$. The parent translation construction proves stationarity and simplicity. Its finite intensity gives almost-sure local finiteness. For bounded $B$, the intensity of parents hitting $B$ is at most $|B|\int N\,d\nu$, and
$\int dz\int C(B-z)^3\,\nu(dC)\leq |B|\int N^3\,d\nu<\infty$.
The compound Poisson sum of their contributions therefore has a finite third moment. These conclusions do not require a bound on the radii.

At order four, (21) cancels the population factor and leaves
\[
 k_4(\mathbf x)=
 b\int_{S^2}\int_0^\infty
 L_\varepsilon(n;\mathbf x)
 \frac{(2\varepsilon-w_{\mathbf x}(n))_+}{(2\varepsilon)^4}
 \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(dn),
 \quad
 L_\varepsilon=\frac{A_{R(\varepsilon)}(n;\mathbf x)}
                    {\pi R(\varepsilon)^2}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Here $0\leq L_\varepsilon\leq1$. The same elementary height integral and (9) show
\[
 0\leq k_4(\mathbf x)\leq\frac{a}{V},
 \qquad a=\frac{b}{24\pi}.
 \tag{24}
\]
This is finite at every nondegenerate quadruple. The measures are mixtures of diffuse densities, so they have no singular component. The first three local moment bounds and (24), followed by the full partition formula, give finite-almost-everywhere full densities through order four. Increasing $T$ makes the intensity arbitrarily small while preserving (23); singletons can supply any missing finite intensity. Thus the specified family is nonempty and has the relevant broad moment and measure regularity.

**Lemma 2.** For (23), whenever $\mathbf x_j$ is a sequence of nondegenerate quadruples with bounded diameters and $V(\mathbf x_j)\to0$,
\[
 V(\mathbf x_j)\,k_4(\mathbf x_j)\longrightarrow a.
 \tag{25}
\]
The density $k_4$ is continuous on nondegenerate configurations.

**Proof.** By the height integral
$\int_0^\infty(2\varepsilon-w)_+(2\varepsilon)^{-4}\,d\varepsilon/\varepsilon=1/(12w^3)$
and (9), the measure
\[
 \mathbb P_{\mathbf x}(d\varepsilon,dn)
 =24\pi V
   \frac{(2\varepsilon-w_{\mathbf x}(n))_+}{(2\varepsilon)^4}
   \frac{d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(dn)
 \tag{26}
\]
is a probability measure. Equation (23) becomes
$Vk_4=a\,\mathbb E_{\mathbf x}L_\varepsilon$.
For every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P_{\mathbf x}\{\varepsilon\geq\delta\}
 \leq24\pi V\int_\delta^\infty\frac{d\varepsilon}{8\varepsilon^4}
 =\frac{\pi V}{\delta^3}.
 \tag{27}
\]
If all diameters are at most $D_0$, then (18) and the disc-intersection bound give, for any $0<\xi<1$, a sufficiently small $\delta>0$ such that
$L_\varepsilon\geq(1-\xi)^2$ whenever $\varepsilon<\delta$, uniformly in all the tuples and all directions. Combining this with (27), then letting $V\to0$ and $\xi\downarrow0$, proves (25).

For continuity, near a fixed affinely independent tuple the width is uniformly bounded below by some $m>0$ over the sphere. The integrand vanishes for $2\varepsilon\leq m$, and after the bound $L_\varepsilon\leq1$ it is dominated by $1/(8\varepsilon^4)$ on the remaining interval. The disc-intersection factor is continuous in the tuple for fixed type. Dominated convergence proves continuity. $\square$

**Theorem 3.** The expanding-cylinder process (19)–(21), supplemented by any independent clusters of size at most three, cannot satisfy S1 for any positive $\lambda,M$.

**Proof.** If the lower laws fail, S1 already fails. If they hold, the total fourth same-parent density is still (23), while the full fourth density must be $k_4+B_4$. Its continuous canonical version permits the limits in Section 1. Along the lifted square, (25) and (6) imply $a_4=a$. Along the collapsing-face path, (25) and (8) imply $a_4=a+\lambda a_3t/3$. These are incompatible because $\lambda,a_3,t>0$. $\square$

The same argument excludes any continuous fourth same-parent kernel with property (25), regardless of its origin. It does not assert that every legitimate cluster kernel has that property. A successful exact kernel must retain the distinction between ordinary planar flattening and collapse of a face with nonzero opposite altitude.

### 5. A fixed-amplitude sequence and its actual process limit

We now address the limit issue with a specific convergence theorem, rather than merely noting missing hypotheses.

Assume the low-intensity conditions from v02:
\[
 2\pi\lambda M^3\leq\frac12,\qquad
 8\pi^3(\lambda M^3)^2+12\pi^2\lambda M^3\leq\frac12.
 \tag{28}
\]
Set
\[
 I=2\pi M^2,\quad J=2\pi^2M^2,\quad W=4\pi M^3/3,
 \quad a_2=\lambda/I,\quad a_3=a_2/(4J).
 \tag{29}
\]
Let $q_2,q_3$ be the restricted kernels from v02, equation (32); on their domains they equal (2), and outside they are zero. Define $K_2^0=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}/D$, $K_3^0=\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}/A$, and write $(\mathcal MF)(x_1,x_2)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}F(x_1,x_2,y)\,dy$ for marginalisation of a triple kernel. The previously proved bounds are
\[
 \tfrac12a_2K_2^0\leq q_2\leq a_2K_2^0,\qquad
 \tfrac12a_3K_3^0\leq q_3\leq a_3K_3^0,\qquad
 \mathcal MK_3^0\leq JK_2^0,\quad
 \int K_2^0(0,y)\,dy=I.
 \tag{30}
\]
These are precisely the inequalities used below; no other historical result is imported.

The finite-cluster process $X_0$ uses correction kernels
\[
 F_3^0=q_3,\qquad F_2^0=q_2-\mathcal Mq_3
 \tag{31}
\]
and singleton rate
$\beta_0=\lambda^2W+Q_3/2$, where $Q_3=\int q_3(0,y,z)\,dy\,dz$.
A size-$r$ stream has anchored mark-rate density $F_r^0/r!$ and emits the anchor and its offsets. This is the v01 construction: (30) proves its nonnegative kernels and finite rates, the intensity is exactly $\lambda$, and it has finite local exponential moments. On $E_4(M)$ its full fourth density is $B_4$ of (4).

Fix any **one finite amplitude $a>0$**. For integers $j\geq2$ choose
\[
 R_j=jM,\qquad c_j=24\pi^4R_j^6a,
 \tag{32}
\]
and use the fixed-radius cylinder law of v02 with reference length $\ell=M$ and
\[
 T_j=j\max\left\{
 1,\
 \left(\frac{2c_j}{\pi R_j^2a_2}\right)^{1/2},\
 \frac{8c_j}{\pi R_j^4a_3},\
 \left(\frac{\pi c_j}{8\lambda^2W}\right)^{1/3}
 \right\}.
 \tag{33}
\]
Let its same-parent densities be $p_{r,j}$ and its intensity be $m_{1,j}$. The bounds in v02, equation (30), now give
\[
 p_{2,j}\leq\frac{a_2}{8j^2D},\qquad
 p_{3,j}\leq\frac{a_3}{4jA},\qquad
 m_{1,j}\leq\frac{\lambda^2W}{2j^3}.
 \tag{34}
\]
Its exact fourth kernel is $aF_{R_j}/V$, independent of $T_j$.

Assign zero values to every density on its null degeneracy set before defining restrictions. As in v02, add finite correction streams with
\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_{2,j}&=q_2-\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}p_{2,j},\\
 r_{3,j}&=q_3-\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}p_{3,j},\\
 F_{3,j}&=r_{3,j},\qquad
 F_{2,j}=r_{2,j}-\mathcal Mr_{3,j},
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
and the singleton rate
\[
 \beta_j=\lambda^2W+\delta_{2,j}
             +(Q_3-\delta_{3,j})/2-m_{1,j},
 \tag{36}
\]
where
\[
 \delta_{2,j}=\int\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}p_{2,j}(0,y)\,dy,\qquad
 \delta_{3,j}=\int\mathbf1_{E_3(M)}p_{3,j}(0,y,z)\,dy\,dz.
 \]
Equations (30) and (34) ensure all the same positivity and intensity requirements as in v02. Therefore the resulting $X_j$ exists on the whole space, has intensity exactly $\lambda$, satisfies both exact lower full laws, has finite third and infinite fourth local moments, and has the required globally diffuse finite-almost-everywhere densities through order four.

The bounds also give
\[
 \delta_{2,j}\leq\frac{a_2I}{8j^2}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \delta_{3,j}\leq\frac{a_3JI}{4j}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{37}
\]
Taking anchored $L^1$ norms and using nonnegative marginalisation in (35),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|F_{3,j}-F_3^0\|_1&=\delta_{3,j},\\
 \|F_{2,j}-F_2^0\|_1&\leq\delta_{2,j}+\delta_{3,j},\\
 |\beta_j-\beta_0|&\leq\delta_{2,j}+\delta_{3,j}/2+m_{1,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]

**Theorem 4.** For every bounded Borel set $B$,
\[
 \mathcal L(X_j|_B)\longrightarrow\mathcal L(X_0|_B)
 \quad\text{in total variation}.
 \tag{39}
\]
Nevertheless (1) holds for every nonzero $f\geq0$ in $C_c(E_4(M))$.

**Proof.** Couple the finite correction parent processes using the common part of their anchored rate densities and independent Poisson processes for the two nonnegative differences. Their mark diameters are below $M$, so only parent anchors in
$B_M=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,B)<M\}$ can affect $B$. This set has finite volume. The probability that any differing finite-cluster parent changes the restriction is at most
\[
 |B_M|\left[
   \frac{\|F_{2,j}-F_2^0\|_1}{2!}
  +\frac{\|F_{3,j}-F_3^0\|_1}{3!}
  +|\beta_j-\beta_0|\right].
 \tag{40}
\]
The cylindrical stream need not have bounded radius. Its probability of contributing any point to $B$ is bounded directly by its expected count, $m_{1,j}|B|$. Combining this with (37)–(40) gives a coupling whose restrictions disagree with probability tending to zero. The coupling inequality proves (39).

On the other hand, the exact full density on $E_4(M)$ is
\[
 \rho^{(4)}_{X_j}=\frac{aF_{R_j}}{V}+B_4.
 \tag{41}
\]
The disc bound gives
$(1-1/j)^2\leq F_{R_j}\leq1$ throughout that domain. Hence (41) converges pointwise there to $a/V+B_4$.

For $f\in C_c(E_4(M))$, its support has a positive volume lower bound and a bounded spatial envelope. All edge distances and face areas are then bounded below as well, by the determinant bounds and the diameter cap. Thus $(a/V+B_4)f$ is integrable and dominates $\rho^{(4)}_{X_j}f$. Dominated convergence yields
\[
 \lim_j\alpha^{(4)}_{X_j}(f)
 =\int(a/V+B_4)f\,d\mathbf x.
 \]
The limiting process $X_0$ has fourth density $B_4$ on the domain. This proves (1), whose strict inequality follows from $a>0$ and nonzero nonnegative continuous $f$. $\square$

The random factorial sums associated with this $f$ are finite almost surely. Their laws converge by (39), and their expectations stay bounded, but they are not uniformly integrable: otherwise those expectations would converge to the expectation for $X_0$, contradicting (1). Thus even local total-variation convergence and a fixed finite fourth coefficient do not rescue this particular limiting route. Its high-population contribution becomes rare in bounded windows while retaining fourth factorial expectation.

## What remains unresolved

The exact all-intensity S1 question is still open in this track. Theorems 1 and 3 exclude two natural extensions of the cylinder construction, not all possible mark laws.

The missing exact same-parent fourth kernel is still
\[
 q_4=a_4/V-B_4
 \quad\text{on }E_4(M),
 \]
jointly realised with (2) by one nonnegative measure on finite configurations and with the required globally diffuse finite-almost-everywhere densities. Its near-degenerate behaviour must respect both (6) and (8). A positive mixture of the fixed-radius kernels does not, and the particular expanding-radius family does not. Dependent within-cluster geometry or other fourth-order kernels are not classified by these arguments.

The all-intensity gap also remains. The sequence in Section 5 still uses (28). The new scoped obstructions apply at all positive intensities, but they do not prove that (28), or any other intensity restriction, is necessary for general S1.
