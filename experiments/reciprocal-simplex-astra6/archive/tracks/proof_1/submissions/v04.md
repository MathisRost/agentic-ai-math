## Proof

### 1. Conventions and full partition formula

Write $E_r(M)$ for the nondegenerate ordered $r$-tuples of diameter below $M$. Here $D$ is pair distance, $A$ triangle area and $V$ tetrahedron volume. A cluster stream is a Poisson process of parents with translation-invariant intensity $dz\,\nu(dC)$, each parent emitting a finite simple mark $C$ translated by $z$.

Use the parent-level nonnegative integrals of v02 before asserting densities or products. Whenever the same-parent densities $k_r$ are finite almost everywhere, the full densities are obtained by partitioning the observed points according to their parents. In particular, for total intensity $\lambda$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho^{(2)}&=\lambda^2+k_2,\\
\rho^{(3)}&=\lambda^3+\lambda\sum_{\text{3 edges}}k_2+k_3,\\
\rho^{(4)}&=\lambda^4+\lambda^2\sum_{\text{6 edges}}k_2
 +\sum_{\text{3 pairings}}k_2(ij)k_2(kl)
 +\lambda\sum_{\text{4 faces}}k_3+k_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
These are all partitions, with their indicated multiplicities. We will prove the needed density regularity for each stream before using (3).

For later finite corrections, a nonnegative symmetric translation-invariant kernel $F_r$, with finite anchored integral
$Z_r=\int F_r(0,y_2,\ldots,y_r)\,d\mathbf y$, defines a size-$r$ parent stream with anchored rate density $F_r(0,\mathbf y)/r!$. It emits the anchor and its $r-1$ offsets. Its same-parent order-$s$ density is
$\mathcal M^{r-s}F_r/(r-s)!$, and its point intensity is $Z_r/(r-1)!$. Indeed there are $r!/(r-s)!$ ordered selections, and symmetry and translation make their integrals equal. This supplies the normalisation explicitly.

### 2. Spherical overlap identities

Let $s_R$ be uniform probability surface measure on the sphere of radius $R>0$ about zero. In coarea notation,
\[
s_R(dy)=\frac{\delta(|y|-R)}{4\pi R^2}\,dy.
\tag{4}
\]
The delta notation below denotes the corresponding coarea integrals; the resulting identities can equivalently be tested against nonnegative functions and obtained by change of variables.

For a pair of distance $D<2R$,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3}s_R(x_1-z)s_R(x_2-z)\,dz
 =\frac{1}{8\pi R^2D}.
\tag{5}
\]
To check the constant, place the two points at distance $D$ on the third axis. In cylindrical coordinates about that axis, the volume element expressed through the two distances $r_1,r_2$ is
$2\pi r_1r_2\,dr_1dr_2/D$. Evaluating at $r_1=r_2=R$ and dividing by $(4\pi R^2)^2$ gives (5). There is no contribution for $D>2R$.

For a noncollinear triangle of area $A$ and circumradius $r$, the centres of radius-$R$ spheres through the triangle, when $R>r$, are
$z=c\pm\sqrt{R^2-r^2}\,n$, where $c$ is its planar circumcentre and $n$ its unit normal. The absolute Jacobian of the three distance constraints with respect to $z$ is
$2A\sqrt{R^2-r^2}/R^3$. Summing the two centres gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3}\prod_{i=1}^3s_R(x_i-z)\,dz
 =\frac{1}{64\pi^3R^3A\sqrt{R^2-r^2}},
 \qquad R>r.
\tag{6}
\]
It vanishes for $R<r$. The tangency value is immaterial when integrating a radius density.

For an affinely independent quadruple let $z_*$ and $R_*>0$ be its unique circumcentre and circumradius. The four equations
$|x_i-z|-R=0$ determine $(z,R)=(z_*,R_*)$. Their Jacobian has rows
$(-(x_i-z_*)/R_*,-1)$; subtracting the first row from the others gives absolute determinant $6V/R_*^3$. Consequently, for any nonnegative measurable weight $m$,
\[
\int_0^\infty m(R)\int_{\mathbb R^3}
       \prod_{i=1}^4s_R(x_i-z)\,dz\,dR
 =\frac{m(R_*)}{1536\pi^4R_*^5V}
 \quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{7}
\]
In fact $1536=6\cdot4^4$, from the determinant and the four surface normalisations.

For completeness, these calculations do not conceal singular masses on the excluded degeneracy sets. Conditional on a sphere, two independent surface points coincide with probability zero. Three distinct points cannot be collinear, since a line meets a sphere in at most two points. Conditional on three distinct points, the plane they determine cuts the sphere in a circle, a surface-null set for the fourth point. Thus coplanar ordered quadruples also have zero conditional measure. Nonnegative mixing preserves these null assertions even when the fourth population moment rate is infinite. Equations (5)–(7), derived on the nondegenerate sets by coarea, therefore give the full measures.

### 3. An actual process with exact fourth same-parent density

Fix $a,\ell>0$ and $T\geq1$. Set
\[
c=1536\pi^4a,\qquad
g(R)=R/\ell+\ell/R,\qquad
N_T(R)=4+\lceil T g(R)^8\rceil.
\tag{8}
\]
At radius $R$ a parent emits $N_T(R)$ iid points with law $s_R$. Its radius-rate measure per unit parent volume is
\[
\nu_T(dR)=\frac{cR^5}{(N_T(R))_4}\,dR.
\tag{9}
\]
Each mark is finite and simple. The type measure is measurable. For $r=0,1,2,3$, with $(N)_0=1$ and $p=4-r$,
\[
\frac{(N_T)_r}{(N_T)_4}
 \leq T^{-p}g(R)^{-8p}.
\tag{10}
\]
All parent and first-three factorial population rates are therefore finite: after $R=\ell u$, the required integrals are
$\ell^6\int_0^\infty u^5(u+u^{-1})^{-8p}\,du$, finite at zero and infinity for $p=1,2,3,4$.

Let $m_1$ denote this stream's intensity and $p_r$ its same-parent densities. Splitting the first-moment bound at $R=\ell$ gives
\[
m_1\leq U_T:=\frac{4c\ell^6}{45T^3}.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed the dimensionless integrals are bounded by $1/30+1/18=4/45$. The raw third population rate is finite by
$N^3=(N)_3+3(N)_2+N$.

These facts give a whole-space stationary, isotropic, simple, locally finite process. For bounded $B$, its expected count is $m_1|B|$; parents hitting $B$ have finite rate at most that expectation. Moreover,
\[
\int dz\int C(B-z)^3\,\nu_T(dC)
\leq |B|\int N(C)^3\,\nu_T(dC)<\infty.
\tag{12}
\]
The compound Poisson sum in $B$ thus has finite third moment. Independent translations prevent coincidences between different parents.

At order four, $(N_T)_4$ cancels from (9), and (7) gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle p_4(\mathbf x)=\frac a{V(\mathbf x)}}
\quad\text{on every nondegenerate quadruple, as a density version.}
\tag{13}
\]
This is a full same-parent measure calculation, with no limiting radius or asymptotic replacement.

The lower contributions admit bounds of the forms needed for finite corrections. From (5), (9) and (10),
\[
p_2\leq \frac{c}{8\pi T^2D}
        \int_0^\infty R^3g(R)^{-16}\,dR
 \leq\frac{S_T}{D},\qquad
S_T:=\frac{c\ell^4}{60\pi T^2}.
\tag{14}
\]
For the last bound, splitting at $\ell$ gives
$\int R^3g^{-16}\,dR\leq\ell^4(1/20+1/12)=2\ell^4/15$.

Likewise (6) gives
\[
p_3\leq\frac{c}{64\pi^3TA}
  \int_r^\infty\frac{R^2g(R)^{-8}}{\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}\,dR
\leq\frac{H_T}{A},\qquad
H_T:=\frac{7c\ell^2}{384\pi^3T}.
\tag{15}
\]
Here put $R=\sqrt{r^2+t^2}$ in the integral. It becomes
$\int_0^\infty Rg(R)^{-8}\,dt$. For $0<t<\ell$ its integrand is at most $\ell$: this follows separately from $g\geq\ell/R$ when $R\leq\ell$, and from $g\geq R/\ell$ when $R\geq\ell$. For $t\geq\ell$ it is at most $\ell^8t^{-7}$. The resulting bound is $\ell^2+\ell^2/6$.

In particular, $S_T,H_T,U_T$ tend to zero as $T\to\infty$, while (13) remains unchanged. The full measures through order four are globally absolutely continuous and finite almost everywhere by (5)–(7), (13)–(15), and then (3). Every nonempty open ball has infinite fourth moment: inside a small separated planar-square neighbourhood, (13) integrates as a positive constant times $\int dh/|h|=\infty$. This does not conflict with the finite third moments or almost-sure local finiteness.

**Theorem 1.** For every $\lambda,a>0$, there is a stationary simple locally finite process of intensity $\lambda$, with the global density regularity and moment class just proved, whose fourth same-parent density is exactly $a/V$ everywhere off degeneracy. Its second and third same-parent contributions can be made arbitrarily small relative to $D^{-1}$ and $A^{-1}$.

**Proof.** Choose $T$ to make $m_1\leq\lambda$, then add independent singletons of intensity $\lambda-m_1$. They change only $k_1$. Further increases of $T$ give the requested lower bounds without changing (13). $\square$

This theorem concerns a same-parent density. Formula (3) still governs the full fourth law.

### 5. Random spherical caps provide the full pair law at every intensity

We next remove the intensity restriction for the pair-law construction. Fix arbitrary $\lambda,M>0$ and an arbitrary desired cap-stream intensity $w>0$. Let $R$ have probability density
$h(R)=M^{-1}\mathbf1_{[M,2M]}(R)$ and write
\[
\mu_1=\mathbb ER=3M/2,\qquad
\mu_2=\mathbb ER^2=7M^2/3.
\tag{24}
\]
For a cap parent of radius $R$, choose a uniform direction $v\in S^2$ and an independent threshold $t$ uniform on $[-R,R]$. Conditional on these variables, emit a Poisson process on the sphere with intensity measure
\[
\kappa(R)\,\mathbf1_{\{v\cdot y\leq t\}}\,s_R(dy),
\qquad \kappa(R)=K R^2.
\tag{25}
\]
This is a finite simple mark. Use parent rate $\gamma$ and the probability type law just specified, with
\[
K=\frac{32\pi\lambda^2\mu_2}{w\mu_1},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{w^2\mu_1}{16\pi\lambda^2\mu_2^2}.
\tag{26}
\]
All population moments are finite: $R$ lies in a compact positive interval, $\kappa$ is bounded, and the conditional count is Poisson with mean at most $\kappa$.

If points $x_i$ lie on the radius-$R$ sphere about a common centre, averaging the cap inclusion probability gives
\[
P_m(\mathbf x;R)=\frac12-\frac{1}{2R}
       \int_{S^2}\max_i(v\cdot x_i)\,\sigma(dv).
\tag{27}
\]
Translation of all $x_i$ does not change the integral, since the mean of $v$ is zero. To obtain (27), condition first on $v$: the threshold must exceed the largest projection, and its admissible interval has length $R-\max_i v\cdot(x_i-z)$. Thus $0\leq P_m\leq1/2$; the upper bound also follows because the mean support function is nonnegative.

For a pair, the integral of its maximum projection is $D/4$: write the maximum as the average of the two projections plus half their absolute difference and use $\mathbb E|v\cdot u|=|u|/2$. Hence
\[
P_2=\frac12-\frac{D}{8R}.
\tag{28}
\]
The cap stream intensity is $\gamma\mathbb E\kappa/2=w$. Applying (5) and (28), for $0<D<M$ all radii exceed $D/2$, and its same-parent pair density is
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_2(D)
 &=\frac{\gamma K^2\mu_2}{16\pi D}
       -\frac{\gamma K^2\mu_1}{64\pi}\\
 &=\frac{A_{\rm cap}}D-\lambda^2,\qquad
A_{\rm cap}=\frac{4\lambda^2\mu_2}{\mu_1}
           =\frac{56}{9}\lambda^2M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Thus the negative constant needed after including *all* parent partitions is built into an actual nonnegative same-parent kernel. Its intensity cost $w$ can be chosen arbitrarily small; larger conditional counts compensate the smaller parent rate.

The radius density is also crucial for global regularity. Applying (6)–(7), with the cap probabilities (27), gives the full same-parent measure densities
\[
u_3(\mathbf x)=\frac{\gamma}{64\pi^3A}
 \int_{\max(M,r)}^{2M}
 \frac{h(R)\kappa(R)^3P_3(\mathbf x;R)}
      {R^3\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}\,dR
\tag{30}
\]
when $r<2M$, and zero otherwise, and
\[
u_4(\mathbf x)=
 \frac{\gamma h(R_*)\kappa(R_*)^4P_4(\mathbf x;R_*)}
      {1536\pi^4R_*^5V}.
\tag{31}
\]
These formulas use (27) independently of the centre of the containing sphere. There is no mass on the degeneracies, by the same conditional null-set argument as in Section 2. They imply globally
\[
u_3\leq\frac{G_3}{A},\qquad u_4\leq\frac{G_4}{V},
\qquad
G_3=\frac{\gamma K^3M^2}{16\pi^3},\quad
G_4=\frac{\gamma K^4M^2}{384\pi^4}.
\tag{32}
\]
For the first bound, use $P_3\leq1/2$ and
$\int_{\max(M,r)}^{2M}R^3\,dR/\sqrt{R^2-r^2}\leq8M^3$.
The latter follows by $t=\sqrt{R^2-r^2}$: the integrand becomes $R^2dt$, with $R^2\leq4M^2$ and interval length at most $2M$. For the second bound, $P_4\leq1/2$ and $R_*\leq2M$ give (32) directly.

The cap process is therefore a whole-space stationary simple process, with globally absolutely continuous finite-almost-everywhere densities through order four and finite local moments of every order. The full pair law of the cap process plus singletons of intensity $\lambda-w$, when $w\leq\lambda$, is exactly $A_{\rm cap}/D$ on $E_2(M)$. The next construction adds the infinite-fourth-moment spherical stream while retaining an exact pair law.

### 6. All intensities: exact pair law, correct moment class and uniform fourth comparison

I=2\pi M^2,\qquad
S_*=\lambda/(8I),\qquad H_*=\lambda/M^4,\qquad
\overline A=A_{\rm cap}+S_*,\quad \overline H=G_3+H_*,
\tag{33}
\]
and define the finite constant
\[
C_0=G_4+\lambda^4M^3/6
       +\lambda^2\overline A M^2
       +\overline A^2M/2
       +4\lambda\overline H M/3.
\tag{34}
\]
Choose $a=C_0/\eta>0$ and use the spherical law of Section 3 with $\ell=M$ and $c=1536\pi^4a$. Choose $T$ large enough that
\[
S_T\leq S_*,\qquad H_T\leq H_*,\qquad U_T\leq\lambda/8.
\tag{35}
\]
For example, one may take the maximum of $1$ and
$[cM^4/(60\pi S_*)]^{1/2}$,
$7cM^2/(384\pi^3H_*)$,
and $[32cM^6/(45\lambda)]^{1/3}$.
All choices are finite for every prescribed pair $(\lambda,M)$.

Add a size-two stream with anchored kernel

F_2=\mathbf1_{E_2(M)}(S_T/D-p_2).
\tag{36}
\]
It is nonnegative by (14), and its intensity is
$Q=\int F_2(0,y)\,dy\leq S_TI\leq\lambda/8$.

**Theorem 3.** The resulting process has intensity exactly $\lambda$, finite third and infinite fourth moments on every nonempty bounded open set, and globally absolutely continuous finite-almost-everywhere factorial densities through order four. On $E_2(M)$ its full pair density is exactly
\[
\rho^{(2)}=\frac{A_{\rm cap}+S_T}{D}.
\tag{37}
\]
On $E_4(M)$ it satisfies (2).

**Proof.** Every stream has been constructed as a nonnegative measure on finite marks. The moment and measure assertions follow from Sections 3 and 5 and the bounded-population correction streams. The infinite fourth moment is already present in the spherical component.

On the pair domain, the same-parent density is exactly
$q_2^*=(A_{\rm cap}+S_T)/D-\lambda^2$, by (29) and (36). The other same-parent densities are
\[
k_3=p_3+u_3,\qquad k_4=a/V+u_4.
\tag{38}
\]
These are actual joint contributions of the one superposed mark measure. Equation (3) proves (37) and gives the exact full fourth measure; no parent partition is omitted.

\[
V\leq M^3/6,\quad V/D_{ij}\leq M^2/6,\quad
V/(D_{ij}D_{kl})\leq M/6,\quad V/A_{ijk}\leq M/3.
\tag{39}
\]
The opposite-edge bound follows by using those edges and a connecting edge in a determinant. Applying (39) to the six, three and four terms in (3) yields

0\leq V\rho^{(4)}-a\leq C_0=\eta a.
\tag{40}

The construction covers every prescribed intensity and diameter without a lower shape cutoff. The remaining failure is not hidden in its fourth-moment class: it has the finite-third/infinite-fourth local behaviour forced by S1.

### 7. Why the all-intensity process does not satisfy the triangle law

Its exact full third density on $E_3(M)$ is
\[
\rho^{(3)}=\lambda^3+\lambda\sum_{\text{3 edges}}q_2^*
              +p_3+u_3.
\tag{41}
\]
It cannot equal $a_3/A$ for any positive constant $a_3$.

To see this, let a noncollinear triangle approach a collinear triple of distinct points whose diameter is strictly below $M$. Its edge distances stay positive and its circumradius $r=abc/(4A)$ tends to infinity. For $r>2M$, $u_3=0$ by (30). Also the bound before (15), now using $g(R)\geq R/M$, gives for $r\geq M$
\[
A p_3\leq
\frac{cM^8}{64\pi^3T}
 \int_r^\infty\frac{R^{-6}}{\sqrt{R^2-r^2}}\,dR
 =\frac{cM^8}{120\pi^3T\,r^6}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{42}
\]
For the equality, put $R=r\cosh u$ and use
$\int_0^\infty\operatorname{sech}^6u\,du=8/15$; the latter follows from $v=\tanh u$ and $\int_0^1(1-v^2)^2\,dv$.
The first four partition terms in (41), multiplied by $A$, tend to zero because their pair distances stay bounded below. Hence
\[
A\rho^{(3)}\longrightarrow0
\tag{43}
\]
